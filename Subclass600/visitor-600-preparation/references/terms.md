# Terms used in preparation

Read before interpreting application roles, family facts or stages. These explanations help preparation; a legal definition must be checked in the applicable legislation before a legal-compliance finding. Policy usage can differ from a defined term. Explain relevant terms in the applicant's language while preserving official names.

| Term | Meaning and execution safeguard |
|---|---|
| Lodgement | Submitting an application through the approved process. Draft creation, payment and submission are different events. An acknowledgement is evidence of recorded submission, not this agent's certification of validity. |
| Visa class / subclass / stream | Different levels of the application and visa framework. Record the exact selected subclass and stream; generic guidance does not establish the applicable approved form or route. |
| Valid application | A legal status determined under applicable application requirements, not a synonym for a complete document folder. This skill flags preparation gaps; it does not certify validity. |
| Applicant | Distinguish a person actually applying from a relative merely disclosed on a form. Departmental policy may use applicant to mean a valid-application applicant; legal usage can be broader. Do not infer validity from the label. |
| Primary applicant / primary criteria | The person named as primary on a form and the person required to satisfy primary criteria are not interchangeable solely because of form ordering. Apply this subclass's actual role rules. |
| Secondary criteria | Where the subclass provides them, criteria for an applicant in the specified family relationship. Not a general permission for dependants to join any visa application. |
| Member of the family unit | A context-dependent policy expression and a legally defined term with visa-specific branches. Keep applicant inclusion, disclosed relatives and relevant non-applicant family separate; ordinary kinship or a cultural title does not establish legal membership. |
| Substantive visa / bridging visa | Different legal visa categories. A bridging visa is not a general extension of the earlier visa. Check actual grant, effect, conditions and applicable definitions; a pending application alone does not establish stay, work or travel rights. |
| Decision maker / delegate | The authorised departmental decision maker. The preparation agent, host, sponsor and other assessment agencies do not decide grant or exercise that person's discretion. |
| Law / policy / evidence preference | Legislation, departmental assessment guidance and requested/preferred evidence have different authority. Missing a policy example is not automatically a statutory failure. Preserve discretion without promising its exercise. |
| Visa grant / refusal | The outcome of a decision. Use these precise terms; do not equate a preparation pass with approval or describe a fresh application as an automatic visa extension. |
| Decision / notification / visa effect | Recording a decision, communicating it and when the visa operates can be different events. No notification is not proof that the application is undecided. |
| Finally determined | A technical legal concept that can depend on review provisions. Do not equate it with notification, finalised portal status or the primary decision; legal review questions are outside this skill. |
| Authorised recipient / migration agent | Receiving documents and providing professional assistance are different roles. Verify the actual appointment and contact details when receipt/deadline questions matter; do not assume one role establishes the other. |

## Subclass application
Visitor applicants ordinarily make separate applications even when travelling or grouping applications together. A host invitation, formal sponsor, disclosed relative and applicant are different roles. Do not import a Student-style secondary dependant route. A newborn during a pending application is a separate verification branch, not ordinary group inclusion.
