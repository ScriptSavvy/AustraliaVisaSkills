# Class BB pathway rules

Use with [source gates](verification.md). These are preparation rules and source targets, not a case decision. “Onshore/offshore” below means physical location at the relevant event, not mailing address. Australian permanent resident for Class BB has the specific regulation 1.03 meaning (non-citizen holding a permanent visa); it is not a general test of usual residence. Do not use overseas time, an expired travel facility, a VEVO lookup failure or a current temporary visa alone to infer past or present status.

## Travel and status first

- Separate permanent permission to remain in Australia from permission to travel and enter as a permanent resident. Check the grant notice and current VEVO (Visa Entitlement Verification Online) result: visa in effect, travel facility or must-not-arrive-after date, and any distinct stay/validity fields. Compare actual intended arrival, not only departure. If still onshore, an expired travel facility does not itself mean a current permanent visa has ceased or that an RRV is necessary for staying. But departure without a usable travel facility, or expiry while offshore, can cause cessation. Check Migration Act s82 and the existing visa terms. If departure or return is imminent and the facility does not cover the plan or cannot be verified, flag immediately: do not rely on the pending RRV; check the grant/VEVO and official urgent-travel channel before committing to travel. Tickets alone do not grant priority or travel permission.
- Do not recommend entering on, or obtaining, a Visitor visa/ETA (Electronic Travel Authority) as a routine workaround. A later substantive visa coming into effect can end the earlier visa subject to statutory exceptions; returning temporarily can affect resident entitlements and future residence counting. Do not infer the outcome from a grant alone. New RRV travel time is not carried over from the old visa; do not promise an extension or deferred grant. Check the actual latest grant. Home Affairs warns that the Tuvalu Treaty stream of the Pacific Engagement visa (192) has unlimited travel while valid and may cease if an RRV is granted; check the exact stream and status instead of recommending an RRV.
- Shared application-time status gateway (155.211 and 157.211): current Australian permanent resident; former Australian citizen who lost or renounced citizenship; or former Australian permanent resident whose most recent permanent visa was **not cancelled**. A current citizen is not this applicant cohort. Natural cessation and cancellation are distinct. An offshore-first permanent grant that ceased before first entry may support former status but does not supply a qualifying prior departure or presence. Continue separate pathway screening instead of a blanket denial. Collect historical grant, cessation and movement records if the label or reason is unclear.

## Separate Class BB applications

Schedule 1 item 1128 governs the application channel, charge, location and specified validity restrictions. Normally one Class BB application per person and both 155 and 157 considered (stored policy permits an explicit written single-subclass request; verify the actual record and scope before using it). A grant under one subclass is not a separate refusal of the other on the same Class BB application. No secondary criteria or combined family application. Family members each apply and meet their own primary gateway; simultaneous separate applications may be linked in ImmiAccount. May apply in or outside Australia, not in immigration clearance. Verify fee and forms; see [procedure](evidence.md#lodgement-and-department-requests).

## Residence evidence ledger — 155.212(2) and 157.212(2)(a)

1. Anchor to actual or clearly labelled intended lodgement date, not decision. Use the five calendar years immediately before application and document the exact boundary and source. For each leg record arrival and departure (or still present), status dates and source at relevant subperiods, with unresolved dates explicitly marked. Ask for the official movement record and grant or citizenship history if the source chronology lacks them. Do not interpret a passport stamp or booking alone as a confirmed stay.
2. Intersect lawful physical presence with periods holding a permanent visa or Australian citizenship. A qualifying period **as a citizen** can count for a former citizen. Do not count temporary-only, bridging-status or unlawful/no-status time. The statute lists exceptions for a concurrently held permanent visa and temporary subclasses 601, 773, 956, 976 and 977; do not assume concurrent holding or infer it from grant dates. Check law for historical entry-permit/transitional records rather than treating them as qualifying automatically. Isolated nonqualifying periods do not create a blanket disqualification.
3. Home Affairs' published method counts eligible arrival and departure part-days, a same-day arrival/departure only once, and no double counting on a same-day visa change. Use a date tool to clip and deduplicate the supported intervals and list assumptions. Do not silently decide a material application-day, leap-date, time-zone, same-day status or missing-leg ambiguity; show alternative counts where supported or leave unresolved. Never reuse the presence counting convention as an absence-counting rule without authority.
4. Compare verified supported days with **at least 730** (155 residence) and **at least one day but fewer than 730** (157 independent residence component). Give an evidence-supported subtotal and only defensible alternatives or bounds; an unresolved part that may cross a threshold blocks that finding. Exactly 730 is the 155 threshold, not the independent 157 band. Do not call presence alone sufficient for grant. Manual assessment is possible even for a residence case; no automatic grant or timing promise.

## Subclass 155 alternatives

| Application-time pathway | Conditions to keep separate |
|---|---|
| 155.212(2), either location | Shared status gateway and qualifying residence evidence above. |
| 155.212(3)(a), outside Australia | Substantial Australian business, cultural, employment or personal ties **of benefit to Australia**; holds permanent visa, **or** last departed as permanent resident, **or** last departed as citizen and later lost/renounced citizenship; immediately preceding **continuous** absence **5 years or more** triggers compelling reasons. |
| 155.212(3)(b), outside Australia | Same ties; was an Australian citizen or permanent resident **less than 10 years** before application; identify last departure **as** citizen/permanent resident, not last temporary trip; absences **totalling more than 5 years** from that departure to lodgement trigger compelling reasons. Map all journeys from that anchor. Exactly five cumulative years is not “more than”. |
| 155.212(3A), in Australia | Same ties; identify most recent permanent visa grant or date citizenship ceased as applicable; a **continuous** absence of **5 years or more** since the relevant date triggers compelling reasons. Do not measure time since last entry alone. |
| 155.212(4), either location | At application a member of the family unit of a person whose 155 is still in effect, **or** who lodged a separate Class BB application and meets 155.212(2), (3) or (3A). A chained family-only basis is not one of those three. An expired travel facility does not establish that the underlying visa ended; verify the family head's current location, status and record. |

Absent a supported reason/criterion, mark only the affected branch unresolved or unsupported; evaluate independent branches. Treat continuous calendar years, cumulative absence and the less-than-ten-year status test as different clocks. Build a dated timeline of reasons and evidence explaining why the trigger-length absence continued. It need not be the same reason every day. Reasons beyond the applicant’s control can carry weight but are not an exclusive test. Examples: caring for ill family, death, work/study, accompanying partner/children, serious treatment, legal proceedings, disaster, travel restrictions, schooling or family reunification. Ask what happened, what made it forceful, when it started/ended and how it affected return. A lawful personal choice can still require individual consideration; no categorical acceptance. Keep substantial ties and absence explanation as separate evidence findings, then show their factual relationship.

## Subclass 157 alternatives

- **Independent (157.212(2)):** residence band above plus **compelling and compassionate** reasons for departing if onshore, or for the **last departure** if offshore. Record and evidence both components (why necessary or forceful, why circumstances call for compassion). Relocation/finalising overseas affairs can be considered in context, not automatically accepted. Last departure on a temporary visa does not itself preclude exploring this branch if the gateway and earlier qualifying days can be supported.
- **Family (157.212(3)):** at application member of the family unit of a person granted a 157 **still in effect**, or a person who lodged a separate Class BB application and meets 157.212(2). Do not require the family applicant to meet the independent residence/departure test as well; the shared status gateway and relevant offshore criterion still apply.
- **Additional offshore criterion (157.213) for either branch:** a continuous absence **more than three months immediately before application** calls for reasons that are both compelling and compassionate for that absence. Distinguish reasons for leaving from reasons for continued absence; the same event may support both only when it actually explains both. Three months is not three years or a fixed ninety days; verify an exact boundary using reliable dates and authority. Do not transfer the 155 five-year tests here.

## Family definition, decision and indicative travel periods

- Member of the family unit is a technical term: check reg 1.12, partner and dependency rules. For ordinary eligibility mapping: spouse/de facto partner; child/stepchild of head or partner (not engaged, married or de facto; under 18, or 18 to under 23 and dependent, or 23+ with qualifying incapacity and dependency); dependent child of a qualifying child. Apply exact law for the case. A parent does not qualify simply as a member of their child's family unit; a close relative may support a **ties** claim without meeting this definition. Check the relationship and dependency at lodgement and the head's status or own application. See [document options](evidence.md#family-records).
- At decision, check passport criterion PIC 4021 (valid passport unless unreasonable to require one; do not presume waiver or equate all alternative travel documents), and SRC 5001 if outside Australia then. Treat deportation/character cancellation history as a high-priority source/legal check. Location at grant can be inside or outside Australia, not immigration clearance. No scheduled visa conditions under 155.6/157.6; this does not remove travel constraints. No general up-front character certificate rule or assumed health-document list; follow any actual request. Section 501 can still apply independently. Do not assess character discretion or clearance.
- Indicative under 155.511/157.511, subject to the governing branch and actual grant: 155 residence, five years; 155 ties/other, one year or shorter if determined; 155 family route uses the family head's **original** or expected qualifying 155 period capped at one year, not time left on the head's facility; 157, three months from grant. Where overlapping 155 pathways would change duration (e.g. own residence and family basis), check the actual statutory sequence, basis applied and grant notice rather than promising the longer period. Repeated RRVs are not barred simply by number but each application stands on its own. Never present a travel period as a processing time.
