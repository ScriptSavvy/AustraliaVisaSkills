## Start with your question

Copy one example, then give only the facts needed. You can use your preferred language.

- **Travel facility:** “Use resident-return-visa-preparation. I’m in Australia and my permanent visa travel facility has expired. I do not plan to travel. Do I need to prepare an RRV application now?”
- **Checklist:** “Use resident-return-visa-preparation. Help me make a checklist for my Class BB application. I’m not sure whether my qualifying days reach 730. Ask one question at a time.”
- **Document review:** “Use resident-return-visa-preparation. Compare my redacted movement and visa records. Tell me which dates you could read and which gaps might change the count.”
- **Department request:** “Use resident-return-visa-preparation. Turn this redacted request into preparation actions. Keep the actual response deadline and timezone visible. Do not submit anything.”
- **Family:** “Use resident-return-visa-preparation. Help me organise separate applications for each person. Keep each person's status, family link and supporting documents separate.”

Tell your agent first about near-term travel, uncertain lawful status, or a live notice deadline. Do not assume a pending application lets you return as a permanent resident. Start with a summary; check the provider's privacy and retention settings before uploading. Avoid passport numbers, logins, or unnecessary personal details. Redact identifiers while retaining the dates and fields needed to compare evidence. A summary or filename is not a document inspection. Additional external processing requires permission.

## What to expect

First-use and standalone outputs begin with disclaimer and licence notices. A focused question gets a focused answer, not a full form. If the agent needs more information it explains adaptive intake once, then shows labels such as **Question 1** and asks one relevant question at a time. You can say “unsure” or skip it; that fact stays unresolved. The number continues through clarifications and replacement-page requests. Ask explicitly if you want grouped questions.

A broad review lists main findings and priorities first. It separates presence, readability, completeness, consistency, currency and compliance with a verified requirement. **Ready for the stated preparation check** describes a named check, not visa readiness. **Missing** means an applicable item is absent. **Needs correction** means a confirmed defect. **Needs verification** means something important is unknown or cannot be read or verified. **Not applicable** needs a supported reason. Ask what evidence or official source would resolve the issue.

Your agent cannot determine lawful status, visa validity, eligibility, authenticity, review rights or permission to travel. It does not submit, pay, withdraw or contact others. Live checks depend on host browsing, and image/OCR checks depend on available tools. Continue supported preparation when those checks fail, but do not turn unavailable information into an answer.
