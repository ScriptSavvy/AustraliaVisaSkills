# Operational preparation knowledge — subclass 590

Read with [verification](verification.md) before any compliance finding. This file preserves stored preparation knowledge, not a certification that every provision remains operative for the applicant's date. Current law and specific request directions control where applicable. Public corroboration and stored knowledge are different: do not invent a public citation for an uncorroborated detail or discard useful preparation solely because corroboration is unavailable.

## 1. Roles, nomination and timing
The guardian provides care/support to a nominating student, not a general family-reunion route. The student nominates on **Form 157N — Student guardianship arrangements**. Inspect the actual form, nominated persons, signatures and relationship evidence. Under-18 nominations need relevant parent/custodian agreement; a nominated relative needs written support. The current public page says a completed 157N must accompany the application. Do not treat an absent form as something guaranteed to be requested later.

Current clause 590.111 requires the nominating student to hold a student visa granted on primary criteria at the guardian decision (S1 in verification.md). Stored main-text guidance generally describes subclass 500. The defined legal term also includes historical student subclasses; do not erase that definition or assume a closed historical route remains available. Concurrent preparation/applications are not simultaneous grant permission. Ask whether the student grant is pending only when that affects the task; a pending grant is a stage dependency, not automatic evidence failure at checklist preparation.

One guardian can have multiple nominating students. Check each nomination, relationship/branch, student grant, accommodation and welfare arrangement separately. Ordinary arrangements generally involve one primary guardian per student; bilateral cases can allow multiple guardians. Do not promise a second-parent solution by splitting sibling nominations.

## 2. Select the guardian branch
| Branch | Trigger and preparation rule | Distinctions to retain |
|---|---|---|
| Student under 18 | Parent/custodian, or relative aged at least 21 with written parent/custodian support; evidence of relationship and ability to personally provide suitable accommodation, support and welfare. | Stored wording distinguishes parent/custodian from the relative age limb. Public overview shorthand is broader. Verify exact age/relationship criteria if material; do not invent an exemption or apply the relative age limb blindly to all branches. |
| Student aged 18 or more | A relative (including parent) aged at least 21, exceptional reasons for residence together, and ability to provide accommodation/support/welfare. | Medical care needs with existing home-country care, or strong established religious/cultural chaperone arrangements, are examples—not exhaustive categories or automatic approval. Ordinary immaturity/parental preference is generally not exceptional under stored guidance. |
| Bilateral benefit | Evidence that grant significantly benefits the relationship between the Australian and another national government; guardian at least 21; written parent/custodian support where student under 18. | Non-relative and multiple-guardian arrangements can arise. National-government support describing the function, or DFAT support for a disabled Foreign Affairs scholarship student, are policy evidence expectations, not a self-certified bilateral status. |

For ordinary branches, declarations can be relevant evidence of accommodation/support/welfare unless contrary information calls them into question. Do not require a lease or medical certificate universally. If contrary evidence indicates physical inability, delegated personal care or suitability concerns, record the actual concern and seek relevant truthful evidence; do not determine character or suitability from speculation.

For a bilateral branch, stored grant-criterion guidance does not require the same accommodation/support demonstration under that particular limb. This does **not** erase residence obligations, other criteria or post-grant condition 8537. If paid care/bodyguard activity is proposed, verify nominated tasks, work condition and relevant State/Territory licensing. A job title or government letter alone does not establish lawful work permission or licensing.

## 3. Intended residence
Check that guardian and student genuinely intend to reside together. Under current 590.212(3), outside the bilateral exception the student must not intend to reside with another **580/590 holder**, or another parent/custodian. The other-holder limb is not expressly limited to primary guardians; do not invent a primary-only exemption. Refer unusual household compositions for applicable interpretation rather than promise eligibility. Compare proposed addresses, actual living arrangements, other parents' plans and multiple sibling nominations. A temporary parental visit is not automatically the same as residence. Same city/State can warrant factual enquiry but is not a conclusive statutory test.

## 4. Family composition and children
**All family-unit members under six matter, even those staying home.** Stored guidance requires **compelling and compassionate** reasons or the bilateral-benefit branch. Do not mark the issue Not applicable just because the child is not applying. Sole-parent responsibilities and a student's medical support needs are policy examples. Evidence may include custody, alternative welfare, medical records and a truthful explanation; none is universally required solely because it appears as an example.

Ordinary secondary grant preparation is limited to a family-unit child **under six at decision**, in the limited circumstances above. A spouse/de facto partner is not a secondary 590 applicant under stored guidance. Do not recommend a Student visa for an older child as an assured route: separate subclass 500 requirements and current onshore/application rules apply.

Record all family members required on the form, not only people applying. Combined applications and newborn exceptions require the specific checks in application-procedures.md. Stored guidance provides no ordinary subsequent-entrant route: a current guardian wishing to include an existing family member may need a fresh combined application, but do not assume current permission to lodge or use that summary for a child born during a pending application.

For each family-unit member under 18 who does not hold a Student visa, check appropriate accommodation, support and welfare for the guardian's proposed absence. Remaining parent/custodian or a relative aged at least 21 are stored policy examples; an under-21 sibling is generally not considered appropriate. The public page requests a carer's acceptance letter describing support/household arrangements and identity/signature evidence for relevant non-travelling children. Inspect the actual arrangement rather than demand a universal bundle for every child.

For an accompanying minor, inspect birth/parentage/adoption records, parental responsibility, consent of relevant non-travelling decision-makers or evidence of sole authority. Form 1229 or a statutory declaration can be relevant public-guidance alternatives; do not demand both automatically. Disputed custody, foreign-law authority and best-interests interpretation require qualified review, not a document-count result.

## 5. Genuine temporary entrant (GTE), not subclass 500 GS
Prepare a truthful account of temporary stay, home-country circumstances, circumstances in Australia, immigration history, compliance and other relevant matters. Route the applicable ministerial direction by application date: Direction 106 covers applications on/after 23 March 2024; Direction 108 covers the qualifying earlier applications not finally determined on that date, including their specified remittals. Direction 108 revoked 69. Refresh any later changes; do not use 69 or apply 108 to all new cases. Do not rewrite this as a Student Genuine Student (GS) test. Under Direction 106 Division 2, review relevant home ties, potential Australian circumstances, time in Australia/ongoing-residence purpose, Australian and other-country visa/travel history, refusals/cancellations, compliance and circumstances beyond control, and both favourable and unfavourable relevant information. These are assessment factors, not a requirement to own property or a numerical test.

Useful evidence may include family ties, right to return, actual employment leave, assets and prior travel/compliance records. These are possible supports, not mandatory proof of property ownership or a fabricated incentive to return. The overall facts matter. Owning property can support ties but is not itself the specified financial-capacity evidence.

Consider how support remains viable when the guardian cannot ordinarily work. Prior non-compliance calls for factual chronology, seriousness, recency and circumstances outside control, not an automatic refusal conclusion. A wish to work or relatives in Australia is not proof of an improper purpose. Help reconcile real contradictions honestly; do not write invented GTE facts.

## 6. Funds and insurance
Use [financial-evidence.md](financial-evidence.md) for alternatives, genuine access, multiple students and no double allocation. Evidence is not waived by a student's streamlined checklist/risk setting. Keep sufficiency for the intended stay distinct from the prescribed initial evidence period. Whole-stay coverage includes each family-unit member who will be in Australia, not only combined applicants. In bilateral cases do not assume the student's whole-stay exception automatically removes the financial instrument's prescribed student evidence; apply the financial reference's verification gate.

Primary and secondary applicants need adequate health-insurance arrangements. Stored/public guidance recognises evidence of OVHC paid for the first 12 months, with other adequate products potentially acceptable. Do not say only a product named OVHC can qualify, or confuse the student's OSHC with guardian cover. Compare insured persons, period, premiums, exclusions and actual intended stay. Coverage must be maintained throughout the stay; a first-year evidence practice is not permission to remain uninsured later. For a shorter stay, confirm the applicable instructions rather than demand irrelevant extra cover by assumption.

## 7. Public-interest and return issues
Stored primary criteria include character/security/foreign-policy checks, Commonwealth debt, health, settlement, exclusion-history, values, integrity/passport and special return matters. Stored secondary criteria include health/insurance/identity/character and relevant custody/best-interests requirements, with different values/special-return coverage. Current Part 590 primary PIC references are 4001, 4002, 4003, 4004, 4005, 4010, 4013, 4014, 4019, 4020 and 4021; primary SRC references are 5001, 5002 and 5010. Secondary PIC references are 4001, 4002, 4003, 4004, 4005, 4010, 4013, 4014, 4017, 4018, 4020 and 4021; secondary SRC references are 5001 and 5002 (S1). Detailed applicability/waivers still need checking. Do not demand police certificates or adult values signatures from infants because the framework mentions character/values generally. Stored settlement guidance concerns a proposed stay longer than 12 months and is rarely a sole refusal basis; it is not a universal extra form. Never compute an exclusion clock using an internally inconsistent summary of cancellation versus departure, or assume a passport exception from authorisation to lodge with an expired passport.

The public page gives conditional police evidence directions based on relevant country residence history (including the Australian Federal Police complete-disclosure certificate, not a State certificate), conditional polio evidence, and Department-requested health/biometrics. Refresh actual age/residence/country/time triggers and individual directions; do not order every applicant every test. Character, health, sanctions, exclusion periods, passport exceptions and integrity consequences require professional judgment where material. Do not infer fraud or an exclusion period from a typo, or promise a waiver. Debt repayment arrangements can matter; it is not invariably a full-payment-only rule.

## 8. Intended duration: preparation, never promised grant
Actual grant/VEVO records determine stay. Stored policy generally aligns the guardian with the student's visa, with age/end-of-school-year and child-sixth-birthday considerations. It describes end-of-year coverage for some students turning 18, a three-month approach for October–December birthdays, a generally six-year maximum with limited exceptions, primary-school student limitations and welfare continuity across multiple students. These are **policy planning considerations, not entitlements or guaranteed expiry dates**.

For a secondary child nearing six, stored policy describes a two-month-after-birthday period. Its historical exception says that, if the child's own 500 period between turning six and the primary guardian's expiry would be less than a full school term, the child's 590 period may align with the primary guardian's expiry. This preserves historical policy knowledge, not a current entitlement or permission to obtain a 500. The current public page instead says until the sixth birthday. Keep that conflict visible; do not choose a predicted expiry or assure a subclass 500 transition. Plan insurance/welfare against actual records and verified instructions. Read verification.md before using any stored duration example.

## 9. Conditions and travel preparation
Verify the actual grant and applicable Schedule 8; a summary does not authorise activity. Current 590.611/.612 distinguish ordinary primary conditions (8101, 8201, 8501, 8516, 8534, 8537, 8538), bilateral primary conditions (8106 replacing 8101, with the same remaining primary set), and secondary conditions (8101, 8501, 8502, 8516). Do not impose the primary set on children (S1).
- **8101:** ordinary no work. Work includes activities normally paid, not just employment for a salary. A genuinely normally-unpaid school helper differs from paid-type homestay work. Calling paid-type activity volunteering does not resolve it.
- **8106:** bilateral cases may have task-specific work rather than 8101. Only relevant nominated tasks; not unrestricted employment. Verify the branch, condition and licensing.
- **8201:** primary-holder study limitation; stored guidance distinguishes ELICOS under 20 hours weekly from ELICOS at least 20 hours/other training beyond three months. It is not an English-test requirement. Do not apply it to secondary children automatically.
- **8501:** maintain adequate insurance throughout stay.
- **8502:** secondary entry not before the specified person; inspect who is named.
- **8516:** continuing criteria/circumstances matter; refer material changed arrangements rather than promise safety from initial grant evidence.
- **8534:** No Further Stay restrictions. Check exact applicable law and actual condition/waiver status; do not turn the historical 'protection or another 590' shorthand into an exhaustive current permission or certify another application valid.
- **8537/8538:** residence/care obligations and departure without student. Check each student, proposed absence, compelling or compassionate reason, suitable alternative welfare and **prior evidence to the Minister**, plus provider approval if under 18. Stored procedure also calls for Department approval of welfare changes; current operational permission/notification instructions need verification. Do not misquote that approval practice as the literal condition or treat notification alone as permission. An intended plan, notification or pending approval is not permission. The stored material has internally problematic unconditional-breach wording; do not declare every approved absence a breach or imply alternative care alone suffices.

For travel without a minor student, evidence can include the reason, dates, temporary welfare arrangements and the education provider's temporary CAAW approval. Nomination of another relative can be relevant but is not automatic approval. Holiday travel is not the stored example of compassionate/compelling reasons. Serious illness/death or other exceptional events depend on actual circumstances. Stored policy treats very long absences (six months or more) as a serious residence/compliance concern, not an automatic cancellation conclusion by the preparer. Cancellation/travel-rights advice remains outside scope.
