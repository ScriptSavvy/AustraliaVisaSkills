# SID 482 nomination: operational preparation reference

Use this reference to prepare and reconcile the **employer's nomination** with the nominee's separate visa application. It covers Specialist Skills, Core Skills and Labour Agreement branches. It does not determine eligibility or authorise lodgement, payment, withdrawal, notifications, third-party contact or access to departmental systems. Use placeholders in working examples; do not invent evidence or expose another worker's personal records.

**Authority and currency:** This reference retains nomination rules and policy distinctions for preparation; it is **not a current-law verification**. A **rule account** describes a legal requirement to check against applicable law; **policy** describes an assessment approach or discretion, not binding law; **examples** illustrate a branch, not an exhaustive evidence list or automatic entitlement; **preparation actions** are practical suggestions. Legislation and applicable instruments prevail. A policy discretion belongs to the decision maker, cannot override legislation and must not be treated as guaranteed. Verify the branch that matters rather than deleting it because public confirmation has not yet been obtained.

**Applicable-date gate:** Before relying on a requirement, retrieve the governing Migration Act/Regulations, applicable instrument or executed agreement and relevant Home Affairs guidance. Record the provision, version/commencement and transitional rules, relevant application/event date and the result. For indexed amounts, retrieve the value effective for the relevant nomination date, not simply today's published value. No copied dollar thresholds or levy rates below are a current value. Keep Core and Specialist income thresholds, the salary high-earner carve-out, the LMT high-earner alternative and the advertising salary-disclosure boundary as **separate retrieval fields**. Also retrieve applicable periods, permitted channels, forms and notification/review requirements before calculating a deadline or choosing an operational route.

## Stage and intake controls

<a id="n01"></a>
### N01 — Apply law, policy and discretion at the right level

**All stages; decision maker assesses, preparer organises.** Use policy to identify relevant facts and explain an unusual case, not as an inflexible extra legal test. Where a case does not fit a policy example, record the actual circumstances and the legal question rather than claiming an exemption or an inevitable refusal. Departmental internal escalation and managerial approval are not applicant contact routes or powers available to a preparer. Sponsor approval, nomination approval and visa grant require separate checks.

<a id="n02"></a>
### N02 — Keep three stages and their actors separate

**Sponsor:** sponsorship approval or an executed Labour Agreement; nomination form, certifications, role, pay, recruitment and levy evidence. **Nominee:** identity, qualifications/experience, family facts, contract acceptance and separate visa evidence. **Payer:** payment identity and any refund entitlement. **Decision maker:** official assessment, discretion, record matching, procedural fairness and decision. An agreement replaces the ordinary standard business sponsorship stage, not nomination or visa. Retrieve the available nomination/visa period for the route; do not assume the proposed period establishes visa duration.

**Preparation action:** Maintain separate sponsorship/agreement, nomination and visa status fields, each with identifiers and decision/event dates. Carry nomination facts into visa preparation, but do not convert sponsor certifications into proof of visa skills, English, health, character or family criteria.

<a id="n03"></a>
### N03 — Distinguish permission to nominate from ability to obtain approval

**Sponsor; lodgement versus assessment.** A prospective sponsor applying for work-sponsor approval, or a party negotiating a work agreement, may have a nomination-lodgement route. Approval still depends on approved standard business sponsor (SBS) status or an executed agreement authorising the recruitment/employment/services. Obtain the sponsor application and status evidence; a pending request is not an approved sponsorship or nomination. Check visa-stage lodgement and grant dependencies independently.

<a id="n04"></a>
### N04 — Check the authorised form and channel

**Sponsor; preparation before lodgement.** Verify the approved online nomination form, including whether 482N (Internet) remains the applicable form, and the applicable version of LIN 19/215 or its replacement. Alternative lodgement requires the instrument's circumstances and application-specific departmental authorisation; a technical outage, even with imminent visa cessation, is not a general permission to lodge offline. Preserve outage and expiry facts for authorised human review. This reference does not seek authorisation or submit anything.

<a id="n05"></a>
### N05 — Prepare fee and SAF inputs before finalising the nomination

**Sponsor/payer; lodgement.** The prescribed nomination fee and applicable Skilling Australians Fund (SAF) levy are payable in full at lodgement, subject to a verified exemption. Retrieve the nomination fee from the applicable form/fee instrument and SAF rules from the Migration (Skilling Australians Fund) Charges Regulations. Record date basis, payer, turnover, proposed stay and calculation separately. Do not infer a fee or levy schedule from refund illustrations. See N09, N19 and N66 for inputs, debt and exemptions.

## Stream, identity, form fields and certifications

<a id="n06"></a>
### N06 — Select the stream before compiling occupation and salary evidence

**Sponsor; ordinary SBS route.** The Specialist Skills branch combines eligible ANZSCO Major Groups **1, 2, 4, 5 and 6** with annual earnings **excluding non-monetary benefits** meeting the applicable Specialist Skills Income Threshold (SSIT). The Core Skills branch requires an occupation on the Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL) in force at nomination lodgement and the applicable rule about ineligibility for Specialist Skills. The Labour Agreement branch follows the executed agreement instead.

**Gate:** Retrieve the applicable occupation instrument, including LIN 24/089 or its replacement, classification version, temporal provisions and indexed income threshold. Test the Specialist branch rather than assuming a high salary always permits it. Record exact stream, occupation name/code and threshold basis for visa matching. Apply the Specialist caveat check at N22; Specialist does not require CSOL membership.

<a id="n07"></a>
### N07 — Reconcile nominee identity and previous application links

**Nominee supplies; sponsor enters; decision maker links official records.** Prepare full travel-document name, date of birth, gender as required by the form, and relevant existing/previous Australian visa TRN or Application ID. Explain name changes and inconsistent records. Compare the nomination and visa identity fields; do not assume matching names prove correct client linkage. The preparer must not attempt departmental client merging or duplicate-record correction.

<a id="n08"></a>
### N08 — Record the occupation and actual workplace

**Sponsor.** Prepare the occupation name and six-digit code, the Specialist major group or agreement occupation where relevant, and actual workplace postcode. For multiple workplaces use the location where most working time will be spent; for unpredictable sites use the work base. A head-office address is not a substitute for the real workplace. **Example:** a mobile tradesperson with undetermined future worksites may use the base postcode. Reconcile the contract, duty statement, work locations and visa-side occupation/stream.

<a id="n09"></a>
### N09 — Substantiate stay and turnover, not just form totals

**Sponsor; levy preparation.** Record the proposed stay and annual turnover using the applicable SAF definition. The turnover method uses ordinary income from the latest income year ending before lodgement: for a lodged Australian return, the company's Total Income or an individual's Total business income; where not lodged, prepare the permitted estimate and its basis. For an overseas business, retrieve the relevant ATO estimation guidance. Keep financial records supporting the figure; it may be checked with the ATO. Confirm the permitted period before calculating levy.

<a id="n10"></a>
### N10 — Treat mandatory lodgement fields as locked

**Sponsor; pending nomination.** Check nominee, occupation, turnover, proposed stay and postcode before finalisation: the rule account treats changes to these required fields as needing a new nomination, not a casual correction. For non-mandatory incorrect information, check whether Form 1023 is the permitted pre-decision/pre-withdrawal correction route. Retrieve any additional-information instrument under regulation 2.73(9)(e); do not assume none exists.

**Unresolved effect:** Limited SAF top-up language does not explain when it can coexist with locked turnover/stay inputs. Do not choose a correction or withdrawal route until the exact error, legal mechanism and linked visa consequences are checked (N19, N83). Replacement nomination is not presumed to relink an existing visa application.

<a id="n11"></a>
### N11 — Prepare truthful sponsorship-benefit certification

**All sponsors.** Check whether any benefit was asked for, received, offered or provided in connection with a sponsorship-related event contrary to section 245AR(1), using the applicable provision rather than a narrow payment-only question. The contemplated event need not actually happen. A negative certification may be accepted under policy, but a positive answer, contrary evidence or allegations require integrity/adverse-information assessment. **Example:** seeking a benefit in exchange for keeping a nomination alive can engage the issue. Do not conceal conduct or manufacture a negative declaration.

<a id="n12"></a>
### N12 — Reconcile ordinary sponsor certifications with the evidence

**Sponsor.** Check employment-law and National Employment Standards (NES) compliance of the contract, subject to verified occupation exemptions under LIN 19/212 or its replacement. Specialist/Core certifications also address a significant majority of the occupation's tasks, qualifications/experience commensurate with the role, and placement within the sponsor or a permitted associated entity. An overseas sponsor cannot use the Australian-sponsor associated-entity placement rule. Obtain duty, qualification and contract evidence supporting each statement; the certification does not itself satisfy separate visa criteria.

<a id="n13"></a>
### N13 — Use agreement-specific task and qualification certifications

**Labour Agreement sponsor.** Prepare the significant-majority task certification against ANZSCO where applicable, or the occupation in the agreement/proposed agreement where there is no ANZSCO code. Reconcile the nominee's qualifications and experience with the agreement occupation. A proposed agreement can inform preparation, but the executed agreement controls approval. Record which evidence supports the sponsor's statements and which separate visa-level requirements remain to be met.

## Approval framework, sponsor status and existing families

<a id="n14"></a>
### N14 — Do not mistake streamlined assessment for exemption

**Decision maker assesses.** Build the checklist from common requirements plus the relevant Specialist/Core or Labour Agreement requirements. Policy permits streamlined low-risk assessment for accredited sponsors using certifications and accreditation material; it does not exempt them from substantive criteria.

**Coverage conflict:** A summary approval checklist can omit the separate capacity-to-employ requirement discussed in detail. Keep capacity evidence under N46 even if the summary table, high salary or accreditation seems to make it unnecessary. Resolve any claimed exemption against the full applicable regulation.

<a id="n15"></a>
### N15 — Check the prescribed nomination process separately

**Sponsor prepares; decision maker assesses.** Run a process-validity check against regulation 2.73 and the applicable instruments: authorised nominator and form/channel, required fields, fee/levy and required accompanying evidence. A persuasive job or qualified nominee cannot cure every process failure. Keep validity/process findings separate from substantive nomination and visa findings.

<a id="n16"></a>
### N16 — Identify adverse information and the actual disregard question

**Sponsor and decision maker.** Approval requires no relevant adverse information known about the sponsor or an associated person, or a case-specific basis to disregard it reasonably. Gather the facts, dates, outcomes and response evidence for any relevant issue. Retrieve the full definitions/tests in regulations 1.13A and 1.13B and applicable guidance; they are not reproduced here in full. Do not treat an associated person as synonymous with a corporate associated entity or claim that corrective action automatically removes adverse information.

<a id="n17"></a>
### N17 — Verify sponsor authority at the approval stage

**Specialist/Core:** confirm approved SBS status at decision. **Labour Agreement:** confirm the nominator is the non-Minister party to an executed work agreement authorising the nominee's recruitment, employment or services. An undecided sponsor/agreement application blocks nomination approval even where nomination lodgement was permitted. Preserve approval, validity and authorisation evidence rather than relying on a receipt or negotiations.

<a id="n18"></a>
### N18 — Prepare the sponsorship-failure response without assuming withdrawal

**Pending nomination; sponsor decides, decision maker handles process.** If sponsorship/agreement approval is refused or the request withdrawn, identify any other undecided sponsorship/agreement application and any pre-existing consent to nomination withdrawal in the form. Policy distinguishes acting on that consent from offering procedural fairness/an opportunity to withdraw where consent is absent, then deciding if no withdrawal occurs. Preserve the actual notice and response deadline for human review. Do not withdraw by default; check the linked visa and refund branch first.

<a id="n19"></a>
### N19 — Resolve SAF debt and underpayment before approval

**Sponsor.** Outstanding SAF debt and penalty must be dealt with in full; unexplained underpayment blocks approval. Policy describes limited top-ups, or withdrawal/re-lodgement with a possible refund, and refusal if an underpaid nomination is neither withdrawn nor lawfully topped up.

**Unresolved correction conflict:** The permissible top-up circumstances are unspecified, while the locked-input refund branch prohibits changing turnover/stay and adding levy. Obtain the original entries, calculation, receipt, debt/penalty notice and proposed correction; establish the legal/operational route. **Cross-stage danger:** withdrawal of the linked nomination may make the already lodged visa ungrantable, and a replacement nomination is not assumed capable of linking to it. Resolve that exact visa's matching position before any authorised action.

<a id="n20"></a>
### N20 — Reconcile secondary holders when transferring sponsorship obligations

**Current 457/482 holders; nominee supplies, sponsor lists.** Prepare secondary holders' names and dates of birth and reconcile family composition privately. The decision maker checks official records; clarification must not disclose an omitted individual's private details to the sponsor improperly. A reasonable-disregard discretion may cover an omission in particular circumstances, such as a dependant studying overseas and not residing with the nominee. Do not assume every omission qualifies or use this as a substitute for visa family evidence.

<a id="n21"></a>
### N21 — Do not treat unsupported secondary holders as a harmless mismatch

**After nomination approval; decision maker's consequence assessment.** An omitted secondary holder who lacks sponsorship support may be referred for cancellation consideration even if omission was disregarded for nomination approval. This is not automatic cancellation and does not authorise a preparer to make a referral. Identify support, actual visa/family status and privacy constraints; obtain a focused assessment of the affected person's position before treating the transfer as resolved.

## Occupation, genuine position and hours

<a id="n22"></a>
### N22 — Separate occupation eligibility from genuine duties

**Sponsor.** Check eligible occupation name/code by stream, the applicable classification version, the CSOL in force at nomination lodgement for Core, the Specialist major-group rule, agreement authorisation and applicable caveats. A listed code alone does not prove actual task alignment; N23–N26 address that separately.

**Specialist caveat rule:** Section 8 of LIN 24/089 (compilation 3, 7 November 2025) applies every relevant item of the Applicable Circumstances List to the Specialist position, not only caveats attached to a matching CSOL occupation row. Specialist Skills does **not** require CSOL membership. Screen the position against the whole circumstances list, document each triggered item and supporting business/role facts, and check temporal application before reliance. The earlier uncertainty about Specialist caveat scope is resolved at the instrument-text level; it is not a reason to omit the caveat check.

<a id="n23"></a>
### N23 — Demonstrate a real position accurately represented

**All nominations; sponsor evidence, decision maker assessment.** The position must exist, match its description and nominated occupation, and not be invented solely for migration or relabelled to appear eligible. Genuineness is not an invitation to judge whether hiring at that price is commercially wise. Policy may accept certification in low-risk cases.

**Preparation action:** Where concerns arise, assemble organisation chart, actual outputs, quantified expansion, vacancy history, customer contracts/demand, Australian workforce context and overtime evidence. **Example:** promotional street work labelled as professional marketing requires examination of the actual role, not just its title.

<a id="n24"></a>
### N24 — Explain relationships, ownership and migration-outcome indicators

**Policy indicators, not automatic refusal rules:** family/personal connections, nominee ownership/directorship, immigration history, a newly established business, very few Australian workers or payment connected to nomination. An individual cannot nominate themselves as the same legal person, but a genuinely distinct corporation can potentially nominate an owner/director. Provide entity and role evidence, truthful relationship disclosures and a genuine-position explanation. A recently created business is a reason to examine evidence, not a fixed minimum-trading-period prohibition.

<a id="n25"></a>
### N25 — Compare likely actual duties, not a copied occupation list

**Sponsor.** Explain how the employee will spend their time within the workplace, staffing and organisation structure. Most time should concern the nominated occupation; limited incidental lower-skilled work can occur. Policy recognises reasonable specialisation, technological change and roles that do not perform every listed ANZSCO task. **Example:** executive-level management in a very small single-site operation may need a different explanation from management of a national multi-outlet business. Use concrete projects, delegated responsibilities and time allocations to address concerns; do not dress up routine work.

<a id="n26"></a>
### N26 — Explain business-role mismatch and repeated positions

**Sponsor.** Where the occupation does not obviously fit the business, document the relevant activity, contracts or genuine diversification. Repeated use of the same position across nominations may warrant additional scrutiny. **Example:** a clinical practice proposing a gardening or digital-production role needs evidence of what that employee will actually do, not an automatic rejection based on the business label. Keep business context/genuineness separate from financial ability to employ and pay (N46); internal escalation remains the decision maker's task.

<a id="n27"></a>
### N27 — Verify full-time hours and lawful work patterns

**Sponsor.** Obtain ordinary weekly hours, roster, overtime expectations and the applicable award/agreement and NES provisions. The policy benchmarks describe a 38-hour week, with industry-instrument patterns of 32–45 hours where consistent with the workplace framework; these are not universal current-law entitlements. Verify the actual applicable standard before classifying the role. Hours beyond the policy upper benchmark or regular excessive overtime warrant a specific explanation and review. Alternative work patterns do not automatically make a part-time position full-time.

<a id="n28"></a>
### N28 — Prepare a specific part-time discretion case

**Decision maker discretion under regulation 2.72(10A), not general permission.** Establish workplace-law consistency and why disregarding the full-time requirement is reasonable. Policy examples include:

- Senior overseas academics retaining primary home-university employment while undertaking a defined fractional project in Australia.
- Highly specialised locum/independent medical practitioners, especially rural/regional, with irregular or multi-site hours. Check the occupation carefully; if Resident Medical Officer **253112** better describes the work, obtain a focused occupation assessment rather than assuming the specialist example fits.
- Highly paid non-executive board members or senior specialised managers attending Australian-listed company meetings during short intermittent visits rather than a weekly full-time roster.

Prepare appointment terms, timetable, other employment, actual duties and remuneration. **Separate gate:** acceptance of part-time hours does not settle the earnings/AMSR or monetary-threshold tests; see N45.

## Contract, employing entity and English dependency

<a id="n29"></a>
### N29 — Identify the lawful employer and signed employment relationship

**Specialist/Core SBS nominations.** Unless a verified occupation exemption applies under LIN 19/212/regulation 2.72(13), the ordinary requirement is employment under a written contract signed by employer and nominee. An Australian sponsor may use a qualifying associated-entity employer; an overseas sponsor must employ directly.

**Evidence gap to resolve:** common ownership, shared branding or an assignment letter does not by itself establish the full legal associated-entity definition. Obtain entity records, relationship evidence and the actual contract, then check the applicable definition. Keep sponsor, employer and workplace identities distinct. Agreement cases require their own terms, not automatic importation of this branch.

<a id="n30"></a>
### N30 — Analyse sole-trader and partnership cases by legal person

**Sponsor.** A sole trader cannot employ/nominate themselves under the ordinary employment-contract rule. Partnerships generally cannot employ their own partners, but particular statutory partnership types may differ; the legal position is not safely resolved by the label “partnership”. Obtain jurisdiction, legal structure, partnership legislation/deed and proposed employment terms for focused legal review. Check both employment capacity and migration-outcome/genuine-position issues. A partnership cross-reference does not establish an exception, and a corporate owner's case must not be rejected as though it were sole-trader self-employment.

<a id="n31"></a>
### N31 — Check contract execution and translations

**Sponsor and nominee.** Prepare the relevant signed employment contract; where not in English, provide a certified English translation. The translation itself need not repeat both signatures if the signed original is supplied. An ABN or contract for services does not meet the ordinary employee-contract requirement. Check legal employer, nominee acceptance, role, ordinary hours, location, period, salary and conditions against the nomination. Apply a verified occupation exemption or permitted alternative only to its actual scope.

<a id="n32"></a>
### N32 — Use a complete intra-corporate assignment letter where policy permits

**Policy evidentiary alternative for a qualifying transfer.** On company letterhead, prepare confirmation of the underlying employment contract, employer company, Australian sponsor where relevant, Australian base salary and allowances, ANZSCO occupation and assignment duration, with company/nominee signatures and dates. This may satisfy specified contract-evidence requirements; it does not create permission for an overseas sponsor to place an employee with an associated entity. The same transfer documents may support select-position LMT, but that branch still needs its submission (N63).

<a id="n33"></a>
### N33 — Document award/agreement engagement and acceptance

**Policy alternative where employment is governed by an Enterprise Agreement or Industrial Award.** Prepare a letterhead engagement/offer letter confirming employee status, instrument name/code and applicable terms, ordinary hours, classification/level, and salary if the instrument does not supply it. Include dated employer and nominee acceptance. A suitable Fair Work Ombudsman engagement template may assist, not guarantee compliance.

**Electronic evidence:** a verifiable company email may establish the employer's signature; a reply accepting the offer should retain the original offer. A website acceptance capture may evidence acceptance. Preserve the complete terms and acceptance trail, not a disconnected screenshot or unsigned summary.

<a id="n34"></a>
### N34 — Handle changed-role English evidence without a universal retest

**Existing 457/482 holder changing employer or occupation.** English is ordinarily a visa-stage assessment. A nomination-stage discretionary request can arise where a former English exemption no longer applies to the new position/current SID requirements. Prepare prior grant/exemption evidence and changed-role facts, and respond to an actual lawful request. Do not demand a new test for every employer transfer or infer test scores/exemptions from sponsor certifications. Retrieve the applicable visa English rules separately.

## Salary, AMSR, benefits and ability to employ

<a id="n35"></a>
### N35 — Keep each salary test independent

**Specialist/Core salary framework; sponsor prepares, decision maker assesses.** Unless a verified high-earner carve-out applies to the relevant checks, assess:

1. The annual market salary rate (AMSR) was determined by the prescribed method.
2. The AMSR **excluding non-monetary benefits** meets the applicable income threshold.
3. The nominee's annual earnings are at least the AMSR.
4. The nominee's earnings **excluding non-monetary benefits** meet the applicable threshold.
5. There is no relevant inconsistent Australian labour-market information, unless reasonably disregarded.

Retrieve the applicable high-earner amount, earnings definition and precise carve-out scope under regulation 2.72 and the AMSR instrument, including LIN 24/096 or its replacement. Do not convert that carve-out into exemption from capacity, genuine position, LMT or all nomination requirements. The LMT high-earner alternative is a separate legal branch.

<a id="n36"></a>
### N36 — Break earnings into countable monetary, non-monetary and excluded items

**Sponsor.** Retrieve the applicable Core Skills Income Threshold (CSIT), SSIT, indexation mechanism/year and earnings definitions, including regulations 1.03, 2.57A and 5.42A where applicable. Do not reuse a prior-year threshold. Build a component schedule: wages; amounts applied/directed on the employee's behalf where countable; agreed value of non-monetary benefits; and excluded amounts. Indeterminate payments, reimbursements and specified superannuation contributions are excluded under the relevant definition. Do not count unguaranteed bonuses/overtime or package totals as cash without checking the legal treatment. Apply cash-only and total-earnings tests separately.

<a id="n37"></a>
### N37 — Choose the AMSR branch before collecting surveys

**Sponsor.** AMSR concerns the full-time annual earnings of an equivalent Australian citizen/permanent resident at the same workplace and location. State the figure, monetary/non-monetary split and prescribed determination method. First establish whether an actual equivalent worker exists and whether an award/agreement governs them. Explain equivalence, classification and location; if evidence gives a range, justify the selected point. Do not choose the lowest advertised figure merely because it meets a threshold.

<a id="n38"></a>
### N38 — Use actual equivalent-worker evidence when there is a comparator

**Equivalent Australian worker in the same workplace/location:**

- **Award/agreement applies:** identify and supply the applicable instrument and relevant classification, level or group.
- **No award/agreement:** explain the comparator's salary arrangements and provide relevant employment contracts/payslips.

Generic survey data alone does not replace the actual-comparator branch. Handle comparator records privately, minimising personal information while preserving the facts needed to establish equivalent duties, pay and conditions. Explain material differences rather than selecting a convenient non-equivalent employee.

<a id="n39"></a>
### N39 — Use the prescribed no-comparator method

**No equivalent Australian worker:** identify any applicable award/agreement and classification. Policy generally does not require additional supporting material for an unproblematic instrument-based rate, but departures, regular shift penalties or higher equivalent local advertisements require explanation.

**No equivalent worker and no award/agreement:** explain the method and use at least two prescribed source types, subject to applicable-instrument verification: Jobs and Skills information, equivalent local job advertisements, reputable remuneration surveys, or registered union/employer-association advice. The policy advertisement-recency benchmark is the preceding six months; retrieve the applicable recency requirement before selecting evidence. Explain location, duties, data date and range selection rather than attaching unexplained screenshots.

<a id="n40"></a>
### N40 — Reconcile salary evidence that could undermine credibility

**Sponsor.** Resolve vague/unidentified surveys, mixed job levels/locations, incompatible datasets and unexplained selection from broad ranges. Threshold-level earnings, large non-monetary components and substantial differences above or below AMSR may attract scrutiny, not automatic refusal. Paying above market is not inherently prohibited. Explain genuine pay-setting reasons and evidence that the business will actually pay the promised amount; do not artificially inflate figures to fit a stream.

<a id="n41"></a>
### N41 — Test comparator monetary pay before relying on benefits

**AMSR cash-threshold rule account.** Non-monetary benefits ordinarily sit above, not inside, the monetary threshold. Regulation 2.72(16)(a) describes a discretion involving total AMSR meeting the threshold and reasonable circumstances. Policy points to genuine salary arrangements, no artificial inflation for a lower-skilled role, and either actual equivalent-worker pay arrangements or guaranteed award/agreement arrangements where there is no comparator.

**Bounded statutory resolution:** [verified updates, U07](verified-updates.md#salary-part-time-roles-and-agreement-ceilings) identifies **2.72(16)(a)** as the comparator-threshold discretion linked to **(15)(d)**, with cumulative statutory conditions. Prepare evidence for each condition in the applicable body, including comparator arrangements or instrument-guaranteed entitlement as relevant. Policy conjunctions may still require a focused interpretation in an unusual case; do not weaken safeguards or count accommodation, meals, clothing or travel as an automatic cash-threshold waiver. The decision maker must actually exercise any discretion.

<a id="n42"></a>
### N42 — Compare the nominee's annual earnings with AMSR

**Sponsor.** Prepare a like-for-like comparison using countable earnings components. Below-AMSR nominee earnings ordinarily require refusal; the limited accepted-part-time discretion is separate (N45), and any high-earner carve-out needs its own verified scope. Higher pay is allowed, but a major discrepancy can raise artificial-inflation/genuineness concerns. Record the explanation and contract/payment evidence rather than simply stating “above threshold”.

<a id="n43"></a>
### N43 — Test the nominee's monetary threshold separately

**Rule account:** the nominee's earnings excluding non-monetary benefits must meet the applicable income threshold. Policy discusses offering the same reasonable benefits arrangement as the comparator and evidence of adequate self-support.

**Cross-reference resolved at the inspected-text level:** [verified updates, U07](verified-updates.md#salary-part-time-roles-and-agreement-ceilings) identifies the nominee monetary test as **2.72(15)(f)** and its distinct discretion as **(16)(b)**. Do not use the comparator's **(15)(d)/(16)(a)** as the nominee's provision merely because earlier shorthand repeated it. Retrieve the applicable version and prepare evidence for each actual nominee-discretion condition; quantify the monetary shortfall separately and retain comparator-arrangement and means-of-support evidence where relevant. This correction does not establish that discretion will be exercised, that benefits count as cash, or that every current case is verified. The bounded regulation check is of the compilation as at **2 October 2026**.

<a id="n44"></a>
### N44 — Test market consistency even if the method looks compliant

**Decision maker assesses.** The prescribed method does not immunise a doubtful AMSR from information inconsistent with Australian labour-market conditions, unless reasonably disregarded. Suspect fabricated advertisements, different-job comparisons, worse conditions or an implausibly low rate require explanation. Policy may accept a properly evidenced method absent contrary information. Near-threshold pay is not itself a refusal ground. Retain the real advertisement/data context and explain the apparent inconsistency; never fabricate market evidence.

<a id="n45"></a>
### N45 — Keep part-time remuneration discretion separate from part-time acceptance

**Only where the limited part-time position branch is accepted.** Regulation 2.72(16)(aa) allows a reasonable-disregard question about the nominee's earnings versus full-time AMSR. Prepare actual hours/pay, equivalent Australian part-time remuneration and means of support. **Example:** continuing home-university pay may substantiate a fractional academic's support. Acceptance of reduced hours does not automatically waive the comparison or permit pro-rating every cash/income threshold. Record each monetary and total-earnings test separately and identify any additional discretion required.

<a id="n46"></a>
### N46 — Demonstrate capacity for the whole nomination period

**Sponsor; separate capacity criterion.** Prepare evidence the business can employ the nominee throughout the proposed period and pay at least AMSR in each year. Use evidence for this nomination, not only material held on an earlier sponsorship/nomination file. Historical facts should underpin projections: balance sheet, profit/loss with prior-year comparisons, latest lodged tax returns and complete intervening-quarter BAS. If latest-year accounts are unavailable, explain and provide previous years plus intervening BAS. Address heavy losses, external administration, funding and supported revenue assumptions.

**Coverage warning:** the detailed capacity criterion may be absent from an overview checklist. Do not infer that accreditation or a high-earner salary carve-out removes it; verify any asserted exclusion against the full regulation, including 2.72(17A).

<a id="n47"></a>
### N47 — Compare non-earnings conditions as well as pay

**Sponsor.** Check conditions against an equivalent Australian worker in the same location: leave, hours, deductions and other relevant benefits/obligations. Less favourable conditions can raise a separate concern even where salary passes. The reasonable-disregard test is case-specific and does not authorise unlawful workplace terms. Preserve contract/NES/award compliance certification and explain discrepancies; missing paid leave or concerning deductions need substantive review, not a salary-only answer.

<a id="n48"></a>
### N48 — Address recruitment discrimination without treating workforce composition as proof

**Australian-operating sponsors.** The nomination framework includes non-discriminatory recruitment. Policy generally accepts this absent contrary information; allegations, workforce patterns or preference for temporary visa holders to avoid LMT can require closer assessment. Workforce composition alone does not prove a breach. Provide actual selection practices and recruitment records where an issue arises. An ITO case in which ordinary LMT is not required must not automatically be described as discriminatory recruitment.

## Labour market testing: select the correct route

<a id="n49"></a>
### N49 — Distinguish four routes before preparing recruitment evidence

**Specialist/Core SBS:** ordinary LMT unless an applicable international trade obligation (ITO) makes the requirement inconsistent or a valid statutory exemption applies. A **select occupation/position** uses alternative evidence, not no-evidence exemption. **Labour Agreement:** apply the executed agreement's LMT/recent genuine Australian recruitment requirements; do not automatically import the standard ITO concession.

Prepare a route note identifying applicable law/instrument/agreement, trigger, evidence and date. For ordinary LMT retain both procedural compliance and the substantive no-suitable-readily-available-worker test. Relevant provisions to retrieve include section 140GBA, regulations 2.72AA/2.76A and the applicable LMT instruments.

<a id="n50"></a>
### N50 — Prove an exemption actually exists

**Statutory exemption route.** A disaster exemption requires the Minister's personal written exercise of the relevant power for relief/recovery needs and the specified sponsor, nomination or class. A skill/occupation exemption needs an applicable instrument. The existence of a legislative power is not evidence of its exercise. Retrieve the actual determination/instrument and its dates/scope; this reference makes no claim that no such exemption exists today.

## ITO branch: cumulative facts and documentary proof

The following are **candidate coverage patterns requiring applicable-date verification**, not a currently certified country or occupation list. Retrieve LIN 21/075 or its replacement and the relevant agreement; check nationality versus permanent residence, employer/entity location, employment/service facts and role scope separately. These ordinary-program concessions do not automatically apply to Labour Agreement nominations.

<a id="n51"></a>
### N51 — Nationality/permanent-residence coverage

**Nominee supplies status evidence; sponsor claims the route.** Candidate citizenship/nationality coverage includes Brunei, China, Japan, Malaysia, Mexico, Peru, Thailand and Vietnam. A distinct candidate group includes citizenship/nationality **or qualifying permanent residence** of Canada, Chile, South Korea, New Zealand, Singapore and the United Kingdom. Check the precise applicable agreement/instrument categories before relying on any country or residence status. Prepare passport/nationality or qualifying permanent-residence evidence; ordinary residence alone is not necessarily the qualifying status.

<a id="n52"></a>
### N52 — Current employee of a qualifying overseas associated entity

**Cumulative branch:** current employment, qualifying association with the sponsor, and the entity's qualifying location. Candidate locations include Canada, Chile, China, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, United Kingdom, New Zealand, Peru, Samoa, Tuvalu, Kiribati, Tonga, Solomon Islands, Niue, Cook Islands, Vanuatu or a qualifying ASEAN country. The policy candidate ASEAN set comprises Brunei, Myanmar, Cambodia, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam; do not substitute a changing regional-membership list for the instrument's definition.

Prepare current employment records, entity-location evidence and corporate association documents. Retrieve the applicable country list and legal relationship test. A transfer label or nationality alone does not satisfy this branch.

<a id="n53"></a>
### N53 — Distinguish substantial-operations and new-operation executive branches

**Branch A:** current employee of an associated entity operating in a WTO member country; nominated as an Executive/Senior Manager responsible for all or a substantial part of Australian operations. **Branch B:** overseas sponsor operating in a qualifying WTO country or the separately identified Tuvalu/Kiribati/Niue/Cook Islands group; Executive/Senior Manager establishing a new Australian operation. Retrieve applicable geography, WTO status and instrument coverage for each branch; do not combine convenient elements of the two.

Candidate code-to-occupation checks: **111111** — Chief Executive or Managing Director; **111211** — Corporate General Manager; **131112** — Sales & Marketing Manager; **131113** — Advertising Manager; **132111** — Corporate Services Manager; **132211** — Finance Manager; **132311** — Human Resource Manager; **133611** — Supply and Distribution Manager; **135111** — Chief Information Officer. Verify classification and scope before use. Prepare duties, reporting authority, entity/overseas employment and Australian operation plans; a management title is insufficient.

<a id="n54"></a>
### N54 — Prove qualifying continuous Australian service

**Candidate ITO branch:** a citizen of a qualifying WTO country/territory, or a specifically eligible permanent resident, with **two continuous full-time years immediately before nomination lodgement** in the nominated Australian position with the sponsor. Permanent residence is not broadly interchangeable with WTO citizenship: the candidate eligible-PR group is Armenia, Canada, New Zealand and Switzerland. Retrieve the applicable service period, status and territorial coverage before calculating it. Prepare payroll, contracts, role/location and continuity evidence plus nationality/eligible-PR proof. Do not extend this ordinary-program concession to an agreement nomination by assumption.

<a id="n55"></a>
### N55 — Establish the facts even if the form omitted an ITO claim

**Sponsor claims; decision maker assesses.** Identify the applicable ITO in the form and evidence. Policy allows consideration where the form omitted a claim but an ITO actually applies, and calls for ITO checking before an LMT refusal. This is not permission to omit evidence deliberately. Labels such as intra-corporate transferee, independent executive or contractual service supplier do not establish coverage on their own; policy treatment of otherwise uncovered applicants under contractual service suppliers still depends on the relevant agreement applying.

## Ordinary LMT: timing, advertising and genuine recruitment

<a id="n56"></a>
### N56 — Build the advertising and redundancy chronology before lodgement

**Sponsor.** Ordinary LMT requires prescribed timing/manner, evidence accompanying the nomination at lodgement, and no suitable Australian citizen/PR or eligible temporary visa holder readily available. The policy timing benchmark is the **four months ending at lodgement**, with the required advertising duration falling inside that window even if publication began earlier. Retrieve the applicable period before calculating dates.

Identify recent redundancies/retrenchments in the same or similar occupation and required nominated-occupation disclosures. Recruitment may need to occur **after** those events; verify the exact statutory scope and period rather than assuming earlier advertising survives. Prepare ad/publication/application dates, receipts/authorisations and redundancy chronology. Do not assume missing at-lodgement evidence can be cured later.

<a id="n57"></a>
### N57 — Check every advertisement's authorised channel

**Ordinary route:** sponsor-authorised English advertising in at least two advertisements through permitted national-reach recruitment websites, national print/radio, or an accredited sponsor's website. Retrieve the applicable count/channel rules before relying on them. Industry-specific recruitment sites must have appropriate reach/use. Policy examples distinguish LinkedIn's recruitment platform from a profile-only vacancy, and a national classifieds site's jobs portal from its general classifieds pages or social-media-only posts.

Third-party placement can be acceptable when sponsor-authorised: prepare recruitment invoices, search summaries or executive-search contracts showing authority. A non-accredited sponsor's own website is not automatically a permitted substitute.

<a id="n58"></a>
### N58 — Check each advertisement's content and continuous application period

**Sponsor.** Prepare title/position description, required skills/experience, sponsor or recruitment-agency name, and salary where intended earnings fall below the **applicable advertising disclosure boundary**. Retrieve that boundary separately from CSIT/SSIT and high-earner amounts. **Instrument duration rule:** LIN 18/036 section 8(5)–(7) (compilation 5, 7 December 2024) permits a continuous application/expressions-of-interest period of at least **four weeks across two or more qualifying overlapping advertisements**; count each overlapping day only once. Verify the applicable compilation and retain publication, overlap and application-acceptance evidence.

**Policy-versus-instrument discrepancy:** the retained policy expectation says each advertisement accepts applications for four continuous weeks. Do not elevate that stricter expectation above the instrument's overlapping-advertisement alternative or reject a package solely because an individual ad ran less than four weeks. Check that at least two ads qualify, each is sponsor-commissioned/authorised under section 8(3A), all required content/channels comply, and their permitted overlaps establish the continuous period without double-counting. Back-to-back non-overlapping runs are not that alternative. If the particular configuration remains disputed operationally, preserve the full timeline and request a focused interpretation distinguishing policy expectation from the statutory route; do not invent a failure.

<a id="n59"></a>
### N59 — Assemble the right at-lodgement evidence package

**Ordinary route:** section 9 of LIN 18/036 requires advertising material to accompany the nomination; prepare copies of all advertising material with publication/application dates and authorisation evidence. **Select-position route:** sponsor's written explanation why no suitably qualified/experienced Australian citizen/PR or eligible temporary visa holder is readily available, supported by proof of the select branch and any recruitment undertaken. A select position changes the evidence route; it does not remove the need for a reasoned submission. Identify the route clearly rather than attaching isolated talent awards or transfer letters with no local-availability explanation.

<a id="n60"></a>
### N60 — Explain recruitment outcomes, not only advertising mechanics

**Sponsor.** Keep response counts, selection records and objective reasons candidates were unsuitable; distinguish suitability from a preference for this nominee. Policy can accept compliant timing/manner and sufficient exposure absent contrary information, but different-position ads, nominee-placed ads, poorer local terms or non-genuine selection undermine the substantive test. An employment contract predating advertising is not alone decisive if other evidence establishes reliable recruitment. Where an existing eligible temporary worker's contract raises the availability question, obtain a focused assessment instead of assuming all temporary holders are excluded.

## Select-position alternative evidence

<a id="n61"></a>
### N61 — Establish genuinely exceptional achievement where that branch applies

**Sponsor and nominee.** Candidate select branches include internationally recognised exceptional/outstanding achievement in a profession or sport, academia/research, or top-level chef work. Policy generally looks for sustained multiple achievements, while a single highly innovative cutting-edge achievement may support the claim. High domestic standing requires a field recognised internationally and a defensible international/Australian comparison; ordinary competence is insufficient.

**Definition control:** the instrument's select-field numbering is irregular; do not invent additional fields or infer a missing substantive condition from numbering alone. Use the complete applicable definition in LIN 18/036 or its replacement, and do not treat illustrative evidence as a visa criterion. Explain the claimed field, recognition and position requirement, then provide the no-suitable-worker submission.

<a id="n62"></a>
### N62 — Choose achievement evidence that explains recognition

**Examples, not a mandatory checklist:**

- **Sport:** international rankings/results, national representation, sporting-body statements, scholarships and reliable media/web coverage.
- **Academia/research:** commissioned reports, books, peer-reviewed publications and journal-impact context, citations, awards, government/peer statements, grants, professional designations/memberships, fellowships, qualifications, research reach, a supervisor's substantiated publication prospects and commercially applicable patents.
- **Chef:** awards, peer assessments, recognised designations, senior international appointments and reliable press/web coverage.

Identify independent authority, date, field relevance and international significance. The sponsor must still explain local/eligible temporary-worker unavailability; nominee achievement documents do not replace that submission or separate visa evidence.

<a id="n63"></a>
### N63 — Explain the need for the qualifying intra-corporate transfer

**Select-position candidate:** employee of an established overseas business, nominated by an associated SBS operating in Australia. Prepare proof of overseas business/employment, corporate association, the need for transfer, Australian duties and assignment terms (N32). Include the written select-position availability submission. This is distinct from an ITO concession: a transfer that fails one ITO test may still require assessment under this alternative, not a claim that no LMT evidence is needed.

<a id="n64"></a>
### N64 — Prove the existing position and sole reason for re-nomination

**Select-position candidate for an existing 457, TSS/SID 482 or 494 holder.** The position remains unchanged and the new nomination is solely because earnings changed, or a sponsor/associated-entity restructure means employment is no longer with the former sponsoring/employing entity. Prepare old/new contracts, role/duty continuity, pay changes and restructure/legal-employer documents. A general employer change, promotion or new position does not automatically fit. Retain the written availability submission and verify the applicable instrument's exact conditions.

<a id="n65"></a>
### N65 — Check high-earnings and health-position alternatives precisely

**Select-position candidates:** earnings at/above the independently retrieved LMT high-earner boundary; ANZSCO Minor Group **253 Medical Practitioners**, excluding **253111 General Medical Practitioner** and **253999 Medical Practitioners nec**; and Unit Group **4111 Ambulance Officers and Paramedics**. Retrieve the applicable classification and definition before relying on a code. These are written-submission alternatives, not ITO/no-evidence exemptions. Do not confuse the medical LMT branch with the part-time medical discretion or a contract/working-condition occupation exemption.

## SAF calculation and Labour Agreement controls

<a id="n66"></a>
### N66 — Calculate SAF from verified inputs and a verified rate schedule

**Sponsor/payer.** Use annual turnover and proposed stay under the applicable Charges Regulations; retrieve turnover tiers, levy rates, period treatment and payment rules. Prepare full-at-lodgement calculation and retain the receipt. Do not extrapolate rates from examples or assume a partial refund permits later input amendment.

**Narrow candidate exemption:** Ministers of Religion, including bishops, and Religious Assistants nominated under the relevant Labour Agreement route. Verify the exact occupation/stream exemption; religious character of a business is not enough. This reference does not assert an exhaustive current exemption list. Resolve debt and locked-input errors under N19/N83 before finalising.

<a id="n67"></a>
### N67 — Turn the executed agreement into a nomination requirements matrix

**Labour Agreement sponsor.** Obtain the signed, effective agreement and applicable variations. Check authorised occupation/code, evidence obligations, LMT/recent genuine recruitment, pay and any concessions against the actual terms, alongside common nomination requirements. Record each agreement clause, fact and evidence item. Do not assume ordinary Specialist/Core salary, LMT or English settings apply unchanged, or infer a concession from another employer's agreement. Track nomination-level and visa-level agreement conditions separately, including nominee qualifications/experience and separate visa concessions.

<a id="n68"></a>
### N68 — Check annual and occupation-specific ceilings, not just system acceptance

**Sponsor; decision maker verifies.** Obtain the applicable agreement year, permitted total, occupation sub-ceilings and approved nomination counts. A total allocation can be divided into distinct trade/occupation limits; unused places in one occupation do not necessarily authorise another. A system may block an expired agreement or exhausted total but does not replace the occupational-limit check.

**Bounded statutory resolution:** [verified updates, U07](verified-updates.md#salary-part-time-roles-and-agreement-ceilings) records **2.72(19)(c)** as comparing the person's made-and-approved nominations in the named visa categories with the agreement's **permitted yearly number**, not another count of approvals or current employee headcount. Prepare the relevant made-and-approved counts, agreement year and occupation limits. Refresh temporal provisions and agreement terms; replacement credits or other adjustments remain unestablished unless supported. Successful form access or total headroom alone is not proof of an available place.

## Decisions, approval lifecycle and existing holders

<a id="n69"></a>
### N69 — Use the written nomination decision as the status evidence

**Decision maker notifies sponsor.** The framework calls for written approval/refusal within a reasonable period, with the written decision and refusal reasons where relevant. Keep the decision, notification date/method, identifiers and reasons. A payment receipt, agent update or sponsor application is not nomination approval. Carry the actual nomination status into visa matching and retrieve any review/response time rules immediately where the decision is adverse.

<a id="n70"></a>
### N70 — Model approval cessation as earliest-event logic

**Sponsor and nominee.** Prepare the approval date, linked visa application and finality status, sponsor status, withdrawal receipt and visa grant events. The lifecycle account ordinarily uses **12 months after approval**, but a qualifying linked visa application not finally determined at that point changes that expiry analysis. If final determination/withdrawal occurs later than that period, its date can itself cease approval. Receipt of the sponsor's written withdrawal or grant of the SID visa is also a ceasing event. Other sponsor/agreement events appear at N71.

Retrieve regulation 2.75(2), the applicable period and meaning of finally determined before calculating dates. Do not treat visa refusal/withdrawal as either always immediately terminating nomination or never affecting it (N78).

<a id="n71"></a>
### N71 — Add sponsorship expiry, renewal, cancellation and agreement events

**Sponsor.** The lifecycle account includes a nomination end day **three months after SBS approval ceases**, subject to renewed SBS status or a qualifying undecided application made before the sponsorship end day; later refusal of that application, cancellation under section 140M(1) and cessation of the work agreement can also matter. Obtain original approval/end dates, renewal lodgement/decision records, cancellation and agreement events.

**Timing uncertainty:** renewal exceptions and end-day wording require exact legal construction. Retrieve regulation 2.75(2) and applicable periods; do not promise a universal grace period or assume a late renewal application preserves the nomination.

<a id="n72"></a>
### N72 — Keep transfer nomination expiry separate from granted visa validity

**Example:** the nomination supporting the original visa ceases on grant. A later nomination approved for a new employer has no special employer-transfer ceasing event and may reach the ordinary approval limit or another earlier event. That expiry is not itself proof the already granted visa ends, sponsor obligations cease or annual repeat nominations are required. Check visa validity/conditions, sponsorship obligations and nomination approval as different legal questions.

<a id="n73"></a>
### N73 — Check the exempt-occupation multiple-employer route

**Existing SID holder.** A successfully nominated holder in an occupation covered by the applicable LIN 19/212 exemption may have a route to work for multiple employers, or change employer without a new nomination, while remaining in the same occupation and holding an effective SID visa. Retrieve the exact exemption and condition 8607 before relying on it. Occupation name similarity, a past exemption or working in health/religion generally does not establish current permission.

<a id="n74"></a>
### N74 — Separate employer-only and occupation changes for SID holders

**Non-exempt ordinary SID holder:** employer change in the same occupation generally calls for a new approved nomination covering the remaining visa period, rather than a new visa solely for that change, unless visa expiry or another requirement changes the answer. Occupation change with the same or a new employer calls for a new SID nomination **and** SID visa. The new nomination attracts its applicable SAF levy. Prepare actual current visa/expiry, employer, occupation and proposed changes; apply the interim-work analysis separately (N75), not a blanket no-work-pending statement.

<a id="n75"></a>
### N75 — Check interim work rights from the actual visa conditions

**Nominee.** Condition 8607 can provide a period after leaving the sponsor to find a new sponsor, apply for another visa or depart, including circumstances permitting work for a new sponsor before nomination approval. This reference supplies no unverified duration. Retrieve the applicable condition, actual grant/visa status, cessation date, any prior periods and proposed work facts. Nomination lodgement alone creates no general work permission, but the usual approved-nomination rule also does not prove all interim work is prohibited.

<a id="n76"></a>
### N76 — Analyse TSS/457 transitions only through the applicable SID transition

**Candidate transition accounts:** an existing TSS holder's employer-only change can use a SID nomination linked to the approved TSS visa; a TSS occupation change calls for SID nomination plus SID visa. A 457 holder's employer and/or occupation change calls for both. Test the applicable CSOL or Specialist occupation/income qualification and transitional instrument before selecting a route.

**Unresolved scope:** detailed matching, current availability and Labour Agreement transition are not settled by this account. Obtain current visa type/conditions/expiry, old/new occupation and sponsor, intended stream and applicable transitional provisions. Do not import the old TSS programme as the foundation or extend an employer-only transition to an occupation change.

<a id="n77"></a>
### N77 — Treat withdrawal as an authorised action with cross-stage consequences

**Sponsor/authorised representative only.** A pending nomination may be withdrawn in writing before finalisation; an approved nomination may be withdrawn before visa grant, with cessation tied to departmental receipt. Verify the permitted channel, including ImmiAccount where applicable, and preserve receipt/status evidence. This preparation workflow does not perform withdrawal. Before recommending that route, establish the linked visa consequence, any review/status impact and exact refund ground. Withdrawal after approval does not generally earn a refund merely because recruitment stopped.

<a id="n78"></a>
### N78 — Reconcile visa refusal with the nomination's actual remaining life

**Nominee and sponsor.** Visa refusal/withdrawal does not invariably end the nomination immediately; it remains person-specific and cannot be reused for a different nominee. But the broad survival statement must be read with N70's later final-determination/withdrawal ceasing event and every earlier sponsor/withdrawal event. Retrieve dates, finality/review status and regulation 2.75(2) before treating it as still available. An onshore nominee may face section 48 restrictions; check the applicable law and actual visa history before proposing another visa application.

<a id="n79"></a>
### N79 — Verify nomination review rights separately from visa review

**Sponsor-side nomination refusal.** The review account identifies section 338(9) and regulation 4.02 pathways, with an exclusion concerning specified Australian-operating SBS/former-SBS local-labour commitment or discriminatory-recruitment grounds. The cited regulation paragraphs **4.02(4B)** and **4.02(4)(d)** need authoritative checking.

**Unresolved operational essentials:** review body/regime, eligible review applicant, precise exclusion, valid notification and deadline are not established here. Obtain the complete refusal notice/reasons and notification evidence and retrieve applicable law urgently. Do not assume the visa applicant has standing to review the employer's nomination refusal, merge nomination and visa review, or invent a deadline.

## Refund branches: identify the payer, ground and stage

<a id="n80"></a>
### N80 — Start with the prescribed refund ground, not an unused-service calculation

**Payer and decision maker.** Regulation 2.73AA provides a prescribed-ground scheme for nomination fees/SAF. Prepare a written request by the payer where required; a reasonable refund without request can be available in specified circumstances. The recipient is the person who paid, not automatically the visa applicant, and payment may be in Australian or original-payment currency under applicable rules. Identify payer, receipts, nomination stage, event/ground and evidence. The branches below are possible grounds, not guaranteed refunds or authority to request/pay one.

<a id="n81"></a>
### N81 — Departmental error causing the nomination

**Candidate fee and SAF refund.** Establish the departmental mistake and that it caused this nomination to be made. **Example:** an official system error necessitating a second nomination to correct the problem. Prepare the error evidence, affected application identifiers, instructions/notice and payment receipts. An applicant's own mistake is not automatically this branch; distinguish it from N83 and retrieve the exact applicable ground.

<a id="n82"></a>
### N82 — Agreement occupation/ceiling withdrawal before decision

**Candidate fee and SAF refund:** an agreement party withdraws a pending nomination **before decision** because the occupation is not permitted or the annual 457/482 nomination ceiling has been reached/exceeded. Prepare the executed agreement, applicable year/occupation allocation, counts, withdrawal reason/timing and receipt. This is not any Labour Agreement withdrawal. Verify the legal ground and visa-link consequences before an authorised withdrawal; a nomination refusal is not the same event.

<a id="n83"></a>
### N83 — Incorrect SAF turnover/stay inputs

**Candidate fee and SAF refund:** incorrect calculation inputs may require pre-decision withdrawal of the pending nomination and a correct new nomination. The locked-input branch does not permit changing turnover/period after lodgement, simply adding levy or seeking a partial adjustment refund.

**Example without a rate assumption:** selecting a shorter stay than intended changes the levy input; prepare original versus intended period and turnover plus both applicable-date calculations. Do not borrow historical example rates. **Unresolved route conflict:** limited top-up language elsewhere does not identify when this prohibition does not apply. Resolve the exact correction power and linked visa consequences before action; replacement is not assumed to relink the existing visa.

<a id="n84"></a>
### N84 — Sponsorship application fails and pending nomination is withdrawn

**Candidate fee and SAF refund:** nomination remains undecided and is withdrawn before decision because the SBS application was withdrawn/refused. Policy contemplates the decision maker identifying related nominations and refunding without waiting for a written request. Preserve sponsor decision/withdrawal, nomination status, consent/withdrawal and payment evidence. Do not assume a refused nomination qualifies merely because its sponsor also failed; timing and event type matter.

<a id="n85"></a>
### N85 — Work agreement not yet entered into

**Candidate fee and SAF refund:** pending nomination is withdrawn before the work agreement is entered into, including failed negotiations. Prepare negotiation/agreement status, execution dates if any, nomination status and authorised withdrawal evidence. A later change of mind after agreement execution is not automatically this branch. Verify both pre-agreement and pre-decision timing and protect the linked visa position.

<a id="n86"></a>
### N86 — Specified final visa refusal, not any refusal

**Finalised nomination; payer.** The rule account permits consideration of fee and SAF refund for SID visa refusal under **sections 501, 501A or 501B**, or **PIC 4001, 4002, 4003, 4007 or 4020**, where the visa application is finally determined and relevant merits review is complete. **Policy distinction:** the described administrative approach refunds SAF only, despite broader statutory wording. Retrieve current law/policy and finality rules before quantifying the request. Prepare exact refusal ground, complete decision/review record and receipts. Do not substitute nomination refusal or another visa refusal ground.

<a id="n87"></a>
### N87 — Linked SID visa granted but proposed employment never commences

**Finalised nomination plus actual associated SID visa grant.** Non-commencement can support a statutory fee/SAF refund question, with the described policy limiting refund to SAF. Nomination approval without the linked visa grant does not satisfy this branch. Prepare visa grant, onshore/offshore grant circumstances, arrival and non-commencement evidence.

**Separate obligations:** retrieve the sponsor's non-commencement notification period/channel under regulation 2.84(2)(b), and the holder's commencement period/start point under condition 8607(4). The holder's clock depends on arrival after offshore grant versus grant while onshore; it is not the sponsor's reporting clock. Cancellation consideration can precede refund consideration; do not assume refund has no visa consequences. This reference does not notify anyone.

<a id="n88"></a>
### N88 — Employment ceases in the qualifying initial period

**Sponsor/payer; candidate SAF-only formula.** The rule account requires an approved nomination, proposed stay **more than one year**, cessation with sponsor/associated entity **within one year of commencement**, and a qualifying written request or reasonable no-request refund. It can apply to a 457 or 482 holder. Retrieve applicable time/amount rules before applying them.

The formula is **SAF paid minus SAF that would have been payable for a one-year nominated stay**, not a daily pro-rata refund of unused time; the nomination fee is not included. Prepare original period/turnover, actual levy receipt, applicable one-year levy calculation, commencement/cessation and payer evidence. **Examples:** a multi-year nomination ending in its first employment year may leave the one-year levy retained, whether the original stay was shorter or longer; calculate with verified rates, not copied examples. Separately retrieve the sponsor's cessation notification deadline/channel and assess holder conditions/work rights. No notification or request is performed here.

<a id="n89"></a>
### N89 — Recognise events that do not themselves create refund entitlement

**Payer.** Outside prescribed grounds there is no general fee/SAF refund. Non-qualifying examples include nomination refusal regardless of its ground; changing one's mind or discovering approval criteria cannot be met; withdrawal after nomination approval; or stopping because the nominee obtained another visa, including permanent residence. Apply any exact prescribed exception separately, not a generic “withdrawal equals refund” rule. Distinguish the specifically listed final visa-refusal branch from nomination refusal, and keep the statutory-versus-policy fee/SAF distinction visible.

## Definitions and official decision boundaries

<a id="n90"></a>
### N90 — Use the correct defined person and agreement

**Preparation terms:** nomination identifies the sponsor's proposed role/person; sponsorship is the separate approved framework; SBS means standard business sponsor. An Australian worker ordinarily means an Australian citizen/permanent resident in this context. An **eligible temporary visa holder** included in LMT's availability test is not thereby an Australian worker for every other test.

A qualifying work agreement must be signed/in effect, involve the Commonwealth and the relevant person/association/partnership, and authorise the relevant 482/494 recruitment, employment or services. Retrieve the legal definitions and actual agreement; do not assume an ordinary commercial agreement, unsigned negotiation or authority for another visa route suffices. Departmental approval/maintenance roles do not become sponsor or applicant responsibilities.

<a id="n91"></a>
### N91 — Keep official powers and unrelated programmes outside preparation authority

**Decision maker only:** official decisions depend on current legal powers/delegations and applicable instruments. Departmental assurance, training, internal support, escalation and consultation arrangements do not add applicant evidence requirements or confer powers on a preparer. A reference to another visa programme does not import that programme's rules into SID. For an unresolved official-power or policy question, prepare the precise provision, facts, effect and clarification needed for an authorised human; do not use internal channels or contact authorities.

Use only the branches triggered by the facts, while retaining unresolved branches in the working assessment. Before calling the nomination package prepared:

- Identify the actual sponsor/employer/agreement, nominee, payer, stream, occupation/code, workplace and period; reconcile the visa-side identifiers and status separately.
- Support certifications with truthful role, contract, qualification, remuneration, capacity and recruitment evidence; mark policy examples and discretionary requests as such.
- Record the applicable-date retrieval results for amounts, instruments, classification/caveats, form, periods, channels, agreement terms and any exemption relied on. An unperformed check remains unverified, not “passed”.
- For each unresolved conflict above, state the case-specific effect, missing authoritative interpretation/evidence and the next focused action. Do not silently resolve a conflict or discard the underlying expertise.
- Preserve pending, approved, refused, withdrawn and ceased as different statuses; assess linked-visa, family, work-condition, review and refund consequences before any proposed change. Preparation completion is not official acceptance or approval.
