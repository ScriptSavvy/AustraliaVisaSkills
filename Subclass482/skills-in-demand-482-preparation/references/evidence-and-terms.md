# Evidence handling, application procedures and terms

## Evidence dimensions: assess separately

| Dimension | Question | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Presence | Was the item actually supplied/inspected? | Filename or user claim is not the contents; not supplied for review is not proven absent |
| Readability | Can all relevant text/figures/pages be read? | Uncertain extraction is a gap; identify exact pages/fields, request one replacement at a time |
| Completeness | Does it include relevant pages, parties, dates, signatures, duties or periods? | Do not add a universal notarisation/signature requirement without applicable source |
| Consistency | Does it agree with other inspected records? | Identify exact conflicting statements; distinguish confirmed mismatch from possible concern |
| Currency | Is its period/version suitable for the actual event date? | Do not invent universal expiry periods for all certificates/documents |
| Verified compliance | Does the evidence meet a verified applicable requirement? | Readable/complete does not establish authenticity, eligibility or acceptance |

Use the five statuses from SKILL.md independently in relevant dimensions. A narrow check need not populate unrelated dimensions; say Not checked rather than pretend verification. For broad reports retain all six dimensions. Source conflicts and unknown triggers are Needs verification. Keep findings concise and give each issue one home.

## Conditional evidence inventory

This is an organising aid, not a list of documents universally required:
- **Nomination:** sponsor approval/agreement and legal entity records; position duties and organisation context; contract and employment arrangements; hours/location; salary components, comparator and market evidence, financial capacity; LMT copies/date records/submission or supported exemption; nominee and existing-family-holder details; fees/SAF/payment evidence; status/approval/decision records. Select exact branch requirements from nomination.md.
- **Visa:** passport/identity and name-change evidence where relevant; nomination acknowledgement/reference; qualifications/transcripts/training and employment/paid-work records; assessment/exemption evidence; English results/exemption evidence; medical recognition or required authorisation where triggered; family relationship/dependency/parental responsibility; insurance arrangements; actual health/character/information requests and grant/history records. Select exact branch requirements from visa-application.md.
- **Supporting/practical suggestions:** a truthful employment chronology, annotated role-to-core-task comparison, clear source-of-earnings breakdown and document index can make existing evidence easier to review. Do not treat these as prescribed forms or fabricate missing records. A CV alone may not prove dates, hours, duties or paid work; seek the next material source only when needed.

## Document processing and translations

Inspect local files with available authorised tools. Prefer redacted copies when enough; retain necessary dates/content for the stated check. Explain why any unredacted field is needed. Do not log passport numbers, account details or private employer information. Use neutral IDs in outputs. No extra external document service is required. Hosted model processing may involve its provider; do not promise local-only processing without checking the host. Obtain permission before another processor, third-party disclosure or retention.

If a scan is unreadable, do not infer what it says. Record readable parts, missing certainty and the exact replacement needed. Automatic OCR/text extraction does not establish accuracy or acceptance. Inspect tables and ambiguous dates carefully; preserve original text alongside any transcription correction. For translations, retrieve the actual Department instructions for translator details, certification and document format applicable to the case; do not impose blanket notarisation, apostille or Australian-only translation rules from memory. Keep original-language document and translation together where official instructions require. Do not translate source titles in a way that loses their identity.

Check current upload formats/limits in actual official instructions or the relevant portal. Do not hard-code upload limits or assume an attachment was submitted because it was uploaded to a chat. Copying a file, paying a fee, uploading evidence and lodging an application are separate events. Record acknowledgement/status evidence before describing submission. Alternative lodgement requires an authorised route; an outage is not blanket permission to use email or a paper form.

For requests, read the actual letter and identify requested item, recipient, delivery/receipt facts, date/time/timezone and extension wording. Calculate deadlines with an appropriate tool only when the controlling rule and facts are known. An extension request is not an approved extension. Do not promise time remains based on a generic processing period or negotiate/contact on behalf of the user without authorisation. Refer urgent consequential uncertainty promptly with the letter and event ledger.

## Integrity and professional limits

Do not manufacture references, earnings, advertisements, contracts, reasons for gaps or explanations for discrepancies. Ask the user for the true facts and distinguish explanation from corroborating evidence. Do not describe an inconsistency as fraud without support. Preserve differing versions and identify the correction needed rather than silently editing source facts.

Nomination fees, SAF obligations/refunds, sponsorship-payment prohibitions, visa charges and legal cost allocation are different topics. Do not tell an employer to transfer prohibited costs to the worker. Retrieve the actual applicable rule before a cost-recovery conclusion; identify payments/contracts for qualified review if concerns arise. No automatic refund, withdrawal or new application action is authorised.

## Plain-language terms

- **SID:** Skills in Demand, the current subclass 482 pathway covered here.
- **TSS:** Temporary Skill Shortage, an earlier subclass 482 program; some existing holders/transitions require separate dated checks.
- **Sponsor:** approved employer or relevant party to an executed work agreement, depending on route.
- **Nomination:** employer's application identifying the worker and proposed occupation/position; it is not the worker's visa application.
- **Nominee / primary applicant:** the nominated worker seeking the main visa criteria.
- **Secondary applicant / subsequent entrant:** a qualifying family applicant, applying with the main applicant or separately under the actual permitted route.
- **Lodgement:** formally making the relevant application in the prescribed way; not merely drafting, paying or uploading.
- **AMSR:** annual market salary rate—the pay for an equivalent Australian worker for relevant work/location, assessed under the applicable method.
- **CSIT / SSIT:** Core Skills / Specialist Skills Income Threshold. Select by stream and nomination date, not today's amount by default.
- **CSOL / ANZSCO:** Core Skills Occupation List / Australian and New Zealand Standard Classification of Occupations. Check exact version, code, tasks and applicable caveats.
- **LMT / ITO:** labour market testing / international trade obligation. An ITO may change a particular requirement, not all requirements automatically.
- **SAF:** Skilling Australians Fund levy, distinct from visa application charges and nomination fees.
- **TRN:** Transaction Reference Number. Keep sensitive identifiers out of reusable examples/reports when not needed.
- **Member of the family unit:** a legal family category with visa-specific exceptions; ordinary family membership alone is not proof.
- **PIC:** public interest criterion, such as health, character or integrity; source-specific applicability and evidence still need checking.
- **RPL:** recognition of prior learning. A qualification obtained through RPL may need examination of the actual learning/assessment basis.
- **Needs verification:** a real unresolved evidence or source gap, not a euphemism for a confirmed defect or a promised favourable result.
