# Skills in Demand 482: visa preparation within the nomination workflow

Use this reference to prepare the visa side of **one sponsorship → nomination → visa workflow**. Read [verified updates](verified-updates.md) first for dated official-source amendments and applicability gates, particularly occupation caveats, assessment scope, English and duration. Its targeted amendments take priority over an older description where the relevant provision and dates resolve the difference. Employer nomination approval and applicant visa eligibility are different decisions. An employer's assurance, an accepted online form or a successful nomination does not establish that a visa can be granted.

This is preparation guidance, not an eligibility determination or authority to submit, pay, withdraw, communicate with an employer or authority, or represent anyone. Prepare drafts and identify gaps for the person responsible to review. Policy guidance does not have force of law; controlling legislation and applicable instruments prevail, and policy allows individual merits to be considered. Examples below illustrate evidence or assessment approaches; they are not universal document mandates. Where a particular rule needs a date-specific check, record the unresolved question rather than present it as verified law.

## How to use the workflow

1. Establish stream, sponsor, position and nomination handoff in [V01](#v01)–[V02](#v02).
2. Prepare application-validity gates for **each person**, including family and skills-assessment commencement, in [V03](#v03)–[V08](#v08).
3. Prepare decision-stage requirements for all applicants in [V09](#v09)–[V14](#v14), and additional primary-applicant requirements in [V15](#v15)–[V21](#v21).
4. Reconcile grant location and duration in [V22](#v22)–[V23](#v23); plan conditions and changes in [V24](#v24)–[V26](#v26).
5. If nomination support fails or refusal occurs, use [V10](#v10) and [V27](#v27) immediately. Keep invalidity, withdrawal, refusal and review separate.

For the applicable steps, record: **person/owner; relevant application or decision date; proposed or actual fact; supporting evidence; mismatch or missing item; next preparation action; unresolved legal or source question**. Mark a step not applicable only with a reason. A complete preparation record is not a prediction of approval.

<a id="v01"></a>
## V01 — Establish stream and stage ownership

**Employer/sponsor prepares:** the sponsorship approval or executed work agreement, proposed occupation and actual duties, employment entity, location, remuneration, proposed stay and nomination materials. **Applicant prepares:** identity, visa history, family details, skills, experience, English and personal decision criteria. Reconcile the two sets of information before treating either stage as ready.

| Stream | Planning basis to reconcile with nomination | Applicant-side preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Specialist Skills | The described occupation framework uses a six-digit ANZSCO 2022 occupation in Major Groups 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6; earnings must meet the applicable Specialist Skills Income Threshold and be no less than comparable Australian workers in that occupation. | Match the nominated occupation and stream to the actual role and evidence; do not infer eligibility from a job title or salary alone. |
| Core Skills | Occupation on the applicable Core Skills Occupation List (CSOL), earnings meeting the applicable Core Skills Income Threshold and no less than Australian workers in the same occupation. | Check the occupation/list and any applicable restrictions through the nomination stage; carry the identical role and stream into visa preparation. |
| Labour Agreement | An employer's executed Commonwealth work agreement addresses a demonstrated labour need not met in the Australian labour market. Negotiation and signing replace the standard business sponsorship step, **not** nomination or visa application. | Obtain the applicable agreement provisions for the applicant, occupation, experience, English and proposed period. An unsigned proposal is not an executed agreement or an established concession. |

**Currency question:** occupation classification, list membership, income thresholds and agreement terms must be checked for the relevant event date. No fixed current stream threshold or full list is supplied here. Keep Australian-worker remuneration comparisons separate from the English earnings exemption in [V20](#v20).

The program overview describes work for the approved sponsor in the approved occupation, eligible dependants able to work and study, repeated entry while the visa remains in effect, and the same workplace rights as Australian citizens and permanent residents. Read this with actual grant conditions, exempt-occupation rules and the change-of-employer pathways below; it is not unrestricted work permission or a promise of a four-year grant.

**Completion check:** both owners have a consistent stream/role record, and the nomination stage remains a substantive employer-owned part of the same preparation, not an assumption that the applicant's visa checklist replaces it.

<a id="v02"></a>
## V02 — Obtain and reconcile the nomination handoff

**Employer/sponsor supplies:** nomination identifier, nominee identity, proposed occupation/code, stream, lodged/approved status, approval/cessation dates if relevant, sponsorship status and any sponsorship bar. **Applicant checks:** the identified nomination actually names them and their proposed occupation; the visa stream ordinarily matches the nomination stream.

At application, the identified nomination can be **approved and not ceased**, or **lodged and not yet decided**. A sponsorship bar on the nominator is a separate validity problem. Do not substitute a sponsor reference or an employment offer for the nomination identifier.

**Legacy nomination branch:** the described transition permits a SID visa application linked to a valid TSS nomination that has not ceased and has no associated TSS visa application. Systems treat the visa as Core Skills initially; the Department may amend it to Specialist Skills where the occupation and income eligibility are met. This is not permission to reuse every old nomination or for an applicant to self-authorise a stream change. Prepare the nomination status/history and occupation/income evidence for an explicit transition check.

**Decision handoff:** a pending nomination can support the application-stage link but cannot support visa grant until the identified nomination is actually approved and remains unceased. Recheck sponsor status **at nomination approval**, not just when the job offer or visa application was prepared. See [V10](#v10).

**Completion check:** a single reconciliation record shows the nomination identifier, person, occupation, stream, stage status and any transitional question. Ask the employer for missing nomination facts; do not guess them from applicant documents.

<a id="v03"></a>
## V03 — Prepare the form, channel and charge checks

The described application framework is Schedule 1 item 1240. Prepare **482V (Internet)** for a primary application or combined primary/family application; a secondary applicant not applying combined with the primary uses **482D (Internet)**. Confirm the approved form and channel for the intended application date. The lodgement instrument is described as **18/018**, with IMMI/LIN naming in the guidance; verify its operative text rather than assume that its age or online availability establishes currency.

If online application is not possible, alternative lodgement is limited to circumstances specified in the instrument **and individual Department authorisation**. A systems outage affecting someone whose visa is about to expire is a usual example, not an automatic entitlement or exhaustive list. The described route for requesting authorisation is the **ImmiAccount Technical Support Form**. Prepare evidence of the technical problem, timing and any authorisation; do not assume an email, paper form or outage report is a valid application. This reference does not authorise sending a request. Confirm the instrument's alternative-channel provisions and live technical process before relying on them.

Every **separate** application, including a subsequent entrant application, has its own base application charge. Combined applications have a different charging structure. Prepare a dated charge calculation using the applicable official schedule, without copying old amounts or treating a combined family as necessarily charge-free.

Refunds can arise under regulation 2.12F, including specified withdrawal circumstances involving absent approved sponsorship/nomination. A withdrawal is **not automatically refundable**. Keep ordinary charge/refund checks separate from the invalidity/refund consequence in [V05](#v05).

**Completion check:** form, intended channel, family grouping, charge basis and any individual alternative-channel authorisation are identified; no payment or application is made through this preparation exercise.

<a id="v04"></a>
## V04 — Check application location and immigration status person by person

An applicant may apply inside or outside Australia, but not in immigration clearance. The prescribed place and manner still apply.

For **every person in Australia at application**, prepare evidence that they hold a substantive visa or Subclass **010, 020 or 030** bridging visa. This includes primary applicants, secondary applicants and children born in Australia. Do not generalise this to any bridging visa or assume a child automatically bypasses the status test.

Prepare a person-by-person table of application location, existing visa class, expiry, conditions and the evidence supporting that status. Identify any other application-validity bar requiring separate examination; these status requirements are not an exhaustive list of all possible validity restrictions. Family members may be in different places if each satisfies their own applicable requirements.

**Completion check:** each person's intended application date and status are supported, not inferred from another family member's visa or from a future bridging visa expectation.

<a id="v05"></a>
## V05 — Distinguish validity, system acceptance and bridging status

Online acceptance is **not proof of Schedule 1 validity**. The described process requires the decision maker to check validity even where the system allowed lodgement; unmet validity requirements can lead to invalidation and refund of the visa application charge. Invalidity is not a merits refusal. Do not promise that an accepted application can be repaired, supports lawful stay, or will receive a particular refund without checking the precise defect and applicable process.

Bridging eligibility is also separate. The guidance describes possible bridging eligibility for a SID applicant who was in Australia when applying, or was outside Australia holding a substantive visa when applying. It does not establish automatic grant, activation, travel permission or immediate work rights.

Prepare and inspect any actual bridging grant/status record for class, commencement, expiry, travel and work conditions. Keep existing visa conditions in view until any replacement takes effect. If there is no bridging grant, record that as a missing fact rather than assume one exists.

**Completion check:** the record distinguishes application receipt, legal validity, substantive visa status, any bridging grant and actual rights. A defect or imminent expiry needs a specific, timely validity/status question, not a generic assurance that lodgement protects the applicant.

<a id="v06"></a>
## V06 — Choose the family application route, including newborns

Members of the family unit can make a combined application with the primary or with another claimed family-unit member. Later secondary applicants normally require their own separate **482D** application; they cannot simply be added to a pending application.

Keep these routes distinct:

- **Child born to the primary after application but before decision:** the described exception is regulation 2.08. Prepare birth evidence, parent/application linkage and the applicable notification/addition process; do not use the ordinary later-entrant rule to exclude this child.
- **Non-citizen child born in Australia to an existing SID visa holder:** section 78 is described as taking the child to hold the same kind/class of visa on the same terms and conditions at birth. Prepare the child's birth/parental visa evidence and confirm the notification and status-record process. This is not the pending-parent-application pathway.
- **Legacy family subsequent entry:** the described SID route includes family of a **TSS applicant or holder**, or a **Subclass 457 holder**. It does not say that the same sentence includes family of a 457 applicant. Establish the primary's exact status and transition before relying on it.

Relationship eligibility, sponsor agreement and visa duration remain separate questions, even after the application route is identified. Use [V11](#v11), [V10](#v10) and [V23](#v23).

**Completion check:** each family member has a supported relationship, chosen application/newborn route, their own location/status checks and a plan for sponsor coverage; do not confuse a birth-related visa status with evidence already being recorded by the Department.

<a id="v07"></a>
## V07 — Check mandatory skills-assessment commencement before application

Mandatory assessment is **not required of every SID applicant**. Establish whether the primary falls within the occupation/nationality or other class specified in the applicable instrument and whether an actual exemption applies. Record the assessing authority, nominated occupation, required assessment type, class trigger and exemption basis.

For a specified class without exemption, the described application-stage gate is that the applicant has **commenced the required assessment**, or has a suitable completed assessment within the required period. The form requests the assessment reference or an explanation of exemption.

A reference needs to be:

- issued to **this applicant** by the relevant assessing authority;
- for the correct assessment type and nominated occupation; and
- unrelated to a final unsuccessful outcome or an assessment outside the applicable period. The described age limit excludes an assessment dated **more than three years before application**; confirm the instrument and dates for the actual case.

A valid pending-assessment reference can satisfy this validity gate while other validity requirements remain to be met. A failed or outdated final assessment does not do so merely because it has a reference number, unless an actual exemption changes the position. Assessment success at decision is a second gate in [V15](#v15).

Where exemption is claimed without a reference, prepare the precise instrument exemption and evidence. A decision maker can consider a different actual exemption even if not identified on the form. If the claim is unclear and the validity issue is quickly/easily remediable, an opportunity to rectify may be considered; **do not rely on a guaranteed opportunity after application**. An Australian qualification based on recognition of prior learning from overseas also needs decision-stage examination; calling it an Australian qualification does not settle suitability.

**Completion check:** there is either a checked, applicant-specific correct assessment reference/commencement record or a supported exemption claim, with unsuccessful/age/type issues explicitly recorded before application.

<a id="v08"></a>
## V08 — Assemble a targeted application pack and keep it consistent

Create a working evidence index for the applicable steps, not a universal upload list. Examples of useful records are identity/status evidence, the nomination reconciliation, family and sponsor agreement evidence, the skills-assessment reference or exemption explanation, relevant qualification/work records and English result or exemption evidence. Use actual requirements and unresolved facts to decide what is needed.

For every factual assertion shared with nomination preparation—name, occupation, duties, employer, worksite, remuneration, experience, family and proposed stay—compare the applicant evidence with the employer's record. Explain a genuine discrepancy or obtain corrected evidence; do not rewrite facts to fit a role. Preserve truthful originals and identify unclear or unverifiable claims.

**Completion check:** each applicable gate below has evidence or a precise gap and next action. Do not label the pack grant-ready solely because every file in a suggested checklist exists.

<a id="v09"></a>
## V09 — Prepare substantial-compliance history for all applicants

Decision-stage requirements apply across streams to both primary and secondary applicants. For an applicant in Australia, the described test is **substantial compliance** with conditions of their last substantive visa and subsequent bridging visa. It is not an invented requirement of perfect compliance, and this reference does not define how substantial compliance is decided.

Prepare the relevant visa/condition chronology and any departures, including dates, factual context and supporting material. Do not hide a departure or declare it fatal without assessing the actual provision. Check clauses 482.211/482.311 and the applicable compliance guidance for a material issue.

**Completion check:** each onshore applicant has a reviewed chronology or a specific unresolved compliance question, rather than a family-wide assumption of compliance.

<a id="v10"></a>
## V10 — Recheck nomination support and failure consequences at decision

**Primary gate:** the **nomination identified in the visa application** must be approved under section 140GB, remain unceased, and have been approved when its nominator was an approved work sponsor. A nomination mistakenly approved against expired sponsorship cannot support grant. A different newly lodged nomination cannot simply replace the identified nomination to secure this visa application.

**Secondary gate:** establish inclusion in the identified nomination, or prepare written agreement that the family member may be a secondary sponsored person from the sponsor associated with the **most recent approved nomination** for the primary. The described written-agreement route includes a standard business sponsor, former standard business sponsor, work-agreement party or former party. Do not obtain an arbitrary current employer letter without checking which sponsor is relevant. Missing agreement evidence is described as something the decision maker should request; prepare it rather than assume the request has already been met.

**Employer/sponsor owns:** nomination status updates, sponsor-at-approval evidence, relevant family inclusion/agreement and any review status. **Applicant owns:** checking the visa linkage and declarations, keeping evidence aligned, and preparing a response if support changes.

| Event affecting identified nomination | Described visa consequence to distinguish |
|---|---|
| Approved but subsequently ceased; refused; or withdrawn | The decision maker is to invite comments about the nomination information and afford an opportunity to withdraw before refusal, subject to the pre-authorised withdrawal branch. |
| Visa form pre-authorises withdrawal if nomination is refused or withdrawn | On either of those events, the visa application is treated as withdrawn immediately and disposed of under section 47(2); it is not an application still available for merits determination. |
| Nomination cessation alone | Do not extend the automatic pre-authorised withdrawal trigger to cessation: that extension is not established here. |

Prepare the actual nomination outcome, date, visa withdrawal declaration, notices, response dates and refund question. Withdrawal, refusal, invalidity and review are distinct; neither a refund nor a review right follows automatically from the event. See [V27](#v27).

**Completion check:** status is refreshed for the identified nomination, family sponsorship is separately covered, and any failed-support branch is resolved without silently swapping nominations.

<a id="v11"></a>
## V11 — Prepare family relationship evidence without hiding definition conflicts

**Apply the default and special routes separately.** The bounded regulation check in [verified updates, U07](verified-updates.md#family-definition-default-and-special-existing-family-route) qualifies the earlier broad family description. Under regulation **1.12(2)**, prepare partner evidence; qualifying unmarried/non-engaged child or stepchild evidence for someone under 18; **dependency evidence for ages 18 to under 23**; and the incapacity-related dependency evidence in **1.05A(1)(b) for age 23 or older**. The default also includes a dependent child of a qualifying child. Do not automatically admit a descendant without the actual relationship/dependency predicates. **Exactly 23 belongs to the 23-plus limb**, not a gap or the under-23 category. Ordinary financial dependence alone does not establish the incapacity limb.

**Existing-holder special branch:** only where the precise existing-secondary-visa/status and application circumstances in **1.12(5)** are established does **482.312(2)** supply its special under-23 wording without the ordinary dependency limb. Prepare current and earlier 457/482 grants, family linkage, primary identity, dates and changed-family circumstances. The stored same-primary expectation needs focused interpretation if material; do not infer permission to change primary applicant. Holding any former visa is not enough, and this is not a universal age/dependency waiver.

**Preparation action:** record age at the relevant event, relationship, marital/de facto/engagement status, dependency/incapacity facts and primary/secondary visa history. Refresh controlling provisions and temporal rules for the actual case. Preserve unresolved special-route, continuity or changed-primary questions rather than select whichever definition is convenient. This bounded textual check covers law compiled as at **2 October 2026**, not a comprehensive later-date verification.

For a secondary **under 18 at application**, prepare parental-responsibility/consent and best-interests facts for PIC 4017 and 4018. Birth certificates, custody orders or consent records may help establish facts in the particular case; the complete legal tests and universal document requirements are not supplied here.

**Completion check:** family eligibility, sponsor consent and grant duration are recorded as three separate gates; any exact-age, dependency, descendant or continuity issue has a specific legal question and evidence plan.

<a id="v12"></a>
## V12 — Prepare sponsorship-payment and insurance declarations accurately

**Sponsorship-payment conduct:** all primary and secondary applicants are subject to the described previous-**three-year** assessment of prohibited conduct under subsections 245AR(1), 245AS(1), 245AT(1) or 245AU(1). There is also a limb permitting conduct to be disregarded where the decision maker considers that reasonable. Do not turn this into either automatic refusal for every transaction or an entitlement to disregard.

A declaration of no prohibited conduct is generally accepted absent contrary information. Where a fact may contradict it, prepare a truthful transaction chronology and supporting records for the statutory conduct/disregard assessment; do not conceal it or label every ordinary payment prohibited. Departmental internal escalation is not an applicant contact obligation.

**Health insurance:** all applicants need adequate arrangements for the intended stay. The described decision approach accepts the criterion where the primary declares adequate arrangements for that duration. It does **not** establish a mandatory policy certificate for every person at lodgement. Prepare accurate coverage/intended-stay facts and any declaration; distinguish that decision criterion from ongoing condition **8501**.

> **Separate-entrant insurance question:** the acceptance wording is framed through the primary's declaration while the requirement covers all applicants. For a separate entrant, establish the applicable declaration/evidence mechanism and adequate coverage for their actual stay; do not invent a universal certificate requirement or assume a past primary declaration automatically covers a new person.

**Completion check:** declarations reflect the facts, insurance covers the intended period as applicable, and any conduct or separate-entrant issue has a targeted next action.

## V13 — Review adverse-information and public-interest gates individually

Adverse information for primary and secondary applicants is linked to clauses 482.216/482.316 and regulations 1.13A/1.13B. The complete operative test is not supplied here. Identify any relevant information, person/entity, dates and response evidence, then obtain the applicable test and exceptions; do not infer automatic refusal from the label “adverse information”.

Use this public-interest inventory for **each applicant across streams**, selecting evidence according to the actual criterion, age and circumstances:

| Criterion | Preparation topic |
|---|---|
| PIC 4001 | Character, including the character test and relevant penal history. |
| PIC 4002 | Character/security assessment. |
| PIC 4003 | Foreign Minister requirements, including relevant foreign-policy/travel sanctions concerns. |
| PIC 4003B | Unreasonable risk of unwanted transfer of critical technology. |
| PIC 4004 | Debts to the Commonwealth. |
| PIC 4007 | Health requirement and any applicable waiver pathway. |
| PIC 4010 | Settlement requirement. |
| PIC 4013 | Relevant immigration history/exclusion period. |
| PIC 4014 | Relevant exclusion period. |
| PIC 4017 | Parental responsibility/custody for applicable minor children. |
| PIC 4018 | Best interests of applicable minor children. |
| PIC 4019 | Values statement for primary and secondary applicants who have turned **18 at application**. |
| PIC 4020 | Integrity of information and documents. |
| PIC 4021 | Passport requirement. |

This is a criterion inventory, not the complete tests and not a blanket demand for police checks, medicals, debt clearances or every listed document. For a triggered issue, establish the controlling criterion, applicable exception/waiver and evidence requested for that person; record what is missing. Do not invent a waiver merely because a criterion has one in some circumstances.

**Completion check:** each applicable criterion has a supported status or a specific gap; conditional child and adult criteria are not indiscriminately applied to the whole family.

<a id="v14"></a>
## V14 — Check special-return history for all applicants

The described primary and secondary framework includes Schedule 5 special return criteria **5001, 5002 and 5010** under clauses 482.218/482.318. Prepare relevant previous departures, removal/deportation, cancellation and exclusion-history facts where applicable, with the actual notices and dates. These are examples of facts to inspect, not a replacement statement of the three tests.

The full criteria and exceptions are not reproduced. Check the actual Schedule 5 provisions and applicable exclusion-period guidance before deciding the effect of a history item. Do not assume all criteria are cleared by a current passport, or that every prior immigration problem creates an unwaivable bar.

**Completion check:** each person's relevant return history is considered, with any unresolved criterion/exception precisely identified.

<a id="v15"></a>
## V15 — Establish genuine intention, skills and the decision-stage assessment outcome

All primary applicants need a genuine intention to perform the nominated occupation and a genuine associated position. An approved nomination generally supports position genuineness, but new facts or applicant-specific concerns can justify further enquiries—for example, evidence suggesting the position exists mainly to secure entry/stay rather than meet a labour shortage.

Compare actual duties, applicant history and intention. Very limited experience paired with a specialist management nomination is an **example of a concern to explain**, not an automatic refusal rule. Prepare a factual explanation and relevant evidence for a genuine mismatch; do not invent experience or rely solely on nomination approval. Relevant claims may be checked overseas where necessary; see [V21](#v21).

The primary must have the skills, qualifications and employment background needed for the nominated occupation: the described stream provisions are 482.221(2)–(3), 482.231(2)–(3) and 482.242A. Existing evidence may be sufficient. Additional evidence or a demonstration in a specified manner can be requested; a **discretionary formal skills assessment** is described as appropriate only when absolutely necessary, not a universal requirement.

**Mandatory-assessment decision gate:** where the assessment was pending at application, prepare the **successful outcome**, correct occupation, authority and relevant validity dates. A valid commencement reference establishes neither success nor final suitability. If successful completion evidence is missing, the described process is to request it and allow a reasonable period before decision; no fixed waiting period or entitlement to indefinite delay is stated. Track actual request deadlines and assessment progress.

**Completion check:** a primary suitability record connects genuine intention and role, skills evidence and any successful mandatory assessment; it explicitly distinguishes application-stage commencement from decision-stage success.

<a id="v16"></a>
## V16 — Match occupational skills and choose proportionate evidence

Use the nominated occupation's **specific Indicative Skill Level**, relevant qualifications and/or experience and actual tasks in the applicable ANZSCO version, rather than only the general skill-level label. Compare the nomination duties with the applicant's real training and work.

- Qualifications must be relevant. Architecture nomination with only a law degree is an example calling for further relevant evidence; advertising study for a marketing role is an example of potentially relevant study. Neither example establishes a universal degree mandate.
- Skills covering the **bulk/core elements** can suffice even without every listed task. A “not elsewhere classified” occupation does not require proficiency in all possible listed tasks.
- Highly skilled applicants may specialise in a narrow range of tasks. Explain the specialisation and its occupational relevance; do not reject it just for not covering the full range.

Where no mandatory assessment applies, choose evidence to establish actual skills. Examples include transcripts, practical professional training, a comprehensive CV and work references with meaningful duties. A vague CV where qualifications **and** experience are relevant may call for more specific references rather than an automatic formal assessment.

Potential reasons for further enquiry include documentary authenticity, concerns about the registered training organisation, unclear recognised-prior-learning basis, work in a different occupation/unit group, or insufficient correspondence between qualifications/experience and role. These justify targeted investigation, not automatic findings of fraud or refusal. Prepare truthful explanations and corroboration for actual concerns.

**Job Ready Program branch:** a former **485 or 500** holder with a successful **Job Ready Program Final Assessment in the nominated occupation** may have the skills criterion accepted without further investigation under the described approach. Identify the applicant, successful final outcome and occupation; do not confuse earlier program stages with the final assessment.

**Australian Recognised Trades Certificates (ARTCs)** are expressly not acceptable for migration purposes in this guidance. Do not substitute an ARTC for the relevant migration assessment or qualifying Job Ready outcome.

**Completion check:** an occupation-to-evidence matrix explains core tasks/specialisation, qualification relevance, work relevance and any concern requiring clarification, without demanding every example document or every ANZSCO task.

<a id="v17"></a>
## V17 — Separate licensing, medical recognition and English exemption

Except for the medical-practitioner requirement described below, demonstrating existing Australian licensing/registration is **not generally a visa-grant prerequisite** in the guidance. Nevertheless, a primary holder must obtain any mandatory registration, licence or membership **before performing the occupation** under condition 8607. Visa grant is not professional permission to practise.

**Medical practitioners:** prepare written evidence from the relevant Australian authority/medical board recognising the qualifications as entitling the person to practise and confirming either registration or that registration **will be given after visa grant**. Actual registration need not already be held at decision under this described grant criterion.

**Registration pathway:** establish the authority, recognition stage, expected timing, permitted scope and any external formal training, supervised practice or pre-registration testing. Guidance advises considering a more appropriate visa for the training period if mandatory authorisation will take **more than 90 days** or require formal training external to the employer. It describes SID as inappropriate for the specified supervised-practice/pre-registration-testing route, **except** professions granting provisional registration while necessary full-registration training is undertaken. Do not turn the exception into a blanket permission to work unregistered or the training advice into a universal refusal rule.

Evidence of already-held required licence, registration or professional membership can generally support acceptance of the skills requirement without further investigation. That is an evidentiary approach, not immunity from other criteria or all further enquiries.

Keep three gates separate:

1. Medical qualification recognition and present/future registration evidence for grant.
2. Actual mandatory authority before performing work and condition-compliance timing.
3. The **already-granted**, unexpired authority and English-level equivalence required for the English exemption in [V20](#v20). A promise of future medical registration does not automatically meet that exemption.

**Completion check:** there is a feasible, evidenced professional-authorisation pathway and no assumption that the visa, skills evidence and English exemption are interchangeable.

<a id="v18"></a>
## V18 — Prepare the sport-specific skills case where applicable

Sport-related skills guidance considers personal commitment, physical fitness, sporting ability and relevant experience in place of formal qualifications. Prepare evidence of:

- an elite **full-time professional** record in the nominated occupation;
- substantial **recent** full-time elite playing/coaching experience assessed against Australian standards;
- genuinely professional relevant/recent experience: this sporting guidance excludes trainee, apprentice, cadet and non-professional experience from that assessment; and
- the nominated principal sporting position not requiring supplementation by ancillary unapproved work or income. The described model is one approved position, not work for other employers/activities outside the nomination.

With **at least three years of elite professional experience in lieu of formal qualifications**, the decision maker may accept skills without further enquiry. Prepare past income records substantiating elite professional status against national income levels and showing sport as the principal income source. Relevant national sport-administration-body references about skill and income may also be requested.

This is occupation-specific discretionary skills guidance, **not a universal three-year SID experience minimum**, not automatic acceptance of every athlete, and not an exclusion of all training/placements under the separate general experience pathways.

**Completion check:** sporting status, recency, full-time professional record, income and the approved position are explained separately from the pre-application experience calculation.

<a id="v19"></a>
## V19 — Calculate pre-application experience and assess industry flexibility

For Specialist/Core primary applicants, the described requirement is at least **one year full-time equivalent** in the nominated occupation or related field within the **five years ending immediately before application**. It is assessed at decision but anchored to pre-application experience; later work must not silently fill an application-date shortfall.

Full-time, part-time and casual work may aggregate to at least **12 months full-time equivalent**. No hours-per-week conversion formula is supplied here. Prepare exact dates, hours/patterns, breaks, duties, skill level and supporting employment evidence; identify overlap so the same time is not credited twice. Explain the basis for equivalence rather than invent a standard hourly divisor.

The year is **not additional** to experience used for occupational skill requirements. For Labour Agreement applicants, the described default is the same requirement unless the work agreement specifies a different **total period**. Do not infer that a period concession waives every other condition; obtain the executed clause.

**Ordinary work and related fields:** work should be in the occupation or involve the same/closely same/closely related tasks at the **same skill level**. A similar title, industry or lower-level supporting role is insufficient by itself. Kitchen Hand/Cook versus Chef is an illustrative lower-skill comparison, not a substitute for examining actual tasks and classification. Relevant industry peak-body endorsement should be considered if available; it is not a universal compulsory certificate.

The ordinary guidance excludes **voluntary or unpaid employment arrangements**, while expressly preserving the following conditional industry pathways:

| Experience pathway | What to prepare and what not to assume |
|---|---|
| Masters/PhD activities | Relevant experience may count, for example medical/research activities. Identify actual work/activities, relevance and periods; a postgraduate enrolment alone is not work credit. |
| Formal placement, internship or apprenticeship in a CRICOS-registered course | Relevant occupational experience may count. Establish the course registration, formal arrangement, activities, dates and occupational relevance. Professional Year internships and medical internships/final-year clinical placements are examples, not the entire list or automatic credit for every course. |
| Formal study arrangement outside a CRICOS course | It must be at the relevant occupation's skill level to be considered. Document the formal arrangement, tasks and skill level; do not accept all non-CRICOS training automatically. |
| Performing arts experience during study | Performance experience may count for the relevant performing occupation, recognising potentially exceptional, globally in-demand talent. Evidence performances and relevance, not merely student status. |
| Niche/generalist occupational experience | Relevant industry experience with transferable skills may count; management consulting and specialist technology are examples. Explain which actual skills transfer and at what occupational level. |

> **Unpaid-placement uncertainty:** ordinary unpaid/voluntary employment is excluded, but the guidance also permits consideration of formal study/placement and other industry experience without fully resolving how unpaid placements interact with that exclusion. Do **not** automatically accept or reject every unpaid placement. Record whether it was paid, the formal course/placement arrangement, work performed, supervision, hours, level, relevance and dates. Prepare an ordinary qualifying-work subtotal separately from the period relying on this flexibility. Obtain an authoritative interpretation of the applicable industry pathway and unpaid exclusion before stating that the disputed period meets the requirement. If it changes the result, show both scenarios and the exact unresolved period; do not invent a legal resolution.

**Completion check:** a dated experience ledger shows qualifying pre-application periods, equivalence basis, occupation/related-field level, any agreement modification and each conditional/disputed industry period. Internal departmental escalation about uncertain experience is not an applicant contact instruction.

## V21 — Align employment entity and respond proportionately to verification

For Specialist/Core applicants, the described requirement is employment in the nominated occupation and, unless an instrument-specified exempt occupation applies:

- an **overseas-business sponsor:** position within the sponsoring business;
- an **Australian-business sponsor:** position within the sponsor or its associated entity; and
- an intended direct employer–employee relationship.

**Employer/sponsor prepares:** actual employing entity, associated-entity basis where applicable, worksite and duties, direct employment arrangement and any claimed occupation exemption. **Applicant prepares:** a consistent account of who employs them, where they work and what they do. Check these facts jointly with the nomination stage.

Working at another business's premises or using its tools does **not by itself** disprove direct employment. Equally, a contract title does not establish the substantive relationship. Record the real arrangement and any exempt-occupation instrument basis without treating all offsite work as prohibited or all labour arrangements as acceptable.

Claims may be verified overseas only where relevant to criteria and necessary for the decision. The described relevant locations include citizenship/usual residence, the origin of references or qualifications, and, for an onshore applicant, the country of the last visa used to enter Australia. These are departmental verification routes, not a universal requirement for the applicant to obtain overseas-post clearance. Prepare authentic, intelligible evidence and truthful clarification of actual concerns; do not contact staff-only channels.

**Completion check:** employment facts reconcile across both stages and evidence requests respond to a real criterion/concern, not an invented universal clearance procedure.

<a id="v22"></a>
## V22 — Reconcile grant location and the primary's period

At grant, applicants may be inside or outside Australia, but not in immigration clearance. Family members may be in different locations at application and grant provided **each** meets their relevant requirements; this does not waive an individual's onshore application-status gate.

The described primary visa period starts **on the grant date** and follows the stay proposed in the identified nomination. It is **generally up to four years**, not a guaranteed four years or an exhaustive absolute cap. International Trade Obligations and other factors can affect maximum stay; duration must be reconciled with nomination provisions and clause 482.511 before calculation. Prepare nominated period, nationality/passport and any relevant trade-obligation basis; do not calculate expiry from intended arrival or application date by default.

The visa is described as permitting multiple travel/entries before the visa period ends. This does not confer bridging travel permission or permission after expiry.

> **Primary-duration qualification:** the overview's four-year description is qualified by the grant rules. The targeted official Core Skills page check records a conditional **up-to-five-year period for Hong Kong passport holders**; see [verified updates](verified-updates.md) for its scope and dated check before applying it. Do not generalise that Core-page statement to all passports or streams. Keep the nominated period and any verified passport/trade-obligation or other exceptional basis explicit; do not promise a duration until both nomination and visa rules are reconciled.

**Completion check:** person-by-person grant location is recorded, proposed period is connected to the identified nomination, and any exceptional duration is supported rather than inferred.

## V23 — Calculate family duration separately from family eligibility

**Use clause 482.511(1), table items 3–5, and [verified updates, U07](verified-updates.md#family-grant-duration-is-not-a-combinedseparate-shortcut).** Combined versus separate application is an input, not a complete duration rule. Earlier shorthand placing every age restriction only under separate applications, or exempting everyone joining a 457/482 holder, must not be used.

Item 4 has specific family-category predicates, an incapacity exclusion and item-5 priority. Where it actually applies, it uses the earlier of the primary's stay end and the end of the day before the secondary applicant's 23rd birthday. Item 5 has specific primary Hong Kong/British National (Overseas) passport circumstances and, where relevant, a class instrument. Do not replace those predicates with a universal non-partner cutoff, blanket joining-holder exemption or universal partner exception.

Record combined/separate route, relationship, birth date, primary visa type/expiry, earlier family grants, incapacity/dependency evidence and claimed passport/class exception. **Do not calculate or certify a family expiry date** in the unresolved special-family/age-23/TSS/passport-class branches: unusual partner/descendant effects under 1.12(5), already-23 cases, a primary TSS visa outside the displayed item-3 list and relevant passport classes require applicable authority and qualified interpretation. Continue evidence organisation; do not infer a matching expiry merely from the primary's grant. The regulation check covers the compilation as at **2 October 2026**, not an exhaustive later-date verification.

**Completion check:** relationship eligibility and duration are independently analysed, with the primary's expiry and any age/exception issue visible. A person can require duration analysis even if their family relationship has been established.

## V25 — Choose the employer/occupation-change pathway and track interim periods

Before a change, identify the **visa actually held**, occupation, exemption basis, expiry, current/new sponsor and approval dates. Employer preparation and applicant condition checks run together; a new job offer is not an approved nomination.

| SID-holder change | Employer/sponsor-owned preparation | Applicant-owned preparation |
|---|---|---|
| Exempt occupation described in **LIN 19/212**, after successful nomination by an approved work sponsor | Check the instrument exemption and same-occupation facts. | The described route allows simultaneous multiple employers and change/cessation of initial employer without a new nomination while staying in the same occupation and the visa remains in effect. Verify the exemption and 8607 interaction; do not extend it to every occupation or an occupation change. |
| Non-exempt employer change, same occupation | Required new employer nomination must be **approved** to support work for the remaining existing visa period; new employer pays a new SAF levy at new nomination lodgement. | A new visa is generally not required solely for same-occupation employer change unless expiry is approaching. Track approval and existing visa expiry; interim periods are not unrestricted ongoing work. |
| Occupation change, with current or new sponsor | Prepare a **new SID nomination** for the new occupation. | A **new SID visa** is also required. Same employer does not remove the occupation-change gate. Check permission/approval timing before changing work. |

The described 8607 cessation framework permits time to find another sponsor, seek another visa or arrange departure, subject to **no more than 180 consecutive days and 365 cumulative days during the visa period** not working in accordance with the relevant sponsored-work requirements in 8607(1)/(2). This includes work for a new sponsor **before nomination approval**. The limits are not merely unemployment days and do not reset to a clean cumulative history with each employer change.

Prepare a continuous dated ledger of compliant sponsored work, cessation, interim work, employer/occupation, nomination lodgement/approval and visa expiry. Reconcile the full condition's start/stop counting mechanics and permitted interim work before advising that a particular activity is allowed. Do not treat the numerical period as blanket authorisation for any work or promise an extension of stay.

**Completion check:** selected change route, employer approvals/levy responsibility, applicant visa/condition gates and both consecutive/cumulative periods are reconciled; unresolved interim permission is stated precisely.

The described transition says new TSS/457 applications are no longer available and distinguishes:

| TSS holder changes employer | New employer's **SID nomination**, linked through transition to the existing TSS visa. |

## V27 — Prepare refusal/review facts without promising review rights

If refusal or loss of nomination support occurs, preserve the actual notices, notification dates/method, application location, identified nomination status, sponsor identity/type, any nomination/sponsorship review record and the visa withdrawal declaration. Prepare a **time-sensitive review-pathway question immediately**; no review deadline is supplied here, so do not wait for a generic checklist or invent a filing period. This reference does not authorise lodging review or withdrawing an application.

**Location classification is at visa application**, not simply where the person is when refused. The described primary-review gates depend on facts **at the visa refusal decision**:

- **Primary applied onshore:** approved unceased nomination **or** pending review of sponsorship refusal **or** pending review of nomination refusal.
- **Primary applied offshore:** the guidance sets out an Australian-business nominator **and** an approved unceased nomination; **or** pending sponsorship-refusal review; **or** pending nomination-refusal review. It also expressly excludes review where the sponsor is an **overseas business**.

> **Offshore-review uncertainty:** the “and/or” grouping is not sufficiently clear to decide how the Australian-business condition applies to all alternative limbs. Preserve that uncertainty together with the express overseas-business exclusion; do not invent an overseas-business pending-review exception. Check the controlling reviewable-decision provisions and the exact refusal-time facts before a conclusion.

**Standing is a separate gate:** the described onshore pathway allows the applicant to seek review; the offshore pathway assigns standing only to the relevant Australian business that sought sponsor approval or nominated the person, **not the offshore visa applicant**. This is not a complete secondary-applicant refusal/review regime. Do not extrapolate primary rules or assume a family member can file in their own name.

The described regime is stated to apply to SID/TSS/457 decisions on or after **13 December 2018**. That historical statement does not establish the current tribunal, current review jurisdiction or deadlines. The guidance's older AAT terminology requires a framework check. Examine the applicable Act provisions on reviewable decisions and standing (including sections 338/347), regulation 4.02, the actual decision notice and current filing rules before stating a pathway or last date.

**Completion check:** reviewability, correct person with standing, tribunal/framework and exact filing deadline are individually established or explicitly unresolved. A nomination review, visa review, automatic withdrawal, invalidity and merits refusal are not treated as interchangeable events.

## V28 — Working definitions and responsibility boundaries

Use these distinctions when reconciling employer and applicant records:

- **Approved work sponsor:** the described statutory approval remains effective, not cancelled/ceased, or the person is a non-Minister party to a work agreement. A partnership or unincorporated association may qualify. A trading name or old approval letter alone does not establish current effective status; check the relevant approval and event dates.
- **Australian worker:** Australian citizen or permanent resident for the remuneration comparisons described here.
- **Sponsorship:** employer approval process, distinct from nominating an occupation for a particular person.
- **Nomination:** employer identifies the occupation for a specific applicant; it is not the visa application or a guarantee of the applicant's suitability.
- **Work agreement:** an in-effect agreement signed by the Commonwealth (through the relevant Minister or Ministers) and the person, partnership or unincorporated association, authorising relevant recruitment/employment/services for the covered 482/494 arrangements under regulation 2.76. Check actual parties, dates and scope; an agreement's mention of another subclass does not transfer that subclass's rules into SID preparation.

Departmental approval, monitoring, policy review and processing updates are responsibilities of authorised officials, not applicant duties. Internal delegation checks, staff training, assurance frameworks, consultation and escalation channels neither authorise the applicant/assistant to exercise decision-making powers nor require the applicant to reproduce internal departmental processes. A blank administrative recordkeeping field creates no applicant obligation.

Supporting guidance can include sponsorship, nomination, agreements, occupations, adverse information, obligations, conditions and notification procedures. References to legacy TSS or other visa subclasses do **not** incorporate their rules as current SID authority. For a live unresolved question, identify the controlling provision, relevant date and exact factual issue; do not infer legal effect from an old cross-reference, internal role or delegation listing.

**Final preparation output:** provide supported findings and priority next actions first, allocate employer versus applicant gaps, and retain specific unresolved questions. Use the integrated skill's notices and reporting rules. Do not submit, pay, withdraw, contact third parties or assert official acceptance/approval on the strength of this reference.
