# Worker protection and paying for sponsorship

Use this reference when a worker, applicant, family member or sponsor raises a bill, reimbursement, payroll deduction, cash-back request, offer, gift, unpaid work or threat tied to sponsorship. After the skill’s opening notices, lead with the specific concern and safest next step. If there is immediate danger in Australia, call [Triple Zero (000)](https://www.triplezero.gov.au/); do not ask the worker to confront anyone or gather evidence first. Otherwise, use what is already known and ask only the next material question under the skill’s numbered intake rules.

## 1. Separate sponsor costs from applicant costs

| What the charge actually covers | Preparation finding and next action |
|---|---|
| **Sponsor-side costs:** becoming, being or having been an approved work sponsor; a nomination under s 140GB(1), including the nomination fee and applicable nomination training contribution charge (Skilling Australians Fund or SAF levy); recruitment specifically for that nomination; agent work for these activities. | For an applicable sponsor, keep these costs with the sponsor. Regulation 2.87 prohibits recovering or seeking to recover covered costs, and taking or seeking action to transfer them or have another person pay covered costs to the sponsor. Check the legal entity, route and dates: the duty can continue after sponsorship approval ends, or after the party leaves the work agreement, if a primary or secondary sponsored person remains. It is not limited to bills sent to the worker personally. Paying in instalments, collecting through an intermediary, using payroll or signing a repayment clause does not make a covered cost transferable. |
| **Genuinely separate applicant-side costs:** the person’s own visa application charge (including applicant-side charges for family), required tests or checks such as English, health, police or biometrics, and separately engaged professional work on the person’s own visa application. | These can be applicant expenses if they are what the charge actually covers, do not reimburse covered sponsor costs and are not a price for getting or keeping sponsorship. Verify applicability, who engaged the provider and any separate obligation to pay. The need for a visa does not turn its application charge into a nomination fee, and a sponsor cannot disguise a nomination fee as a visa charge. |
| **Unclear or mixed account:** a package price, a single invoice for both stages, a deposit with no breakdown, or a worker paid the agent or government directly. | Read the engagement terms and itemise each line by client, service, stage, amount, fee or disbursement, invoice issuer, intended payer, actual payer and recipient. Reconcile against government receipts and proof of payment. If safe, obtain separate, accurate billing for sponsor and applicant work; correct the allocation, not merely the label. If already paid, keep the records and seek advice about recovery options; do not promise repayment. If allocation is not supported, mark the affected lines **Needs verification**; do not clear or condemn the entire bill based on its label or a single lawful line. |

Sources: [Migration Regulations 1994, reg 2.87](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc245377449); [Home Affairs — Cost of sponsoring](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/employer-subsite/Pages/cost-of-sponsoring.aspx); [Home Affairs — SID Core Skills stream, Cost](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/skills-in-demand-visa-subclass-482/core-skills-stream). Do not reuse stored fee amounts. For a first-time sponsor applicant with no existing approval or commenced work agreement, check the separate approval criteria (e.g. reg 2.60S) and the event-payment rule below rather than assuming the ongoing sponsor duty has begun. If a work agreement is involved, check its executed terms and s 140H of the Act for any variation of a duty; do not treat a draft agreement or a worker’s consent as a variation. See [sponsor obligations](sponsor-obligations.md) for duty timing and [nomination](nomination.md) for the employer’s fees and levy.

## 2. Check separately whether a benefit is the price of a sponsorship event

- **Who did what?** Section 245AR covers asking for or receiving a benefit from another person in return for the event. Section 245AS covers offering or providing a benefit to another person in return for the event. A demand or offer can matter even if nothing was paid, nothing was accepted and the event never happened. The actor is not necessarily an approved sponsor: a prospective employer, worker or applicant, family member, recruiter, agent or other intermediary may be involved. Section 245AR has criminal and civil penalty routes; s 245AS is a civil penalty provision. Do not attribute liability to anyone by role alone.
- **What was sought or promised, and in exchange for what?** A benefit can be money, a deduction, property, a gift, an advantage or a service. Examples to check include wage kickbacks, unpaid work (including work by a family member), underpayment or inflated charges if tied to the event. Identify the actual or proposed sponsorship step: for example, applying for sponsor approval or to vary it, agreeing to sponsor or maintaining that agreement, making or not withdrawing a nomination, hiring or retaining someone in the visa-linked role, or grant of a prescribed visa. The test is an exchange, not just proximity to lodgement. A lawful salary arrangement, ordinary profit from employment or a genuinely independent loan repayment is not evidence of that exchange by itself. Check what the parties said or did that ties the benefit to the event, whether or not it went ahead.
- **Does the law cover this event?** Check [Migration Act 1958, s 245AQ](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc242887727) and [regs 5.19L–5.19N](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc245377595) for the prescribed class, visa and event, as relevant. The Regulations include SID and TSS under subclass 482; the event definition also covers relevant work-agreement events, and reg 5.19N specifies additional events for labour agreements that are not work agreements. Do not assume every visa or employment arrangement is covered. If the conduct took place partly or wholly overseas, record locations and seek review of [s 245AW](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc242887733) rather than assuming it is outside Australian law.
- **Was it payment of a reasonable amount for actual professional services?** Sections [245AR(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc242887728) and [245AS(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc242887729) except reasonable payment for a professional service provided or to be provided by the ask/receive person (s 245AR), the offer/payment recipient (s 245AS), or a third person. Check the actual provider, agreed scope, work performed or contracted for, fee breakdown and basis for reasonableness. Complexity can justify a higher fee; an invoice, agent title, high price or third-party payment does not settle the test either way. The exception does **not** make covered sponsor costs recoverable under reg 2.87.

**Apply both checks.** An agent’s genuine, reasonably priced nomination work can still be a sponsor cost that may not be passed on. A demand that a worker repay the levy to keep their job or nomination calls for checking both cost shifting and the exchange. A wage deduction or cash-back demand may also raise a separate workplace issue. Describe observed facts and possible concerns; do not declare a breach, guilt, enforceability or entitlement to a remedy on incomplete facts.

## 3. Preserve evidence without increasing risk

1. **Put safety first.** If safe, pause before committing to a disputed line or further payment and get independent help; do not tell someone to confront the requester, resign, stay in the job, stop work or take on new work. Do not use a blanket instruction to stay or leave; separate immediate safety, workplace questions and the specific visa or work-condition question. If there is a threat, coercion, document retention, violence or possible trafficking, use a safe device and contact method. Never require a perfect file before offering help.
2. **Build a dated timeline.** Note who asked, offered, received or paid, who benefited and in what roles; where and when; the terms used and the event the benefit was tied to; any threats, what actually changed, and what did not happen. Mark firsthand facts, recollection and inference separately. Record visa/nomination/employment dates only where material.
3. **Keep originals you can lawfully and safely access.** Preserve messages and emails with dates, job advertisements or offers, contracts, fee agreements, itemised invoices, receipts, bank or transfer records, payslips and rosters. Match amounts to the timeline. Keep secure originals and share the minimum necessary via a safe channel; use redacted copies for preparation. No covert recording or unauthorised access. Do not disclose worker details or evidence to the sponsor, recruiter, nominee or any agency on the worker’s behalf.

## 4. Offer voluntary help and keep visa questions separate

- **Workplace pay, deductions, cash-back or entitlements:** the [Fair Work Ombudsman — Visa holders and migrants](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/find-help-for/visa-holders-migrants) explains workplace rights, how to seek help, interpreter access and anonymous reporting. Asking the Ombudsman for help does not itself cancel a visa. Migrant workers can seek help with workplace entitlements even when they are concerned about work rights or a visa-condition breach; those visa questions remain separate.
- **Suspected sale of sponsorship, sponsor cost recovery or exploitation:** see [Home Affairs — Workers rights and visa reporting protections](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/working-in-australia/work-rights-and-exploitation) for support options and [Australian Border Force — Border Watch](https://www.abf.gov.au/about-us/what-we-do/borderwatch) for voluntary reporting. An anonymous report is an option, not a guarantee that facts cannot identify someone, that information stays in one agency, or that action will be taken. Ask the service how information will be used or shared before providing identifying details. A union, community legal centre or independent adviser can help consider options without first approaching the employer. The skill does not submit reports, contact anyone or send evidence; the worker chooses whether and how to seek help or report.
- **Threats about a visa:** an employer cannot itself cancel a visa. Sponsor action, loss of a job, a report and a visa decision are different events. [Migration Act, s 116(1AC)–(1AD)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc242887394) describes a discretionary cancellation ground for sponsorship-event benefits, not automatic cancellation. Neither reporting nor evidence of pressure establishes visa immunity or civil/criminal immunity. Do not blame a worker who was pressured; preserve that context. Prioritise review of any actual notice/deadline and the holder’s grant, visa conditions, expiry and cessation facts; no generic period or interim work instruction.
- **Conditional protections:** [Home Affairs — Strengthening Reporting Protections Pilot](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/working-in-australia/work-rights-and-exploitation/strengthening-reporting-protections-pilot) explains how to seek protection in relation to certain visa-condition breaches linked to exploitation. Distinguish the factors the decision maker must consider under [reg 2.43A](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc245377360) from the conditional protection under [reg 2.43B](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc245377361). Check the actual visa and condition, certificate and dates or other applicable evidentiary route, link to exploitation, future visa purpose, and required written commitments and any previous failure to comply. These protections do not determine other cancellation grounds or liability for sponsorship payments. A tip-off or receipt is not confirmation of protection. The [Workplace Justice visa](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/employing-and-sponsoring-someone/migrant-worker-protections/help-reporting-protections-workplace-justice-visa) is a separate potential pathway to stay and pursue a workplace matter, with its own timing, certification and criteria. Do not infer eligibility, extended stay or work rights.

## 5. Hand off only what the next stage needs

Keep three distinct questions: applicable sponsor duty and facts; the employer’s nomination fee, levy and truthful written conduct certification; and any applicant-specific visa criterion or existing visa condition. See [sponsor obligations](sponsor-obligations.md), [nomination](nomination.md) and [visa application](visa-application.md). For SID, [reg 2.73(12)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html#_Toc245377419) calls for the nominator’s written certification about asking for or receiving a benefit under s 245AR(1) in relation to that nomination. Offering or providing under s 245AS can raise a separate concern but is not the same certification test. Schedule 2 clauses [482.213](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html#_Toc245286267) and [482.313](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html#_Toc245286285) address the primary and secondary applicant’s own conduct under the specified provisions over the previous three years, with a reasonable-disregard limb. Do not apply a common lookback to every stage, imply automatic refusal or suggest omitting facts from official answers.

Use the dated links as source anchors, not as proof of law for every event date. Check the applicable legislation, amendments and transitions at use; verify the current guidance and official help channel before relying on them. If a material trigger, source or fact cannot be verified, state what is missing, what it blocks and which specific record, provision or independent professional review would resolve it. Keep worker-sensitive records out of shared or reusable material.
