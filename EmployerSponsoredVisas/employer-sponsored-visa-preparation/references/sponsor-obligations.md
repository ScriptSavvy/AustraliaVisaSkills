# Ongoing sponsor obligations

Use this reference for standard and accredited business sponsors, former sponsors where a duty survives, and the employer party to a work agreement. It covers SID and relevant TSS/457 and 494 obligation branches, not temporary activities sponsorship. Use the [494 nomination and visa preparation references](integrated-workflow.md#select-the-pathway-before-applying-any-rule) for application-stage checks. Do not assume a temporary sponsorship duty identifies a 186 nomination test. A worker’s visa conditions, employer duties and workplace law are separate tests. Accreditation does not replace the ordinary duties. See [Standard business sponsorship](standard-business-sponsorship.md), [Agreement](labour-agreements.md), [Worker costs](worker-protection.md), [SID nomination](nomination.md) and [SID visa](visa-application.md).

**Authority and date.** The statutory clocks below follow [Migration Regulations 1994, compilation C291](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03), displayed for **3–11 November 2026 only**. Verify the law, instruments and any transition for the relevant event before finding compliance. Under [Migration Act 1958](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest) s 140H, read the executed agreement, schedules and variations: apply any validly varied prescribed obligation as varied and identify any additional term expressly identified as a sponsorship obligation (other agreement terms can still matter). Do not infer that the agreement replaces every regulation or permits a concession not in the text. [Home Affairs’ obligations page](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/employing-and-sponsoring-someone/existing-sponsors/standard-business-accredited-obligations) is practical guidance. Some of its triggers and endpoints differ from the Regulations; do not combine them. Policy notes describe officer practice, and evidence examples are preparation options, not independent statutory tests.

## Establish which clocks apply

Record sponsor approval, cessation/cancellation and agreement commencement/end; nomination lodgement and approval, relevant grant/visa in effect or last substantive visa, actual work start/cessation, any new sponsor’s nomination **approval**, departures, and the linked status of each primary and secondary sponsored person. Also record the date each change occurred, when any request was made/received or legally deemed received, and evidence of response. Use the r 2.57 definitions: “no primary or secondary sponsored person in relation to the sponsor” is **not** “no sponsored employee on payroll”.

| Duty | Start | End / separate clock |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooperate with inspectors, r 2.78** | Approval day or work agreement commencement. | Five years after sponsor status or agreement ceases. Not calculated from the worker’s start or last day. |
| **Equivalent employment, rr 2.79 / 2.79A** | Nomination approval if the primary already holds a relevant visa; otherwise grant based on that approval. | Earlier of employment cessation or further substantive grant in effect outside the relevant group (457/482 for r 2.79; 494 for r 2.79A). Officers ordinarily assess actual employment from work commencement; that does not move the statutory start. |
| **Departure travel, r 2.80** | For the covered 457/482/494 sponsorships, linked nomination approval if the primary already holds the relevant visa, otherwise grant based on that nomination. | For each primary or secondary, earliest of another approved sponsor’s nomination approval identifying the primary, the applicable further substantive grant in effect, or departure **and** cessation of the relevant visa (and cessation of a qualifying Subclass 020 Bridging B visa if held on departure). The grant test is outside 457/482 if the person’s last substantive visa was 457, otherwise a different subclass. Employment ending is not itself a stop. Check the funded person’s visa **at request** separately. |
| **Commonwealth location/removal costs, r 2.81** | Day each sponsored person becomes unlawful; relevant costs are those incurred from then until their departure. | Five years after that person leaves; liability requires a compliant written request. Not the travel-request clock. |
| **Keep records / provide records, rr 2.82 / 2.83** | Approval day or agreement commencement. | Two years after the first day both sponsor status/agreement has ceased **and** there is no primary or secondary sponsored person in relation to the sponsor. The separate five-year per-record maximum under r 2.82 is not a whole-duty endpoint and does not shorten other recordkeeping laws. |
| **Notify Immigration, r 2.84** | Approval day or agreement commencement. | Ends after the first day the above two conditions occur concurrently, **not** two years later. Each reportable event has its own deadline. |
| **Nominated work, r 2.86** | Linked nomination approval if the primary already holds the relevant visa; otherwise grant based on that nomination. | Earliest of employment cessation, another approved sponsor’s nomination approval identifying the primary, a further substantive grant in effect (outside 457/482 if last substantive visa was 457, otherwise different subclass), or departure plus cessation of the nomination-linked visa and, where specified, the Bridging B held on departure. |
| **No recovery or transfer, r 2.87** | Approval day or agreement commencement. | Ends on the day both sponsor status/agreement has ceased and no primary or secondary sponsored person remains in relation to the sponsor. The public guide instead states a two-year tail; do not apply it as the r 2.87 endpoint. |
| **Non-discriminatory recruitment, r 2.87C** | SBS approval for an SBS lawfully operating in Australia. | Covers recruitment during the approval period and ends when SBS status ceases. Not the same duty as the application declaration. |

## Inspection, records and reporting

**Cooperation (r 2.78).** Cooperate with an inspector appointed under s 140V exercising the specified Migration Act powers, including lawful requests made through an authorised assistant. Do not obstruct, conceal people or documents, inhibit assistance, threaten or intimidate. Prepare a responsible contact, accessible records and a log of lawful requests and responses. Officer policy looks at sponsor representatives with actual or apparent authority, not every worker’s conduct automatically; distinguish an inspector’s statutory production notice from a request under r 2.83, and do not treat missing records as automatic obstruction.

**Keep records (r 2.82).** Keep reproducible records of: written travel requests and date received, how and when paid, amount and people funded; reportable-event notices, including date, method and destination; worker tasks and locations; written employment contracts; and turnover substantiation for nominations attracting the SAF levy. If r 2.79 or r 2.79A applies, also retain wages paid, amounts applied for/on the worker’s direction, non-cash benefits with agreed value and period, and comparable terms and dates for actual equivalent worker(s) where present. Keep agreement-specified records where relevant. Keep legacy training records only for the applicable pre-12 August 2018 SBS approval cohort with Australian operation at approval or variation, as specified in r 2.82(3)(g); do not conflate that exception with current turnover records under (3)(h). Travel-payment, certain wage/disbursement and legacy training records must be independently verifiable under the regulation’s default rule; check for any applicable specification instrument. Bank transfers/reconciliations are useful; policy treats unsupported cash records as hard to verify, not a blanket ban on cash. A missing payroll record and underpayment are separate findings.

**Provide records (r 2.83).** Read the actual written notice for requested material, method, statutory service under Act ss 494B–494C and due date. The provision concerns records required by applicable Commonwealth, state/territory law or r 2.82 and information related to sponsorship administration or the work agreement, within the provision’s conditions. The statutory minimum is seven days after deemed receipt; 14 calendar days is ordinary officer guidance, not the legal minimum. If more time is needed, request it in writing before expiry with reasons; use the original deadline unless an extension is granted. Track each requested item, supply what is available, explain gaps truthfully and retain the submission receipt. A partial response is not proof of compliance.

**Notify Immigration (r 2.84).** Use the electronic methods specified by the applicable instrument. [IMMI 18/040](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00303/latest/details) specifies the ImmiAccount “Notification of sponsorship changes” form or `sponsor.notifications@abf.gov.au`; retain a dated receipt/copy even if an agent notified. An email to another departmental mailbox is not the prescribed method. For SBS/agreement sponsors:

- Employment cessation may be reported in advance; r 2.84(6) item 1 sets 28 days from actual cessation. If a previously notified end date changes or employment continues, notify again: the earlier of 28 days from the date previously notified **as the cessation date** or 28 days from actual cessation (if any). Do not count from the day the first notice was sent.
- Other prescribed changes generally have 28 days from the event: failure to start on the agreed date, change to actual duties, covered address/contact changes, agreement training/contact changes or specified legacy training changes, legal entity ceasing, appointment of a new company director, partnership admission, appointment to the managing committee of an unincorporated association, and payment of r 2.80 travel costs. Home Affairs also asks sponsors to keep names, registrations and structure details updated; identify the actual statutory trigger separately. Sending notice is not approval of a new entity or role.
- Check the precise insolvency/bankruptcy event under r 2.84(3)(i)–(l): individual debt or insolvency agreement, sequestration, relevant debtor’s petition or declaration, composition/scheme; for companies, administrator, voluntary/court winding-up, provisional liquidator, court-approved compromise, controller/receiver, initiated deregistration or appointment of a restructuring practitioner. Check relevant events affecting a partner or managing committee member. Use the appointment, resolution, order or other dated instrument rather than estimating from financial distress alone.

## Equivalent employment and nominated work

**Pay and conditions (rr 2.79, 2.79A).** For SBS nominations made on or after **18 March 2018** under r 2.79 and the r 2.79A (494) SBS branch, test all three separately: annual earnings promised at nomination approval; earnings an equivalent Australian citizen/permanent resident earns or would earn doing equivalent work in the same workplace/location; and non-pay conditions at least as favourable as for equivalent work at the same location. For earlier 457 nominations, compare overall terms and conditions both with the approved terms and with the equivalent Australian worker, using the historical provisions. For agreement sponsors, check the actual agreement’s employment floor and variations. The high-earnings exception is for the SBS branches, not automatically agreement sponsors: compare qualifying annual earnings with the instrument under r 2.79(1A)(b) (also used by r 2.79A(2)); [IMMI 18/033, s 5](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L00284/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) specifies AUD 250,000 in the checked version. Check date and actual earnings; this is not the SID income threshold or a general employment-law exemption.

- Compare real duties, responsibilities, experience, qualifications, location and hours, not title or occupation code alone. If no comparable worker exists, use contemporaneous award, agreement and relevant local market evidence; monitor changes rather than freezing comparator pay at approval. An officer’s short look-back or recent-approval shortcut is not a waiver of a statutory test. Check award/agreement and workplace-law minima independently.
- Reconcile approved contract and nomination to gross payroll, timesheets and independently verifiable payment per pay cycle. Check overtime, extra hours, unpaid hours, deductions, leave and non-pay entitlements. Under r 2.57A, distinguish wages, amounts dealt with for the worker and agreed reasonable benefits genuinely delivered from reimbursements, prescribed superannuation contributions and indeterminate payments (for example unguaranteed overtime, bonuses or commission). Revalue shared benefits on the actual evidence. Policy may accept cash replacing an approved non-cash benefit at equivalent value; do not offset promised cash with new non-cash benefits. Where paid offshore, keep dated AUD conversion and top-up records. Do not use annual averages to hide pay-cycle gaps.
- A reduction in hours or unpaid leave is not an automatic exception. Document the reason, worker’s request/agreement, leave approval, dates, duties, pro-rata rate and applicable award or agreement. Policy may accommodate a documented graduated return, injury/illness or compelling personal circumstances without reducing the approved hourly equivalent or changing duties, subject to actual terms and law. Its usual short unpaid-leave approach (generally no more than three months, subject to workplace entitlements or compelling circumstances) is not statutory permission; do not rely on inconsistent full-time-only agreement language. A business downturn, forced leave or a pay reduction calls for an actual compliance check, not a generic concession.

**Duties and employer (r 2.86).** Compare actual work at each location with the approved occupation, skill level and nomination conditions; keep the applicable occupation instrument and caveats with the approval. Closely related incidental tasks or a promotion still within the same occupation are distinct from regular unrelated work. Notify changes to duties even where no new nomination is needed. For a different occupation, check an approved new nomination and applicable new visa requirements before the change. Temporary agreed higher duties (policy considers short arrangements up to two months, with dates and pay documented) do not override the nominated-occupation or notification rules. Document legitimate leave or delayed start rather than assuming no work or a different job is permitted.

- For non-exempt 457/482 work, r 2.86(2A): Australian SBS at approval or last approved variation may use direct employment or a qualifying associated entity; overseas-only SBS at that time and an agreement party generally must employ directly, subject to applicable agreement terms. Confirm an associated entity under the Corporations Act test, not common branding. A client worksite alone does not establish labour hire. Check who directs, pays, contracts for and employs the worker.
- For the specified 457/TSS cohorts and SID Core/Specialist (r 2.86(2AB)), r 2.86(2AA) also requires a written employment contract and bars SBS recruiting for supply or hiring out to unrelated businesses (to **any** other business for overseas-only SBS), unless the occupation is specified under r 2.72(13). Do not import these clauses wholesale across streams. For 494, rr 2.86(2B), (2BA) separately govern direct/associated employment and the SBS written-contract and on-hire restrictions, with the r 2.72C(14) instrument providing the occupation exception. An occupation exception is not a general contractor, labour-hire or visa-condition permission; check the exact instrument, work agreement, nomination and worker’s conditions.

## Travel, costs and recruitment conduct

**Departure travel (r 2.80).** A valid written request can come from the funded primary or secondary, one on the other’s behalf, or the Minister/delegate on their behalf. It must name each funded person and the intended passport country (specify which for multiple passport holders) and be made while that person holds a listed visa. Pay reasonable necessary one-way costs, ordinarily from the Australian residence to departure and economy/equivalent travel to that country, within 30 days of receipt under the statutory reasonable-cost benchmark. Keep quote, request, proof of payment and r 2.84 notice. Reasonable reimbursement may apply to self-funded travel; personal upgrades, discretionary stops, moving costs or lease penalties are not automatically covered. Check actual sponsored status of family, including any qualifying Australian-born child. Non-start, dismissal or resignation does not by itself remove the duty. Date a valid request separately from subsequent departure/visa cessation. Do not pay twice for costs already met under the regulation; check any fresh sponsorship/nomination after return rather than assuming it can never recur.

**Location/removal (r 2.81).** Distinguish actual statutory Commonwealth costs for each unlawful person from a worker’s travel request. A Minister/delegate notice must use s 494B service and allow at least seven days from s 494C deemed receipt. The sponsor amount is capped at AUD 10,000, with qualifying r 2.80 costs already paid deducted from the lower of actual costs and cap. Keep earlier payment evidence. Do not seek reimbursement of a sponsor obligation amount from the worker or someone paying for them; check Act s 140J(3). Policy describes requesting payment after departure and case-specific discretion; do not assume a demand will issue or infer a worker debt allocation from this reference.

**Protected employer costs (r 2.87).** Do not transfer, make another person pay, recover, or attempt any of these for protected costs connected with becoming, being or having been a sponsor, nomination (including SAF), related professional fees and costs specifically of recruiting for nomination. Screen payroll deductions, repayment clauses, deposits, demands through third parties and agreed recruitment inducements (e.g. employer-promised visa fees or relocation). A worker’s consent does not make a protected charge permissible. Separate genuinely worker-side visa expenses from expenses incurred to recruit that worker, and separately check any benefit demanded for a sponsorship-related event. See [Worker costs](worker-protection.md).

**Recruitment (rr 2.57, 2.87C).** Check for direct and indirect discrimination on citizenship or immigration status, except a practice engaged in to comply with Commonwealth, state or territory law. Keep adverts, accessible channels, job-related selection criteria, shortlist and reasons, and evidence for genuine language/skills needs or internal transfers. Status-based screening or overseas-only advertising may warrant review; workforce patterns and complaints are policy review signals, not statutory quotas or proof. An internal promotion, genuine transfer or re-nomination is not by itself discriminatory. A signed declaration, LMT evidence or LMT exemption does not prove recruitment conduct. Other discrimination grounds are assessed separately under applicable workplace and discrimination law.

**Report what the records support.** For each triggered issue, give the sponsor, rule, event date, response date, evidence and next concrete action. Separate missing proof, clear date/document inconsistencies and policy questions. If an actual notice or status change is urgent, preserve the originals and prioritise its verified deadline and relevant professional review. Do not predict enforcement, penalties, cancellation or visa outcomes.
