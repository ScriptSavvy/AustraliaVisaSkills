# Standard business sponsorship

Use this reference to prepare or review the **employer’s** SBS application, renewal or accreditation request. An SBS approval belongs to the approved legal person; it is not an approved position, a visa or permission to start work. The Core Skills and Specialist Skills streams use the SBS route. For sponsorship under a signed work agreement, use [Agreement](labour-agreements.md), not another SBS application for that agreement. Then check [SID nomination](nomination.md) and [SID visa](visa-application.md) separately. For 494 use the [494 nomination reference](sesr-494-nomination.md) for the separate Employer Sponsored and Labour Agreement authority gates. For 186 use the [186 nomination reference](ens-186-nomination.md); do not impose this SBS application as a universal step.

**Authority and date.** The statutory rules below were compared with the [Migration Act 1958](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest) and [Migration Regulations 1994, compilation C291](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03), displayed for **3–11 November 2026 only**. For other dates, verify the applicable compilation, instruments and any transition before making a compliance finding. [Home Affairs SBS guidance](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/employing-and-sponsoring-someone/sponsoring-workers/becoming-a-sponsor/standard-business-sponsor) is implementation guidance, not a substitute for the text. Policy describes how cases may be handled; the suggested evidence below is not a list of statutory documents.

## Identify the applicant and operating branch

- **Legal person first.** Match the applicant’s legal name, entity type and capacity to the register, decision notice if already sponsored, financial records, employment contract and nomination. A sole trader is the individual; a company is distinct from its owners. Partnerships and unincorporated associations can be treated as persons for sponsorship purposes; incorporated associations, cooperatives, government bodies and not-for-profits may also be candidates depending on their legal structure. A trade name is not a legal person. For a joint venture, identify who actually operates and employs: use the agreement, parties, signatures and any incorporation records rather than relying on the venture name. A sole trader cannot employ themselves; nomination of a partner normally raises the same problem, but special partnership structures require case-specific review of an actual employment relationship.
- **Trustee, not trust.** Use the trustee’s legal name and applicable own identifiers in the applicant fields; separately identify the trust and its ABN. A trustee acting only in that capacity may not have an ABN of its own. Obtain the deed’s parties and execution pages and amendments, especially any trustee change. Trust accounts can evidence the trustee’s operation in that capacity. If a partner acts through a trust, identify the trustee as the partner. The same legal person can operate in different capacities; multiple ABNs alone do not prove multiple legal persons or automatically require multiple approvals.
- **Registration evidence.** Use applicable active ABN, ASIC company extract/ACN or ARBN, state/territory incorporation certificate if relevant and business name extract when trading under a separate name. A registration certificate that does not identify its holder may not resolve a mismatch. For a franchise, identify the actual operating franchisor or franchisee; compare the business name extract and signed agreement’s relevant pages. Where an ABN is not legally required, explain and evidence why instead of inventing one. A wrong identifier for the identifiable applicant calls for clarification and, if already lodged, a documented correction with consent; replacing one legal applicant with another is not a clerical correction. Prepare a new application if the intended person was not identified in the original; first check effects on any linked nominations and visas.
- **Australian versus overseas business.** Under rr 1.03 and 2.59, test where this legal applicant actually operates lawfully, not just where registered. A foreign entity with an ABN or ARBN but no Australian operations is not thereby an Australian operator. If it operates locally, identify whether it is the foreign person through a registered branch or a separate Australian operating entity. For the overseas-only branch, prove lawful establishment and actual operation in the home jurisdiction and, under r 2.59(h), the covered person/cohort (457, TSS or SID holder, or SID applicant/proposed applicant) and one of two purposes: establish/assist an Australian operation with overseas connections on the applicant’s behalf, **or** fulfil/assist performance of a contractual obligation of the applicant. Do not infer an overseas-only 494 route from the general website. Useful evidence includes a funded expansion plan (location, timetable, staffing, capital and activity), signed joint venture (parties, purpose, duration and responsibilities), or contract showing the applicant’s Australian deliverables and the worker’s role. Relevant signed sections and sufficient context may be usable instead of the whole confidential document; do not obscure identity, obligations or dates.

## Approval criteria and useful evidence

| Statutory gate | What to prepare |
|---|---|
| **Prescribed application** (rr 2.59(a), 2.61) | Use the prescribed online form and fee via ImmiAccount. Use the renewal form only if this same person is a current SBS other than an overseas business sponsor at lodgement; use the full form otherwise. [LIN 19/215, ss 6–7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/Latest/F2019L01451) specifies 482SR (renewal), 482S (full) and the AUD 420 fee; confirm the applicable instrument before lodgement. The alternative requires a departmental email authorising the specific case and no statutory bar to electronic lodgement. The Department must identify a problem that prevents online lodgement. It must either be unfixable, or be unlikely to be fixed by the end of the day the authorisation email is sent **and** an affected visa applicant will become unlawful by the next business day. If authorised, reply with the supplied approved form 482S, the authorisation email and the officer’s name and position number by the end of that day (ACT legal time). An outage or approaching expiry alone is not permission to email an application. Retain payment receipt, acknowledgment and TRN. |
| **Lawfully operating** (r 2.59(c)) | Establish both registration/licensing as applicable and real operating activity. A dormant company, bare registration or business plan alone does not establish operation. A new business can qualify without 12 months’ trading if its actual activity is evidenced. Choose a coherent mix: recent financial statements with comparative figures, tax return and BAS, business bank activity, invoices/contracts, payroll, lease/equipment, completed purchase, annual report or accountant confirmation. For older accounts, use intervening complete quarterly BAS where available; for a start-up, use an operational plan alongside executed contracts and business bank records, with premises, staff or early BAS if relevant. Home Affairs suggests different evidence bundles for established, smaller and new businesses; explain missing periods and use credible alternatives. Test actual activity separately from commercial viability or the ability to fund a nominated role. |
| **Local labour and recruitment** (r 2.59(f)) | If lawfully operating in Australia, attest in writing to a strong record of or demonstrated commitment to employing local labour **and** declare in writing that the applicant will not engage in discriminatory recruitment. Officers may ordinarily rely on truthful form declarations; they do not replace nomination labour market testing or ongoing conduct requirements. |
| **Adverse information** (rr 1.13A, 1.13B, 2.59(g)) | The test concerns information known to Immigration about the applicant **or an associated person**, unless reasonable to disregard it. Check relevant contraventions, investigations/proceedings, regulatory warnings or other actions, insolvency and bogus documents or materially false information. Association is not limited to corporate control: consider relevant current or former family, management/employment, advisory, common intermediary and corporate/control links. Check applicable spent-conviction protections. Organise dated findings, response/correction, remediation and evidence explaining why disregard might be reasonable; do not assume it. This is distinct from the corporate “associated entity” employment test. |
| **No cost shifting** (r 2.60S) | Identify actual or attempted transfer, third-party payment or recovery of sponsorship, nomination (including SAF levy), specific recruitment and related agent costs. Document who incurred, paid or was asked to pay each item; check contracts, invoices, transfer records and deductions. A truthful negative declaration can ordinarily suffice absent contrary evidence. The Minister may disregard a criterion under r 2.60S(4) if reasonable; this is not a waiver of ongoing duties or any separate prohibition. See [Worker costs](worker-protection.md) and [Sponsor obligations](sponsor-obligations.md). |

## Existing approval, renewal and structural change

Record separately: prior legal entity, approval/grant and start dates, expiry or cancellation, new application lodged and paid, new decision and start, each nomination’s reference and status, and worker grant/employment dates. Read the actual approvals and ImmiAccount records.

| Scenario | Preparation consequence |
|---|---|
| **Current Australian SBS, renewal before expiry** | Update changed details using the sponsor-change form before renewal; Home Affairs suggests applying about two months before expiry, not a statutory lodgement window. Under r 2.63A(2)–(4), if the person still held non-overseas SBS approval immediately before the day of the new grant, the new approval starts immediately after the earlier approval ceases, but ends five years **after the grant day**, not five years after the deferred start. It does not start if the earlier approval is cancelled before that start. The public SBS page instead says renewal starts on approval rather than expiry; do not use that shorthand to calculate overlap or continuity. |
| **Timely renewal, earlier approval lapses pending decision** | No automatic continuation of SBS status. Where the prior approval was no longer held immediately before grant day, apply the grant-day start rule. Home Affairs describes putting unfinalised nominations on hold until the renewal decision; processing can resume if approved. On refusal, check the nomination’s instructions, any withdrawal consent and any fairness notice; do not assume approval, refusal or withdrawal has already occurred. |
| **Lapsed before lodgement, or first application** | Use the full application. Do not assume a fresh application cures existing nomination links; public guidance warns of refusal of related unfinalised nominations when lodgement was late. Check actual nomination references, status and departmental directions before taking any step. An SBS application can coexist with a nomination in preparation or processing; it is not approval for decision purposes. Separately assess an **already approved** Core/Specialist SID nomination under rr 1.03 and 2.75: the nomination end day is three months after SBS cessation. Check whether the person is then an SBS or an SBS application lodged before cessation is still undecided on that day; also check refusal of such an application, cancellation and any earlier cessation event. A processing hold is not this statutory rule. |
| **Overseas SBS** | Full route even if currently approved. The overseas approval ordinarily begins on grant and ends on the earlier of the start of a later SBS approval or five years after grant. Do not import the Australian renewal sequence. |
| **Change of entity versus change within the same person** | Compare old and new legal name, ACN/ABN, registered capacity, deeds, employer, and effective dates. A new company replacing a sole trader or a new trustee cannot inherit the old approval; plan the new entity’s SBS and fresh nominations for affected workers, and verify worker-specific timing before changes to employment. A director or trade-name update alone is not proof of a new person; check notification duties separately. A change in ownership, group relationship, sponsor operating branch or insolvency also warrants checking the actual approval, employer identity and [Sponsor obligations](sponsor-obligations.md). |

Regulation 2.62 provides for written approval or refusal notice, with reasons on refusal. A sponsorship fee refund under r 2.61(4)–(6) is discretionary where the application arose from a Department mistake; keep the payer’s written request and evidence. Merits review of an SBS refusal is not universal: r 4.02(4A) excludes a refusal where r 2.59(f) was not considered, generally the overseas-only case. Use the actual notice and applicable review rules immediately; do not infer review rights or a deadline from an accreditation outcome.

## Accreditation is a policy status, not another statutory SBS criterion

Check [Home Affairs accredited sponsor guidance](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/employing-and-sponsoring-someone/sponsoring-workers/becoming-a-sponsor/accredited-sponsor) and the actual ImmiAccount route. Accreditation can be sought with SBS approval or renewal, with no separate accreditation fee when requested that way. The public guidance says a sponsor lodging a new SBS while still current need not reapply for accreditation; confirm carry-over and the effective dates in the actual decision rather than assuming it after lapse, restructure or adverse information. Priority and streamlined treatment of certain low-risk nominations do not waive statutory criteria or promise a time. The optional accredited sponsor character reference is a separate [SID visa](visa-application.md) evidence branch, not a character waiver or a substitute for Australian certificates.

| Public category | Evidence screen (not a legal eligibility finding) |
|---|---|
| 1 Government agency; 2 Australian Trusted Trader | Confirm agency status or current Trusted Trader status and at least 75% Australian workers in the Australian workforce. |
| 3 Low volume | At least 85% Australian workforce in Australia, AUD 4 million annual turnover in each of two years, SBS for at least one year, at least one qualifying approved nomination in two years and over 97% approved in the last year. |
| 4 High volume | At least 75% Australian workforce in Australia, same turnover, SBS for at least two years, at least ten qualifying approvals in two years and over 97% approved in those two years. |
| 5 Major investment | At least AUD 50 million invested in Australia that directly generated Australian employment, SBS for at least one year and over 97% approved in the last year. Public guidance seeks at least one qualifying approval in the last **year**; more permissive officer guidance describes **two years**. If the result depends on the difference, confirm with Home Affairs; do not assume eligibility. Evidence investment, jobs and their causal link. |
| 6 STEM start-up | Australian STEM business developing a distinctive product or service, with funding received from an ESVCLP within the two years immediately before the request. Policy expects full fund registration, not conditional registration; gather registration and dated funding documents. An overseas branch opening or mere new ABN is not by itself a start-up. The detailed public list and operational categories include six despite generic references to “five”. |

Across categories, prepare monitoring history, genuine employment templates meeting applicable National Employment Standards, and an enterprise agreement or current salary table with market basis. Retain role, date, subclass and result for every nomination in the period: public categories 3–5 count named SID/TSS/457 cohorts and use approval percentages, while officer criteria use 482 examples and a non-approval rate; supported cohorts, denominators and 494 template coverage are not worded identically. Do not assume interchangeability. For categories 3–5 the ordinary policy screen excludes sole traders and partnerships. A large professional partnership may warrant case-by-case consideration in those categories; an associated entity or a technical restructure may warrant it in categories 3–4. Provide a documented case for special consideration rather than treating any exception or disregard of monitoring as automatic. Failure to gain accreditation is not refusal of an otherwise eligible SBS. Revocation ordinarily ends priority/streamlined benefits, not SBS approval by itself. If suitability is challenged, gather the notice, response deadline and current/alternative category evidence; do not predict action.
