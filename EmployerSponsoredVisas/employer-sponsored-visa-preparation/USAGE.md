## Ask about one stage or connect several
- **Standard business sponsorship:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. We are preparing a first sponsorship application. Help us organise evidence of which legal entity is applying and what it actually does. Focus on sponsorship only.”
- **Labour agreement:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. We have a draft variation and a nomination coming up. Identify what we can rely on now and which agreement schedules or notices are missing.”
- **Ongoing obligations:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Help us organise records for a sponsor change notification and distinguish that deadline from any separate Department request.”
- **Disputed charge:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. I was asked to reimburse the nomination fee. Help me identify the concern, what to keep safely and my options. Do not contact anyone.”
- **482 nomination and visa:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Help me prepare the employer nomination and my visa application together. Keep the two stages and responsible people separate. Ask one question at a time.”
- **Nomination:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Review our redacted nomination evidence for the stated stream and date. Check role, contract, salary evidence and LMT. Tell us which items need verification; do not submit anything.”
- **Visa:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Review the documents I provide for my visa application and their match with my nomination. Do not infer document contents from filenames.”
- **Focused consistency check:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. My inspected contract and nomination show different job titles. Explain whether the duties/code need checking before calling it an error. Do not start full intake.”
- **Department request:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Help me organise evidence for this redacted Department request. Start with the actual deadline uncertainty and requested evidence, not a general checklist. Do not contact the Department.”
- **Existing holder/change:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Organise the records needed to check a proposed employer or occupation change. Do not treat a lodged nomination as permission to work.”

- **482 → 186 TRT:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Help me organise my 482 grants, bridging periods and employment with two employers for a proposed 186 TRT application. Check visa holding and employment separately; don’t presume every period counts.”
- **494 Employer Sponsored:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Compare our nominee’s contract worksite with the nomination and regional certifying body’s advice. The headquarters and actual site differ. Keep nomination evidence separate from the worker’s visa criteria.”
- **187 transitional TRT:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Help me work out which historical grant or application evidence establishes the 187 transitional cohort. Do not reuse 186 TRT rules.”
- **494 → 191 evidence handoff:** “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. List the 494 grant, location and tax-assessment records I should prepare, without assessing the full 191 application.”

## Save time with a résumé
If several work-history details are needed, you can share a redacted résumé (CV) or type a short summary instead. Leave out contact details, home address, date of birth, identity numbers and referees' details. Keep relevant job titles, dates, duties and qualifications. Your agent provider may process files you upload.

Example: “Use employer-sponsored-visa-preparation. Start my work history from this redacted résumé. Ask one question at a time about anything important that’s missing or inconsistent. Tell me what still needs supporting evidence.” The résumé helps organise your history; it does not replace required evidence.

## Stage-specific results and limits
Sponsorship, labour agreement, nomination, visa, sponsor obligations and shared consistency results remain separate, with the responsible employer/sponsor or applicant identified. An unrequested stage is **Not assessed — not requested**, not a favourable readiness finding. The same issue should not be repeated in multiple tables.

The [verification reference](references/verified-updates.md) contains targeted source checks and unresolved matters. Changing rules must be refreshed for your dates. If browsing or OCR is unavailable, the agent should continue independent checks and identify what cannot be verified. This skill prepares employer requests, not a regional head agreement. It does not cover full 191, 491, closed 187 Direct Entry or complete historical TSS/457 application assessment. Refusal, review deadlines, complex legal interpretation and work permission require a focused qualified review, not a disclaimer alone.

The skill cannot certify family expiry dates in unresolved special-family, age-23, TSS or passport-class branches. It can organise the relevant records and the question for qualified review. It does not determine individual interim work permission or assume nomination approval resets condition-day counts. Never invent contracts, references, advertising, pay records or explanations; protect employer financial records as well as applicant records.
