## Start with your task

- **First use:** “Use `parent-visa-preparation`. Help me organise a 103 preparation checklist. I haven't applied.”
- **Switch or stage:** “I have a 173 grant. Prepare a 143 next-step checklist and show what you need to check about my grant and dates.”
- **Sponsor or both stages:** “Check my preparation for 870 sponsorship and the related parent visa. Keep the two stages and any blockers separate.”
- **Evidence review:** “Compare these redacted certificates for names and parent-child links. Tell me what can and cannot be confirmed.”
- **Department request:** “Help me turn the attached redacted request into a dated action list. Flag wording or receipt dates that could change the deadline.”

In new tasks the agent should show both opening notices. When it needs information, it explains adaptive intake once and normally asks one numbered fact or document request per message. A total appears only for a fixed, fully known set. You can skip a question or say you're unsure; it stays unresolved. Tell the agent if you prefer grouped questions. For a narrow question, expect a direct answer instead of a full intake or report.

## Share less, check more

Start with a redacted list, verified dates or individual relevant pages. Check how your agent handles documents before sharing information. Do not share account credentials, full tax identifiers, genetic test files, private child records or complete files when snippets will do. A missing readable page is different from a confirmed mismatch. The agent can help locate a gap; it cannot authenticate records, submit a case, guarantee a result or infer that you may stay or travel from a checklist. Ask for the governing source and applicable date before relying on a requirement. If official sources or files cannot be read, continue supported preparation but treat affected checks as **Needs verification**.

**Ready for the stated preparation check** only means the specified check passed. **Missing** means a relevant item is absent. **Needs correction** means an observed defect. **Needs verification** means an unanswered rule, fact or reading question. **Not applicable** requires evidence of non-applicability.

For a non-newborn family member you want to add to a pending 173 or 884 application, ask for a checklist of the facts and written confirmation needed. The skill can help you prepare that request; it does not assume Form 1436 establishes a legal route. Ask separately about newborn notification and application rules.
