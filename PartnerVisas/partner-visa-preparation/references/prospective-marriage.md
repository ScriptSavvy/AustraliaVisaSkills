# Prospective marriage and transitions to Partner visas

Use this reference for a proposed Subclass 300 application, an undecided 300 application, or a current or former 300 holder. These are different legal positions. General preparation support is not legal advice, an application-validity decision or a promise of approval. Read [verification](verification.md) for dated coverage and refresh the rule relevant to the actual application, marriage, entry and decision dates.

Read this with [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md), [relationship and sponsorship](relationship-and-sponsorship.md), [children and checks](children-and-checks.md), [application procedures](application-procedures.md), [document handling](document-handling.md) and [terms](terms.md). Changed relationships also require the relevant substantive branch in [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md).

## Ordinary prospective-marriage preparation

Subclass 300 is a prospective-marriage pathway, not an already-married Partner application or a guarantee of later permanent residence. The ordinary plan is to enter Australia, marry the specified prospective spouse after first entry and within the visa period, and live together as spouses. After marriage, a genuine continuing relationship may support a separate combined 820/801 application in Australia. It is not an automatic conversion of the granted 300 visa. After first entering Australia, the couple may marry overseas, but travel, return permission, the visa period and the later onshore application requirements remain separate checks.

Prepare evidence against these distinct questions:

- **Age:** the primary applicant and prospective spouse must each have turned 18 at application. A later birthday does not establish that an application-time requirement was met.
- **Qualifying prospective spouse and sponsorship:** identify the proposed spouse's Australian citizenship, permanent residence or eligible New Zealand citizenship and the actual sponsorship. Being a New Zealand citizen alone does not establish the defined eligible status. Sponsorship restrictions and approval are separate from relationship evidence.
- **Personal meeting:** the couple must have met in physical presence after both turned 18. Internet, telephone and written contact help show development but do not replace this meeting. Use actual travel, meeting and dated photographs or other available records, without imposing a photograph quota.
- **Personal knowledge:** show how the couple know one another and their developing relationship. There is no universal knowledge test or required level of detail. Cultural context may explain how the relationship developed; it does not remove the personal-meeting or genuine-intention requirements.
- **Genuine marriage intention:** the proposed marriage must be intended to be valid for Australian migration purposes and intended to occur within the visa period. Prepare genuine arrangements, not invented reservations or a fabricated exact date.
- **Intention to share married life:** organise the couple's actual understanding of living arrangements, mutual commitment, communication, personal circumstances and future plans. The ordinary spouse evidence factors can help explain intentions, but they are guidance here, not a requirement to prove that an unmarried couple already satisfies every married-relationship factor.
- **Decision-time continuation and freedom to marry:** the named intentions and relationship requirements must continue at decision, and there must then be no impediment to the marriage under Australian law. A NOIM receipt is not proof of freedom to marry. Obtain relevant final divorce, death or other status records where needed; a pending divorce or foreign document needs its actual legal effect checked.

Arranged marriage is not automatically a non-genuine marriage. Real, informed and voluntary consent matters. If either person indicates reluctance, coercion or family pressure, prioritise confidential, safe handling. Do not seek confirmation from an unsafe partner or relatives, expose a confidential disclosure in a shared summary, or coach answers to conceal the issue. Existing evidence and a safe chronology can be organised without collecting a trauma narrative. Marriage recognition, unusual divorce orders, prohibited relationships, proxy or customary marriages and disputed consent need the exact legal question reviewed rather than an assumed universal rule.

There is no standalone death or family-violence primary grant exception in the named ordinary 300 criteria. A pending applicant is not yet a 300 holder for the holder-based 820 exceptions. This does not justify declaring every other pathway unavailable. Establish the actual stage, event and status before considering an alternative.

## Celebrant letter, NOIM and uncertain wedding dates

For an Australian ceremony, the usual preparation evidence is a signed, dated letter on the authorised celebrant's letterhead, identifying the venue and proposed date or date range and, in most cases, confirming that a Notice of Intended Marriage (NOIM) has been lodged. Verify the celebrant's authorised status through the Attorney-General's official marriage resources where that check is material. Do not declare a document genuine merely because its format looks right.

Keep the following distinctions:

1. The visa criterion is genuine intention to marry within the visa period. It does not prescribe a universal exact wedding date or a single compulsory document type.
2. A celebrant's confirmation of a lodged NOIM will ordinarily be the useful evidence; do not routinely demand the NOIM itself as well. Where the letter cannot reasonably be obtained, preserve strong alternative evidence, such as a completed signed NOIM, and the truthful reason for the missing letter. Acceptance of an alternative is for the Department, not this agent to waive.
3. A celebrant may decline to accept the NOIM before interviewing the couple. A letter explaining that position can help explain the evidence gap. It is not a fictitious confirmation that the NOIM was lodged.
4. Lengthy and uncertain visa processing can make a distant fixed booking unreasonable. Explain genuine plans and the need to align a date or range with the eventual visa period. Do not require a costly non-refundable booking simply to fill a checklist.
5. For a marriage in Australia, the stored ordinary notice framework is at least one month's notice and no more than 18 months before the ceremony. A NOIM is ordinarily valid for up to 18 months. Confirm current celebrant instructions and any proposed exception before relying on these periods; the visa process does not extend NOIM validity.
6. The parties need not sign at the same time or place. The appropriate witness depends on the signing location. Stored overseas categories include an Australian diplomatic or consular officer, a notary public, and specified authorised Commonwealth or Austrade employees. Australian categories include an authorised celebrant, Commissioner for Declarations, justice of the peace, barrister, solicitor, medical practitioner, or federal, State or Territory police officer. Confirm the current prescribed form and witness's actual authority; do not treat every professional or overseas solicitor as automatically eligible.
7. Lodging or witnessing a NOIM does not establish that a previous divorce is effective or that there is no impediment to marriage. Where an applicant cannot obtain a home-country no-impediment certificate, retain obtaining attempts and the reason; stored preparation practice includes a Commonwealth statutory declaration explaining the inability. Do not call that declaration an automatic replacement establishing legal capacity.

Reconcile proposed dates across the application, celebrant letter, NOIM and travel plans. An old proposed date is not automatically deception; record the change truthfully. The intended ceremony still has to fit the applicable visa period and first-entry sequence.

## Application location, grant location and visa conditions

An ordinary 300 application must be made outside Australia, including the relevant secondary applicants, under Schedule 1 item 1215. The current grant rule in 300.412 allows grant in or outside Australia, but not in immigration clearance. The grant-location change commenced on **1 July 2024** and covers applications made before commencement but not finally determined, and applications made on or after commencement. It does not authorise ordinary onshore 300 lodgement. Forms, authorised alternative channels, charges and other validity requirements must be checked separately before any proposed action.

Do not transfer historical offshore-grant directions to a current case. The earlier concession and its dates are explained in [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md). Permission to grant a visa in a location is not permission to enter Australia, remain lawfully, depart on a bridging visa or disregard a first-entry date.

Read the actual grant notice and visa record, separating:

- **Visa duration under 300.511:** a Minister-specified date at least nine and no more than 15 months after grant; where no date is specified, the default is nine months. The 9–15-month amendment applies to grants on or after **27 February 2021**, regardless of application date.
- **First entry under 300.611:** entry must occur before the specified first-entry date. Stored policy generally sets no more than 12 months from grant, including a 15-month visa, but that is not authority to replace the actual date in the notice.
- **Primary conditions:** 8515 prohibits marriage or entry into a de facto relationship before entering Australia; 8519 requires the marriage for which the visa was granted within the visa period. Condition 8502 may also be imposed and prevents entry before the person specified in the visa.
- **Secondary conditions:** 8520 is mandatory for the secondary 300 visa; conditions 8502 and 8515 may also be imposed. Inspect the child's actual notice and the applicable wording with [children and checks](children-and-checks.md), rather than assuming the primary's conditions are identical.

A condition breach, an actual cancellation and visa expiry are different events. A breach does not automatically mean cessation. Conversely, do not promise that a breach or cancellation cannot affect an 820 application, lawful stay or decision-time continuation. See the explicit holding and cancellation gate below.

### Nine-month evidence direction versus 9–15-month stay

Official 300 public guidance has two different preparation directions: the overview describes a **9–15-month** stay and the period in the grant letter, while the marriage-arrangements evidence direction asks for evidence of marriage **within nine months of grant**. Do not silently reconcile those statements into a universal nine-month visa or marriage deadline, or disregard the narrower evidence request. Record the actual grant date, specified expiry, first-entry date, conditions and current request. Use the applicable legal provisions and grant notice for legal chronology; seek current Department clarification of a conflicting evidence expectation. Until resolved, the affected preparation timing is **Needs verification**, not automatically met or failed.

### Historical extension cohorts

These fixed extensions are historical, not a present extension offer:

| Provision | All cohort conditions | Fixed consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 300.512 | The 300 visa was in effect at some time from **6 October 2020 through 10 December 2020 inclusive**, and the holder was **outside Australia on 10 December 2020**. | Despite the ordinary duration, travel, enter and remain until **31 March 2022**. The inserting amendment commenced retrospectively on 6 October 2020. |
| 300.513 | The 300 visa was in effect on **15 September 2021**, and the holder was **outside Australia that day**. | Despite 300.511 and 300.512, travel, enter and remain until **31 December 2022**. The inserting amendment commenced retrospectively on 15 September 2021. |

The fixed end dates are past. They may matter when reconstructing historical holding, entry, marriage or later application facts. Do not infer restoration, a current extension, extra time to marry now, or an exemption from a condition. There is no ordinary provision for a further 300 application in Australia simply to delay marriage beyond the visa period.

## Marriage while the 300 application is undecided

### Ordinary notice-based deemed applications

Regulation 2.08E concerns a person who applied for Class TO, subsequently married the person specified as prospective spouse **after application and before the Minister's decision**, and whose marriage is recognised as valid for the Act. It is not the route for a person already married when making the 300 application, a marriage after 300 grant, or a different visa applicant who happens to marry.

Where its predicates apply, the person is also taken to have validly applied for Class BC and Class UF, corresponding to **100 and 309**, on the day **Immigration receives notice of the marriage**. This is not 820/801 deeming merely because the person is in Australia. The regulation's deemed-validity mechanism is distinct from the ordinary outside-Australia lodgement rule; do not require a second ordinary offshore lodgement as an automatic consequence of an onshore marriage. Individual validity still requires establishing that the actual statutory predicates apply.

Keep four records separate: original 300 application date, marriage date, legal notice-receipt date and later administrative entry or recognition date. A later officer becoming aware of a received notice does not automatically move the deemed application date. That date may affect the permanent-stage clock and other application-time rules. A sent message without evidence of receipt leaves a material fact unresolved.

The official 300 **If you marry** directions request a registered marriage certificate, a request for 309/100 consideration, a statement withdrawing the 300 application, an ImmiAccount change update and notification through the Partner processing enquiry channel. Preserve these as preparation directions, including the requested statement, but **do not withdraw the application** or present signing withdrawal as a statutory deeming prerequisite. Regulation 2.08E says the Partner applications are “also” taken made; it does not automatically withdraw the 300 application. The ordinary future-marriage 300 criteria cannot simply remain satisfied after the marriage. A withdrawal or refusal is a distinct consequential action requiring proper authority and advice on its effects. Establish marriage validity and notice facts before treating a Partner application as already made.

Regulation 2.08E(3) treats the TO first-instalment payment as payment of the BC first instalment. Stored preparation treatment does not require a difference solely because the BC charge subsequently increased. This is not a universal refund entitlement, proof that every later child is paid for, or a promise that every unusual charge question is settled.

The marrying primary applicant's deeming does **not** automatically transfer all 300 secondary applicants. Prepare each child's mechanism separately under 2.08A, 2.08B or an independently applicable newborn rule, with the relevant relationship, whereabouts, written request and charge-if-any requirements. Use [children and checks](children-and-checks.md); do not assume the child's original 300 payment establishes Partner addition.

### Marriage during merits review

The current review mechanism is **regulation 4.15(4)**. From **14 October 2024**, the former review provisions 2.08E(2A) and (2B) were repealed and the mechanism relocated. This is a substantive cross-reference correction, not merely replacing “AAT” with “ART”.

Current 4.15(4) addresses the specified TO review circumstances, including marriage to the specified prospective spouse after the Minister's decision but before final determination, notification to the ART, recognised marriage validity, and the **ART deciding to remit** for reconsideration. Its permitted order takes the application also to be BC/UF applications on the **remittal day**. Do not use marriage day, Department notice day or later system entry as an interchangeable date. Do not tell the applicant this text obliges the ART to remit every such case or deems the Partner grant criteria satisfied.

Preserve pre-14 October 2024 AAT and former 2.08E provisions for genuinely historical cases; their application and transition need the original refusal, notification, review, marriage and remittal records. Do not offer judicial-review-only marriage conversion. Court orders or reopening histories need their actual terms and applicable law reviewed. This skill does not determine review access, deadlines, jurisdiction, lawful status, restoration or bridging consequences.

## Current and former 300 holders seeking 820/801

Use the full branches in [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md), not a single “marry and convert” checklist:

- A **current holder with a valid ongoing marriage to the original 300 sponsor** may be considered under 820.211(6), with sponsorship and continued spouse requirements. That clause does not expressly add an entry-on-300 or conditions-compliance limb.
- A **current or former holder using the ordinary spouse or de facto route** is assessed under 820.211(2), including its own sponsor and status conditions. Its sponsoring partner may be the original or a subsequent qualifying partner. Do not assume the marriage requirement in (6) excludes every de facto case.
- A **former holder using 820.211(5)** must have no substantive visa at application, have last entered as a 300 holder, have married the person they entered to marry, and have ceased holding a substantive visa after marriage, with the continuing spouse and sponsorship predicates. A de facto relationship alone does not meet this limb.
- A **current holder whose original sponsor died before application** has the separate 820.211(7) branch. Current text allows the counterfactual continued spouse or prospective-spouse relationship without the former marriage or Australian-close-ties prerequisites.
- A **current holder whose relationship ceased before application** has the 820.211(8) family-violence or specified child-rights alternative. A **former holder under (9)** must have **no substantive visa**, not merely no 300, and the other (9) predicates. Neither branch retains its former prior-marriage prerequisite; (9) does not have (5)'s last-entry-as-300 limb.
- A **former holder with death alone** cannot simply be treated as a current holder under (7). A former holder with another substantive visa does not meet (9)'s status wording, but that is not a conclusion that every Partner route fails. Never advise expiry, departure or withdrawal to fit a branch.

**Unresolved sponsorship gate:** for an unmarried exceptional (7), (8) or (9) route, 820.221A still contains marriage-based current/former-300 exception wording and a general sponsorship-approval requirement. Its interaction with the reformed unmarried routes is not resolved here. Do not invent an exemption, demand unsafe perpetrator cooperation, or declare the reform unusable. Preserve relationship, status and event evidence; the precise remaining question is how 820.221A applies to the selected unmarried branch under the applicable text and current official practice.

**Unresolved holding and cancellation gate:** application-time status under 820.211 and decision-time continuation under 820.221 are separate. A hidden condition-compliance limb must not be inserted into (6), but a post-application cancellation must not be promised to have no effect. Obtain actual grants, conditions, entry, marriage, expiry/cancellation and application records for qualified status and continuation assessment. This gate applies in the affected branch, not only in a general disclaimer.
