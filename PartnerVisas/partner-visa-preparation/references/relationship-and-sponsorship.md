# Relationship and sponsorship

## How to use this reference

Use this shared evidence core with [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md), [prospective marriage](prospective-marriage.md), [children and checks](children-and-checks.md) and [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md). This file does not choose an application route, decide eligibility or determine character. It supports truthful chronology, evidence organisation and identification of questions requiring an applicable official instruction or qualified assessment.

**Knowledge versus verified law:** “Stored preparation guidance” below preserves practical policy knowledge; it is not legislation or proof that an internal practice remains current. “Inspected legal baseline” identifies particular provisions compared with official text in the bounded review of 17 November 2026. The Migration Regulations compilation inspected was compiled on 3 November 2026 and displayed for 3–11 November. Targeted examination of the 12 and 13 November amendments found no change to the relationship and sponsorship provisions discussed here; it was not a complete interacting-law audit. The Migration Act text inspected was compiled on 8 October 2026. Use [verification](verification.md) for the version applicable to the person's application, events and decision, including historical cases. Do not turn the review date into a blanket current-law claim.

Examples are possible evidence, not document quotas, compulsory financial arrangements or guarantees of acceptance. A missing item may require an explanation or alternative evidence, not an invented fact. Unknown applicability remains **Needs verification**. Keep document presence, readability, completeness, consistency, currency and legal compliance separate; an apparently complete file does not establish authenticity or official acceptance. Use [document handling](document-handling.md) for sensitive records and translations.

## Identify the relationship basis

**Inspected legal baseline:** Section 5CB of the [Migration Act 1958](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/2026-10-08/2026-10-08/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) recognises de facto partners of the same or different sex. The couple must not be in a married relationship with each other for section 5F purposes, and must have:

- mutual commitment to a shared life to the exclusion of all others;
- a genuine and continuing relationship;
- living arrangements satisfying the alternatives discussed below; and
- no prohibited family relationship.

The family restriction covers a child, including an adopted child, another descendant, and people with a parent in common, including adoptive relationships. For this purpose, an adoption being declared void or ceasing to have effect is disregarded. Specialist adoption or parentage questions need verification, not a family-tree guess.

Married and de facto relationships receive recognition under different definitions. A marriage certificate alone does not establish a genuine, continuing relationship. Verify marriage recognition and the applicable spouse criteria rather than treating every overseas ceremony as sufficient. Migration recognition does not automatically establish recognition under another law or for another benefit.

**Stored preparation guidance:** A person still legally married to someone else is not automatically excluded from a de facto relationship. Establish that the former relationship has actually ended and the current relationship is exclusive. Preserve divorce or annulment records where available, or the genuine obstacles and reasonable attempts to obtain them, with other evidence of the relationship ending. Conversely, a divorce order alone does not disprove an ongoing relationship with the former partner. Ordinary co-parenting communication is not, by itself, evidence of a continuing romantic relationship. Do not demand unsafe contact with a former partner to prove separation.

## Build a truthful relationship timeline

Distinguish first meeting, dating, commitment to a shared life, actual living arrangements, separations, reunions, marriage and registration. A dating anniversary is not automatically the start of a de facto relationship. Identify the factual basis for the claimed commencement date rather than backdating it to satisfy a duration rule.

Record the original combined or other legally relevant application date from actual records. Keep it separate from payment, upload, temporary grant and permanent-stage evidence dates. An earlier grant does not settle whether the relationship continues at a later assessment; update the timeline and evidence through the review date rather than simply duplicating the initial bundle.

**Stored preparation guidance:** Where a couple ended a relationship and later reunited, earlier intermittent periods do not simply add up to the required continuous period immediately before application. Distinguish a genuine temporary separation from an ended relationship followed by a new one. A recent relationship with another partner may affect exclusivity and the claimed start date; it is also a separate question from the additional duration criterion. Explain real inconsistencies without scripting an idealised relationship history.

## Living together, time apart and never-cohabited cases

**Inspected legal baseline:** Section 5CB(2)(c) contains alternatives: living together **or** not living separately and apart on a permanent basis. Do not impose uninterrupted co-residence as a universal legal test, or treat different addresses at application or decision as an automatic failure.

For time apart, organise the dates, reasons, actual contact, emotional and practical support, shared commitments, reunions and genuine plans to live together. Work, travel, immigration barriers and other explanations need facts, not assumptions. Living together is relevant evidence of the relationship as a whole, even where physical separation does not settle the particular living-arrangements limb. Do not invent a fixed deadline to start living together.

**Material interpretation limit:** Stored guidance contains inconsistent statements about couples who have never lived together. The official judgment needed to resolve the detailed judicial interpretation was not available for substantive inspection. Do not use never having cohabited as a categorical statutory veto, and do not reverse that correction into automatic satisfaction. Future intention alone does not prove an already established de facto relationship. Truthful evidence preparation can continue; a consequential conclusion for a never-cohabited couple needs provision-specific qualified assessment of the living alternatives, genuineness, commitment and applicable dates. See [verification](verification.md).

## Consider all four evidence areas together

**Inspected legal baseline:** Regulation 1.09A requires consideration of all circumstances and each of the four areas for de facto assessment in subclasses 309, 100, 820 and 801. They are matters to consider collectively, not four independent pass marks or a points score. Their examples are non-exhaustive; relevant evidence outside the list can matter, and weight depends on the particular relationship. Do not conclude that one missing document category automatically defeats the relationship.

For other visa classes, consideration of these prescribed factors is not the same statutory mandate. Stored policy recommends their use for thorough and consistent assessment, but this does not make a subclass 300 prospective spouse satisfy the ordinary Partner de facto test. Use the distinct [prospective marriage reference](prospective-marriage.md).

**Stored preparation guidance:** The information considered can include submitted records, supporting witnesses, interview responses and relevant third-party adverse information lawfully available in the case. Do not treat an allegation as an established fact or try to obtain private records without authority. Preserve the actual concern and evidence needed for a truthful response, with safe handling and the applicable comment process.

### Financial aspects

Consider joint assets or major purchases, loans and liabilities, pooling of resources, legally binding obligations to one another, and the way day-to-day expenses are shared. Relevant records can include property or car loan agreements, a guarantee, an existing power of attorney and evidence of rent, utilities or other shared expenditure.

For a joint account, its actual operation over time can be more informative than its mere opening. A recently opened or nearly unused account is not equivalent evidence to a sustained pattern of shared costs. Couples may keep separate accounts: show the genuine arrangement and relevant transactions rather than opening an artificial account for appearances. Separate responsibility for expenses should be described honestly, not concealed. No joint account, property purchase, loan guarantee or power of attorney is universally required.

### Nature of the household

Consider actual living arrangements, responsibility for housework and any joint care and support of children. Possible records include a lease, rental receipts, utilities and correspondence addressed to either or both partners at the same address. Explain genuine arrangements where only one name appears, the couple lives with others, or conventional joint records are unavailable. Do not require a child or a particular domestic division of labour.

### Social aspects

Consider how the couple represents the relationship, the knowledge of friends and family, and shared activities or future plans. Declarations to government or commercial bodies, joint invitations, memberships, travel, photographs and statements from people with direct knowledge may help. Do not impose a photograph, social-media or public-disclosure quota. Laws or traditions that discourage openly acknowledging the relationship can explain limited public evidence; do not expose someone to harm to create it.

Witnesses should describe what they actually know, not repeat an applicant's script. Current public 100 and 801 preparation guidance asks for two statements from people aged at least 18 who know both partners and their relationship; Form 888 can be used. The inspected Form 888 is a supporting statement, not a universal requirement for a JP-witnessed statutory declaration. Do not universally require Australian citizenship or permanent residence; supply applicable identity/status evidence as instructed. Initial validity declarations, sponsor Commonwealth statutory declarations and family-violence declarations are separate roles. Use the stage instructions and [application procedures](application-procedures.md) before specifying execution requirements.

### Nature of the commitment

Consider duration, time lived together, companionship, emotional support and whether both people envisage a long-term shared life. Evidence may show knowledge of one another's background and family circumstances, combined personal affairs, continued support during separation, and provision for one another through a will or superannuation nomination. These are examples, not instructions to make a will or financial nomination.

Relationship length is one component of commitment, which is itself one part of the overall assessment. A long relationship does not automatically establish genuineness; a shorter one is not automatically unreal. Keep this evidentiary assessment separate from the additional application-date duration rule.

## Additional age and duration criteria

**Inspected legal baseline:** Regulation 2.03A of the [Migration Regulations 1994](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/2026-11-03/2026-11-03/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html) is additional to the de facto definition. A relationship can meet that definition yet fail an additional visa criterion. Do not label a relationship unreal merely because it is less than 12 months old.

- **Age:** For a visa application relying on a de facto claim, both parties must be at least 18 at application. This applies to primary and secondary de facto claims. A later birthday does not cure the historical age requirement. Duration exceptions do not remove it.
- **Duration:** Subject to the exceptions below, the existing de facto relationship must span at least the 12 months ending immediately before the application date for the listed classes. This is not universally a requirement for 12 months of co-residence. Processing time after application is not a substitute.
- **Class coverage:** The list includes any permanent visa, Business Skills (Provisional) Class EB, Skilled Employer Sponsored Regional (Provisional) Class PE, Student (Temporary) Class TU, Partner (Provisional) Class UF, Partner (Temporary) Class UK and a General Skilled Migration visa as legally defined. This list does not assert that every listed class is currently open for new applications. Class TO/subclass 300 is not separately listed; do not transfer the ordinary de facto duration rule to its primary intended-marriage basis. For classes outside the duration list, this provision does not prescribe a minimum relationship period, but the actual relationship definition and any other applicable criteria still matter.
- **Stage:** Permanent classes are not omitted, but ordinary 309/100 and 820/801 applications are combined. Do not invent a fresh legal application date or another 12-month wait at routine permanent-stage evidence submission. Other deemed, separate or exceptional applications require their own records and applicable date; use [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md).

Stored policy assesses these additional criteria at decision while testing the specified application-date facts. It also discusses marriage to the same partner before decision. Treat any change to spouse basis conditionally on a marriage recognised under section 5F and the actual visa's application and decision criteria; do not promise that marrying automatically cures a deficient application. See [verification](verification.md) and [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md).

## Duration exceptions and their evidence

The exceptions below concern regulation 2.03A(3), not automatic visa grant, sponsorship approval, the age requirement or satisfaction of section 5CB.

| Exception | Preserve the condition and prepare evidence |
|---|---|
| Compelling **and** compassionate circumstances, paragraph 2.03A(3)(b) | Explain why the particular circumstances meet both descriptions, with supporting records. Stored policy treats this as a high threshold; genuineness alone is not enough. A dependent child of the relationship or illegality of de facto relationships where a partner resides are non-exhaustive policy examples, not automatic exemptions. Pregnancy alone is not treated as sufficient in that guidance, but exceptional or unique pregnancy-related circumstances must not be ruled out without considering the facts. |
| Current or former permanent humanitarian visa holder, paragraph 2.03A(4)(a) | The partner is or was such a holder; the de facto relationship with this applicant existed **before** that grant, and the partner informed Immigration of it **before** grant. Preserve the grant and pre-grant disclosure records, not merely a later assertion. |
| Partner applying for a permanent humanitarian visa, paragraph 2.03A(4)(b) | Establish that exact application and the de facto relationship. Do not substitute the compelling-and-compassionate paragraph for this separate limb. Confirm the regulation 1.03 meaning of permanent humanitarian visa rather than relying on the word “refugee” or assuming temporary protection qualifies. |
| Qualifying registered relationship, subregulation 2.03A(5) | Match completed registration to the prescribed law **and kind of relationship** below, with its date and certificate. A registration application receipt is not completed registration. |

The humanitarian duration limbs are different from the subclass 100 humanitarian-sponsor waiting-period exception in clause 100.221(6). Do not transfer that waiting exception to subclass 801 or use either mechanism to decide the other. Stage-specific treatment belongs in [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md).

## Qualifying registered relationships

**Inspected legal baseline:** The federal reference is section 2E of the Acts Interpretation Act 1901 and section 5 of the [Acts Interpretation (Registered Relationships) Regulations 2019](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00280/asmade/2019-03-12/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html). That instrument commenced on 13 March 2019 and repealed the 2008 instrument. Select historical law for earlier events rather than applying the 2019 table retrospectively.

| Jurisdiction and prescribed law | Prescribed kind of relationship |
|---|---|
| New South Wales — Relationships Register Act 2010 | A relationship registrable in accordance with Part 2. |
| Victoria — Relationships Act 2008 | A registrable domestic relationship registrable in accordance with Part 2.2. |
| Queensland — Civil Partnerships Act 2011 | A relationship entered into in accordance with sections 4 and 5. |
| South Australia — Relationships Register Act 2016 | A relationship registrable in accordance with Part 2. |
| Tasmania — Relationships Act 2003 | A significant relationship for which a deed of relationship may be registered in accordance with Part 2. |
| Australian Capital Territory — Domestic Relationships Act 1994 | A civil partnership entered into in accordance with sections 37C and 37D. |
| Australian Capital Territory — Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act 1997 | A civil union that must be registered under section 32A. |

This is the inspected federal law/type table, not an audit of present local registry procedures or a guarantee about a particular certificate. It contains no Western Australia or Northern Territory entry; that is not a claim that those jurisdictions can never record any relationship. Nor does any foreign or Australian certificate qualify merely because it says “registered”.

**Stored preparation guidance:** Registration completed after visa application but before decision can engage subregulation 2.03A(5), whose captured wording does not impose an application-date registration requirement. Verify completion, legal category and applicable version; do not promise acceptance from a pending registry application. Valid qualifying registration addresses this duration exception without adding invented registry conditions, but it does not prove the underlying de facto relationship or remove age criteria. A registry may not have assessed the migration relationship or residence questions; keep those separate.

## Sponsorship scope and historical boundary

The following is a **limited** sponsorship knowledge core, not the entire sponsorship regime. Other restrictions, including regulations 1.20J, 1.20K, 1.20KA and the child-protection provisions of 1.20KB, require separate verification. A clear police certificate does not establish that a sponsor can sponsor. The interaction between unmarried exceptional subclass 820 pathways and clause 820.221A remains unresolved: load [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md) and [verification](verification.md), and do not conclude automatic refusal, exemption or pathway loss.

**Inspected legal baseline:** Regulations 1.20KC and 1.20KD concern sponsorship approval for Class TO/subclass 300, Class UF/subclass 309 and Class UK/subclass 820. They do not apply to the associated Class BC/subclass 100 or Class BS/subclass 801 sponsorship assessment. That is not an exemption from all permanent-stage sponsor or child-safety checking.

The 2016 amendments apply to the specified visa applications **made on or after 18 November 2016**, under Schedule 13 clause 5504. Record the actual application or deemed-application date. A later decision or remittal is not, just by being later, a new application. A pre-boundary 300 history does not grandfather a new 820 application. For a deemed Partner application following marriage, the legally operative notice-receipt date can differ from the original 300 application and marriage date. Use [prospective marriage](prospective-marriage.md) for the actual deeming or review mechanism, not a decision-date shortcut.

Stored policy locates ordinary sponsorship approval within the visa decision assessment, rather than a separate approval notice to the sponsor. Do not represent lodgement of a sponsorship form, an old approval or this preparation review as current approval. The applicable stage and criteria must still be checked.

## Keep people, stages and checking windows separate

| Person and purpose | Preparation rule |
|---|---|
| Sponsor, Australian check under regulation 1.20KC(5)(a) | The request specifies an Australian jurisdiction. The statutory power has no 12-month Australian residence threshold. Current public sponsor instructions direct a complete-disclosure AFP National Police Certificate. |
| Sponsor, foreign check under regulation 1.20KC(5)(b) | For each specified foreign country, count cumulative residence of at least 12 months since the **later** of ten years before the **request date** or the sponsor's sixteenth birthday. Do not substitute application date, uninterrupted residence or total time across different countries. Update post-application residence where relevant. |
| Permanent-stage applicant | Public 801 guidance uses foreign residence since 820 grant; public 100 guidance uses residence since 309 grant, with cumulative 12 months in a country. Do not apply the sponsor's ten-year request-date window. Age, family and 445 cases have their own triggers in [children and checks](children-and-checks.md). Australian certificate repeat instructions are conditional, not a universal fresh purchase. |
| Permanent-stage sponsor and children | Stored guidance retains possible sponsor checks for certain under-18 applicants and child-interest criteria, including possible reuse of earlier checks unless adverse child-interest information exists. No application of 1.20KC at that stage does not mean no possible check. Use [children and checks](children-and-checks.md) for those criteria. |

The Minister may request sponsor police checks on one or more occasions. The sponsor is the person requested under regulation 1.20KC, not the visa applicant. A representative or authorised recipient appointed by the applicant is not automatically appointed by the sponsor. Record each person's actual authority separately; use the applicable appointment instructions, not an assumed shared appointment.

## Australian checks, expiry and reuse

**Public instructions inspected on 17 November 2026:** The [Home Affairs subclass 820 sponsor instructions](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/partner-onshore/temporary-820) and the corresponding 300/309 sponsor directions require complete-disclosure AFP National Police Certificates, not state or territory certificates or standard-disclosure certificates. Use the current immigration/citizenship purpose and actual application instructions. The pages describe 12-month validity and say the Department will advise if certificates expiring before decision need to be supplied again. The 820 page also says a sponsor who supplied checks for a previous 300 sponsorship may not need to resubmit them; the Department will advise. Expiry is neither automatic refusal nor guaranteed reuse.

**Stored preparation guidance, current internal practice not fully verified:**

- Australian checking policy distinguishes **any presence** in Australia since age 16 from never having been present since that age. It includes short visits and presence at ages 16–17, not only residence of 12 months. A never-present-since-16 practice is stored, but the general public AFP direction does not expressly confirm an exception. Obtain applicable official clarification before relying on non-provision; do not disregard a request.
- An expired Australian or foreign sponsor check may be considered under stored policy where finalisation delay was outside the applicant's or sponsor's control **and** the check showed no relevant offences. This is not an entitlement or a confirmed current waiver.
- Stored policy limits subsequent requests to sufficient reason to believe there has been an intervening relevant conviction or outstanding relevant charge. The statute permits requests on more than one occasion; do not use the stored limit to reject a new request.
- For a later 820 application, stored policy distinguishes no earlier 300 assessment, an earlier assessment with no new concerns, an earlier assessment with new concerns, and a different sponsor. Preserve the prior assessment and certificates: no earlier assessment, new concerns or a different sponsor can call for checks; an earlier assessment without new concerns can support a reuse enquiry. This does not establish current eligibility or dispense with an actual request.
- Stored exceptional family-violence or child-of-relationship non-request practice is not a blanket sponsorship exemption, particularly given the unresolved unmarried 820 interaction. Seek branch-specific verification without making sponsor cooperation a condition of safe preparation.

There is **no current sex-based check waiver established by this reference**. Do not infer one from the sponsor's sex, ignore an actual request, or claim that silence in a public page proves an internal practice was repealed.

Keep issue date, certificate type, names/aliases, country, prior attachment and assessment, post-check history and the exact current request. Sponsor check validity does not determine an applicant's first-entry deadline. Stored document guidance accepts electronic sponsor checks without a universal original/certified-copy requirement, while permitting specific originals or certification requests where authenticity is questioned. Follow an actual instruction and [document handling](document-handling.md); do not label a scan authentic merely because it is accepted for upload.

## Requests, delays and unavailable foreign checks

**Inspected legal baseline:** Regulation 1.20KC(6) permits non-approval where a requested check is not provided within a reasonable time. It does not legislate a universal 28-day response period. Stored policy uses 28 days from request as guidance for providing the check or at least evidence of requesting it, with flexibility for longer periods. Evidence of an application for a certificate supports an extension request; it is not the certificate, an automatic extension or a waiver.

Preserve the actual request, intended recipient, sending method, receipt/non-receipt evidence, stated response date, obtaining attempts and any written extension. Do not import ordinary applicant information-request notification rules into sponsor requests. Stored sponsor-request guidance discusses rebuttable receipt presumptions of the seventh working day for prepaid post within Australia and the sending time for electronic communication. These service-law details have not been independently refreshed here: do not calculate a legally binding deadline from them without verifying the applicable law and actual facts. Returned post, a bounced message or an incorrect address can matter. Use [application procedures](application-procedures.md) for request management.

If a foreign check cannot be obtained, identify the country-specific obstacle, official directions, applications, replies and efforts made. Stored sponsor policy uses a **standard Commonwealth statutory declaration**, not the applicant-specific Character Statutory Declaration, setting out inability to obtain the check, efforts made, and either no convictions in that country or full conviction details. Verify current execution and the actual requested alternative; the full country-checking procedure is not established here. Do not submit a false “no convictions” statement or promise a waiver. Regulation 2.03AA's applicant penal-document waiver is not automatically the sponsor's waiver machinery.

No response, no evidence of attempts and no satisfactory explanation can expose sponsorship approval to refusal discretion. An agent must not declare refusal inevitable or approved from the response history alone. Identify the precise missing check or action, assist with a truthful response or extension request, and escalate a consequential outstanding deadline promptly.

## Relevant offences and significant criminal record

**Inspected legal baseline:** Regulation 1.20KC(3) concerns **both** a conviction for a relevant offence and a significant criminal record **in relation to relevant offences**. Do not classify every police entry, charge or general character concern as this particular ground, or treat absence of this ground as overall sponsorship clearance.

The relevant offence categories cover Australian and foreign offences involving:

- violence against a person, including murder, assault, sexual assault and threats;
- harassment, molestation, intimidation or stalking;
- breach of an apprehended violence order or a similar state, territory or foreign order;
- firearms or other dangerous weapons;
- people smuggling;
- human trafficking, slavery or slavery-like practices, including forced marriage, kidnapping or unlawful confinement; and
- attempts, or aiding, abetting, counselling or procuring commission of the specified offences, as defined in subregulation 1.20KC(2).

Foreign offence names may not reveal their legal elements. Preserve the court's finding, conviction record, sentence, translations and relevant legal description; do not map a foreign label to an Australian category by word matching.

Regulation 1.20KD defines significant criminal record for this purpose. Preserve the following distinctions for qualified assessment, **not an automated criminal-record score**:

| Feature | What the source-supported rule requires the reviewer to distinguish |
|---|---|
| Thresholds | Death, life imprisonment, one term of imprisonment of at least 12 months, or two or more terms totalling at least 12 months, for the relevant offences. Do not mix in unrelated offences merely to reach a total. |
| Concurrent terms | Count the whole of each term, even if served concurrently in whole or part; do not count only elapsed prison time. |
| Early release | A shorter period actually served does not replace the sentence imposed. |
| Suspended sentence | Stored policy treats it as a sentence of imprisonment even where no time was served. Preserve the order and length for legal assessment rather than treating suspension as no sentence. |
| Periodic detention | Use the number of days the sentence requires in detention, not the outer calendar span alone. |
| Specified residential orders | Following conviction for a relevant offence, court-ordered residential drug rehabilitation or a residential program for the mentally ill is treated by reference to required participation days under subregulation 1.20KD(4). Do not treat every voluntary treatment program as imprisonment. |
| Quashing or nullification | The conviction or sentence is disregarded in the circumstances specified in subregulation 1.20KD(5). Obtain the actual order. |
| Pardon | The relevant rule requires a pardon whose effect is that the person is taken never to have been convicted. Not every pardon has that effect. |

**Stored interpretation:** For these particular sponsorship and related disclosure provisions, a recorded conviction is distinguished from a finding of guilt without conviction. A good-behaviour bond or intervention program can have different legal effects depending on the order. Obtain the actual record, not a self-description. No relevant conviction, non-relevant convictions alone, or relevant convictions without the significant-record threshold do not establish the refusal ground in 1.20KC(3); other restrictions and safety concerns can still apply. Relevant convictions below the significant-record threshold can still engage the separate disclosure-consent issue.

An unresolved relevant charge is not itself a conviction establishing this ground. Stored policy allows obtaining case-status information and, where appropriate, considering near-term finalisation without unreasonable delay; it does not promise that proceedings will finish before a visa decision. Existing relevant convictions and sentences must be assessed independently. Charges can matter under separate child-protection provisions even where the 1.20KC conviction test is not established.

## Refusal ground, discretion and children

**Inspected legal baseline:** Where both limbs of regulation 1.20KC(3) apply, the Minister must refuse sponsorship approval **unless** approval is considered reasonable under subregulation (4). Separately, failure to provide a requested check within reasonable time can engage subregulation (6) discretion. Neither is an automated decision for this agent.

Subregulation (4) requires consideration of matters including time since sentence completion, the best interests of the sponsor's or primary applicant's children, and relationship length. These are considerations, not separate pass marks, and the list is not exhaustive. Prepare accurate evidence for all relevant matters, without predicting the discretion's exercise.

**Stored preparation guidance:**

- Record sentence completion and subsequent history. The policy cautions against approval based solely on elapsed time where less than five years has passed; five years is not a statutory minimum, a safe harbour or an automatic approval rule. A still-serving sentence does not mean the other considerations disappear.
- Children's interests include biological, adopted and stepchildren of either the sponsor or primary applicant. Stored policy treats their best interests as a primary, not the only, consideration, even where the sponsor does not respond or does not raise them.
- Useful child-specific evidence can cover time lived with the sponsor, financial/emotional/physical care, involvement in education or health decisions, relevant Australian or foreign orders, community integration, and likely consequences of refusal or relocation. Court orders restricting residence or contact must not be displaced by a generic assertion that access to both parents is always best.
- Risk of harm can weigh against approval. Never encourage remaining in an abusive relationship, unsafe contact or breaching an order to strengthen a child's or partner's evidence.
- A long relationship does not alone justify approval, and a short relationship does not alone dictate refusal. Preserve whether the relationship existed during offending or sentence, what the applicant knew before any Department disclosure, and the applicant's freely expressed views. Do not coach a statement of support or willingness to accept risk.

Where an under-18 applicant and registrable-offence concerns trigger both regulations 1.20KB and 1.20KC, stored guidance addresses child-protection assessment first. The precise scope and exceptions of the separate child-protection regime need [children and checks](children-and-checks.md) and [verification](verification.md); do not claim that the discretion here overrides them.

## Disclosure consent and adverse information

**Stored preparation guidance, subject to applicable Schedule 2 criteria:** Police-check provision, sponsorship approval and consent to Department disclosure are different issues. Consent is sought from the sponsor, not inferred from the applicant's agreement. Relevant convictions may require disclosure consent even without a significant criminal record; non-relevant convictions and unrecorded findings are not automatically disclosable under this particular mechanism.

- A recorded express consent in the sponsorship form is distinguished from a blank answer. Stored policy does not require repeating an existing consent solely because time has passed. Verify the actual consent, its scope and any later change; do not infer authority to disclose through this agent or any third party.
- Express refusal of consent can raise a distinct visa-criterion issue, not simply a refusal ground under regulation 1.20KC. Silence is not initially an express refusal: the sponsor should have an opportunity to respond. Failure to respond to that request has its own possible consequences.
- Stored procedure contemplates putting the absence of consent or approval to the applicant for comment, without revealing protected conviction details or protected reasons for non-approval. Do not insert the sponsor's criminal history into a draft adverse-information response or share it through the applicant's representative merely to explain a missing consent.
- Where refusal grounds are being considered, preserve any sponsor invitation to comment and the applicant's actual adverse-information notice. Stored procedure distinguishes those opportunities and the bar on premature decision during the applicable applicant-comment process. Do not invent a notice, assume universal service or review rights, or treat silence as proof that procedural requirements were satisfied. Verify the actual section 57/63 process, exceptions, service and response period through [application procedures](application-procedures.md).

This reference does not authorise obtaining the sponsor's record without permission, contacting a former partner, disclosing a conviction, signing a consent or withdrawing an application. Those actions have separate authority, privacy and safety requirements.

## Safe communication and informed choice

Stored policy envisages an applicant interview before a favourable exercise of regulation 1.20KC(4), with consented disclosure, to help establish free and informed choice. It does not require such an interview merely because sponsorship will not be approved. An interview is a safeguard, not proof of legal sufficiency or a guaranteed Department procedure in every case.

The applicant should have an opportunity to speak privately without the sponsor. In-person discussion is preferred in stored guidance where possible; telephone discussion may be appropriate, but identity, coercion and risk of retaliation require attention. Do not assume an answer is freely given because it arrives in writing, especially if a controlling sponsor is also the authorised recipient. Establish a safe contact method and authorised recipient before sending sensitive questions or documents. Do not expose an applicant's disclosures to the sponsor or require the sponsor's presence.

Disclosure is intended to support informed choice, not force withdrawal or continuation. No agent should pressure the applicant to accept risk, promise that leaving necessarily ends the visa pathway, or make independent evidence work depend on perpetrator cooperation. If the relationship has ended, there is violence, a death, child-rights issue or an unsafe sponsor-contact problem, retain relevant earlier evidence and load [exception pathways](exception-pathways.md) before asking for an ongoing relationship declaration. A deceased, former or unsafe sponsor is not automatically the right person to contact.

## Preparation handoff

Keep a concise case-specific record of:

1. The relationship basis and factual chronology, including the supported commencement date and any material gaps.
2. Evidence in each relevant area, truthful alternatives, differences needing explanation and coverage through the present review date.
3. Both birthdates, the actual application date, and any claimed duration exception with its precise evidence and unresolved conditions.
4. For checks, the person, stage, country, dated residence/presence, exact request, certificate scope and issue date, previous attachment, new concerns and response/extension status.
