# Conditional pathways, relationship changes and remittal

Use this reference when an exception is relevant **or has not yet been excluded**. These are substantive preparation branches, not determinations that an applicant meets every requirement. General preparation support is not legal advice, evidence-sufficiency assessment or a promise of approval, lawful status or review access. Preserve independently useful evidence work while a material legal question remains unresolved.

Read [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md) for application location, validity, stage clocks and reforms; [prospective marriage](prospective-marriage.md) for 300 intentions, conditions and marriage deeming; [relationship and sponsorship](relationship-and-sponsorship.md) for relationship definitions and sponsor rules; and [children and checks](children-and-checks.md) for children, secondary applicants and public-interest checks. [Operational knowledge](operational-knowledge.md), [application procedures](application-procedures.md), [document handling](document-handling.md), [terms](terms.md) and [verification](verification.md) remain applicable.

## Safe use and applicable dates

Do not tell a person that leaving a relationship necessarily removes every possible Partner pathway. Do not require them to remain with, contact, obtain a declaration from, or share account access with an unsafe sponsor. If immediate danger is disclosed, prioritise appropriate local emergency and safety support. Ask only safe, necessary facts, one at a time; an existing document inventory and broad event dates can often be prepared without a detailed account of violence. Keep confidential disclosures out of shared partner summaries.

The **1 July 2024** reforms apply the specified regulations 1.23/1.25 and Schedule 2 changes to applications made before commencement but not finally determined, or made on or after commencement. They do not automatically reopen finally determined cases. Historic entry, marriage, holding and grant-basis facts remain material. The separate review relocation from **14 October 2024** is explained in [prospective marriage](prospective-marriage.md). Use the applicable historical text and actual order for historical cases, rather than renaming an old tribunal reference and retaining a repealed mechanism.

Every branch below leaves other applicable validity, status, sponsorship, health/character, integrity, family and decision criteria intact unless specifically displaced. **No single universal marriage, Australian-entry or family-violence condition applies to every exception.** An absence of an express entry limb in one provision does not waive the onshore location required to make a combined 820/801 application.

“Family violence” in the branches means the legally relevant violence committed by the sponsoring partner specified in that route, established through the applicable Division 1.5 mechanism. It is not this agent's certification. “Specified child-rights circumstances” means the two-sided rights, orders or maintenance circumstances in the next section, not parenthood alone. “Member of the family unit” and “dependent child” have different definitions; do not substitute one for the other.

## Exact child-rights alternative

The applicant must have **custody or joint custody of, or access to, at least one child**, or a **residence order or contact order under the Family Law Act 1975** relating to that child. In relation to the same child, the sponsoring partner must have **court-granted joint custody or access**, a **residence or contact order under that Act**, or an **obligation under a child-maintenance order or another formal maintenance obligation**.

Match both sides to the actual clause: 820.211(8)(d)(ii), 820.211(9)(e)(ii), 820.221(3)(b)(ii), 309.221(3)(b)(ii), 801.221(6)(c)(ii) or 100.221(4AA)(c). This is an independent alternative to family violence where the clause says so; violence need not also be established for that child-rights limb.

Inventory existing birth/parentage evidence, court orders, relevant maintenance instruments and any parenting documents, noting dates, parties, terms, changes and the actual rights claimed. A birth certificate alone does not establish every required right or obligation. A parenting plan is not automatically a court order; sole-custody facts matter. Do not infer the complete legal result from a general statement that parents normally share responsibility, assume old and new family-law terminology is interchangeable, or advise litigation strategy. The exact legal effect of unusual orders or disputed parental rights needs qualified review. Child addition and parental-responsibility visa checks are different questions in [children and checks](children-and-checks.md).

## Branch matrix: before the initial Partner application

### 1. Pending 300, ordinary prospective marriage

An ordinary 300 applicant must satisfy the intended-marriage, age, personal-meeting, personal-knowledge and shared-life intention requirements, including continued named requirements at decision and no Australian-law impediment then. Initial application is outside Australia; sponsorship and other criteria remain. A pending applicant is not yet a 300 **holder** for 820.211(7) or (8).

There is no standalone death or family-violence primary grant exception in the named ordinary 300 criteria. Do not nevertheless declare every other route unavailable. Marriage before decision needs the separate 2.08E notice-based analysis or the applicable review mechanism, not an invented 820/801 conversion.

### 2. Current 300, valid ongoing marriage to the original sponsor

Clause 820.211(6) requires holding 300 at application, a valid marriage to the sponsoring partner, the required sponsorship and continued spouse status; 820.211(1) also excludes a 771 holder. The relevant partner under 820.111 is the **original 300 sponsor**. There is no express entry-on-300 or conditions-8515/8519-compliance limb in (6).

Decision-time continuation or an applicable exception is assessed under 820.221. Ordinary later 801 assessment is under 801.221(2), with its waiting rule and exceptions; marriage does not confer permanent residence. **Holding/cancellation remains gated:** do not promise post-application cancellation is irrelevant. Obtain the actual status, grant, conditions, marriage and application records for the unresolved continuation question described below.

### 3. Current or former 300, ordinary spouse or de facto route

Clause 820.211(2) is a separate route, not an assumed extension of (6). It requires the relevant spouse/de facto relationship, sponsorship and sponsor restrictions; where there is no substantive visa, its Schedule 3 alternatives or compelling-reasons provision require separate assessment. Under 820.111 this route can concern the **original or a subsequent qualifying sponsor**, unlike (5)–(9).

No general entry-on-300 limb is stated in (2). An unmarried or de facto current 300 holder is not automatically excluded from every route because (6) requires marriage. Actual imposed conditions, validity and status effects remain separate. Do not assume a new partner can replace the sponsor in an already existing permanent application.

### 4. Former 300, ongoing marriage and no substantive visa

Clause 820.211(5) requires **no substantive visa at application**, **last entry as a 300 holder**, marriage to the person the applicant entered to marry, cessation of substantive-visa holding **after that marriage**, current spouse status and the required sponsorship. Preserve the original sponsor and the exact last-entry, marriage and cessation sequence. A de facto relationship alone does not meet this limb.

The applicable basis must continue or an available death/cessation alternative under 820.221 must apply. Do not append the ordinary (2)(d) Schedule 3 test as a universal additional limb of (5), but do not waive other validity or criteria. Later 801 ordinarily uses its ordinary route unless the actual 820 grant fits a specified exceptional basis.

### 5. Current 300, original sponsor died before 820 application

Under 820.211(7), the applicant **holds 300**, the original sponsoring partner has died, and the Minister must be satisfied that the applicant would have continued as **spouse or prospective spouse** but for the death. The former prior-marriage and Australian-close-ties limbs have been removed. There is no express entry-on-300 limb in (7), but the combined 820/801 application still has to be made onshore, outside immigration clearance.

Continuation of this basis is addressed by 820.221(1)(a). A person **holding 820 granted on continued satisfaction of (7)** has the particular later link in 801.221(6AA), without the ordinary two-year limb. This is not an exception granting 300 itself after death. **For an unmarried case, the interaction with 820.221A sponsorship remains unresolved; changed holding/cancellation and 820.221 continuation also require the exact applicable assessment.** Do not invent a marriage prerequisite or a blanket sponsorship exemption.

### 6. Current 300, relationship ceased before 820 application

Clause 820.211(8) requires **holding 300**, cessation of the relationship with the **original sponsor**, and either qualifying family violence or the specified child-rights circumstances. The previous valid-marriage prerequisite is removed. The family-violence victim classes are the applicant; a member of the applicant's family unit **who made a combined application with the applicant**; or a dependent child of the sponsor, applicant or both. The perpetrator must be the specified sponsoring partner. Child rights are an independent alternative, not a requirement to prove violence as well.

Continued (8) satisfaction at the 820 decision, followed by **holding an 820 granted on that basis**, links to 801.221(6AA), without the ordinary waiting limb. Do not require unsafe sponsor cooperation. **The unmarried 820.221A interaction and holding/cancellation/continuation remain explicit unresolved gates**, not a finding that the applicant is exempt or excluded.

### 7. Former 300, relationship ceased before 820 application

Clause 820.211(9) requires **no substantive visa at application**, prior 300 holding, cessation of the relationship with the original sponsor, and qualifying family violence or the specified child-rights circumstances. “Does not hold 300” is not an adequate substitute for “does not hold a substantive visa”. The prior marriage-while-300 limb has been removed. Unlike (5), **(9) does not state a last-entry-as-300 requirement**; do not import one from another eligibility or charge provision.

The violence classes are the applicant; the applicant's family-unit member who made the specified combined application; or a dependent child of the applicant, sponsor or both. The original sponsor is the perpetrator for the violence limb. The onshore application location remains. Continued (9) satisfaction at decision and **holding 820 granted on that basis** link to 801.221(6AB), without the ordinary waiting limb.

A former 300 holder with another substantive visa does not meet this limb's status wording; this does not establish failure of every Partner route. Never advise allowing a visa to cease to fit the provision. **The unmarried 820.221A interaction, actual status and decision-time continuation remain gated unresolved questions.**

### 8. Former 300, death alone or a different status position

Do not transfer the current-holder death branch to every former holder. Clause 820.211(7) expressly requires current 300 holding; (9) is not a free-standing former-300 death-only provision. Record the exact current and past visa, expiry or cancellation, entry, event and application dates.

A particular limb's limitation is not universal ineligibility. Independently available ordinary routes or an exception in an actual pending application need their own examination. Do not advise expiry, departure, withdrawal or a new application from this matrix, or invent an equivalent route to avoid a missing predicate.

## Branch matrix: pending 820 or 309

### 9. Pending ordinary 820, ongoing relationship

Establish which of 820.211(2), (5) or (6) applied **at application**, rather than reconstructing a convenient basis from later facts. The partner and sponsor meanings in 820.111 preserve the original/subsequent distinction. Clause 820.221(1)(a) requires continued satisfaction of the applicable limb, with the other decision criteria.

Later ordinary 801 assessment is under 801.221(2), or the separately applicable ministerial alternative, with its own holding, relationship and waiting conditions. It does not impose a new universal relationship-duration period starting at 820 grant. Changed holding or cancellation must not be promised irrelevant to continuation.

### 10. Pending ordinary 820, sponsor dies

Clause 820.221(2) concerns an applicant who would continue to meet **820.211(2), (5) or (6)** except for the sponsoring partner's death, and who would have continued as spouse or de facto partner but for death. It is not an exception for every prospective relationship. There is no longer an Australian-close-ties limb, and this clause does not add a post-application entry requirement.

**Holding an 820 granted under 820.221(2)** gives the distinct permanent link in **801.221(3)**, without the ordinary waiting limb. Preserve the actual exceptional decision basis; the label “bereaved applicant” alone is not the grant record.

### 11. Pending ordinary 820, relationship ceases

Clause 820.221(3) requires that the applicant would continue to meet **820.211(2), (5) or (6)** except for cessation, plus qualifying family violence or the specified child-rights circumstances. The violence classes here are **the applicant or a dependent child of the sponsor, applicant or both**. Do not substitute the broader combined-family-unit class in 820.211(8)/(9). The sponsor must be the perpetrator and Division 1.5 timing/evidence still applies.

**Holding 820 granted under 820.221(3)** links to **801.221(4)** without the ordinary waiting limb. The clause does not add a requirement that the violence caused the separation. Genuine prior relationship evidence remains important; do not demand evidence of a continuing current relationship that the exception addresses.

### 12. Pending 820 originally applied under a current/former-300 exception

Applications under 820.211(7), (8) or (9) retain their **application-time** predicates: current/former holding, no-substantive-visa wording where applicable, original sponsor and relevant death, violence or child-rights facts. Do not replace these with the ordinary pending-820 branches above.

Clause 820.221(1)(a) expressly includes continued (7), (8) or (9). The actual recorded 820 grant basis determines the later **801.221(6AA) or (6AB)** link. **A changed visa-holding position between application and decision and the unmarried 820.221A sponsorship interaction remain unresolved for the affected case.** A source-backed application-time branch is not proof that every continuation or sponsorship question is settled.

### 13. Pending 309, ordinary spouse or de facto relationship

Clause 309.211(2) requires the spouse/de facto relationship with the qualifying Australian citizen, permanent resident or eligible New Zealand citizen at application. Initial ordinary application is outside Australia. Clauses 309.221(1)(a) and 309.223(a) preserve continued relationship and same-person identity; sponsorship and other criteria remain unless an actual exception disapplies a provision.

Grant can be in or outside Australia but not immigration clearance. The later ordinary 100 route is under 100.221(2), or its separately applicable ministerial alternative. Broader grant location does not change initial application location or grant permission to travel.

### 14. Pending 309, intended marriage

Clause 309.211(3) permits the intended-valid-marriage application basis; it is not confined to already married applicants. Apply the distinct intended-spouse sponsorship criteria in 309.213(2). This limb does not require prior 300 holding.

Ordinary 309.224(a) requires that the marriage has occurred and that the applicant remains the intended person's spouse. Exceptional 309.224(b) requires the applicable 309.221(1)(b) exception **and spouse/de facto status before death or cessation**. Mere engagement does not meet that safeguard. Use the fuller intended-marriage preparation in [initial and permanent stages](initial-and-permanent-stages.md).

### 15. Pending 309, sponsor dies

Clause 309.221(2) requires that the applicant would continue to meet 309.211 except for death and would have continued as spouse/de facto partner but for death. An intended-marriage applicant must also satisfy 309.224(b). This death limb has **no current post-application-entry or onshore-decision predicate**. Clause 309.222 is disapplied by its subclause (4) for the specified death/cessation cases; do not demand fresh cooperation from a deceased or unsafe sponsor under that disapplied provision.

Grant remains in/outside Australia, not immigration clearance. At permanent stage, 100.221(3) independently requires the relevant 309 holding and death/counterfactual conditions. Its 100.221(7)(b) waiting exception allows grant within two years; it is not automatic 100 approval.

### 16. Pending 309, relationship ceases

Clause 309.221(3) requires that the applicant would continue to meet 309.211 except for cessation, plus its qualifying violence or child-rights limb. Intended-marriage applicants also need 309.224(b).

The violence limb requires **entry to Australia after making the application** and a victim who is **the applicant or a member of the family unit of the sponsor, applicant or both**. It does not require entry on a 309 or presence in Australia at decision. The child-rights alternative has **no express entry requirement**. Clause 309.222 is disapplied by (4) in these specified cases, not every sponsorship provision for every visa.

Later permanent assessment must independently fit **100.221(4)**, including post-application entry for violence, or **100.221(4AA)** for child rights. Subclause (7)(b) permits grant before two years. Do not turn the earlier exceptional 309 grant into an automatic permanent-grant shortcut.

## Branch matrix: after first-stage grant and early permanent assessment

### 17. Holding 820, ongoing relationship to 801

Ordinary 801.221(2) requires holding 820, the continued sponsorship specified in (b), and spouse/de facto status with the sponsoring partner. Subclause (2A) separately concerns specified ministerial grants. Establish the actual sponsor and grant basis; do not reuse the current-300 wording.

Usually two years must have elapsed since the original application, or the specified ministerial decision for (2A), subject to (6A)/(7). The ordinary clock does not restart at 820 grant. Fresh relationship evidence and other criteria remain.

### 18. Holding 820 granted on an earlier exceptional basis

Identify the exact grant basis and subsequent holding, not merely present relationship status:

- An 820 granted under **820.221(2)** links to **801.221(3)**.
- An 820 granted under **820.221(3)** links to **801.221(4)**.
- An 820 granted on continued satisfaction of **820.211(7) or (8)** links to **801.221(6AA)**.
- An 820 granted on continued satisfaction of **820.211(9)** links to **801.221(6AB)**.

All four require **holding the qualifying 820**. If the grant notice does not identify the legal basis, obtain the decision record or safe official confirmation; do not guess from an upload category. These are separate 801.221(1) alternatives without the ordinary (2)(d)/(2A)(c) waiting limb. Do not force a current ongoing-relationship checklist or demand a new sponsor declaration from a former, deceased or unsafe sponsor. Other 801 criteria survive. An unresolved earlier unmarried sponsorship or holding issue is not silently erased by the proposed link.

### 19. Holding 820, sponsor dies before 801 decision

Clause 801.221(5) requires holding 820, that the applicant would meet (2) or (2A) except for death, and that the applicant would have continued as spouse/de facto partner but for death. The Australian-close-ties limb has been removed. Actual holding is still required; cancellation/status must not be assumed harmless.

Subclause 801.221(7)(d) allows grant within two years without promising timing or approval. This is different from the link for an 820 already granted on a pre-grant death exception.

### 20. Holding 820, relationship ceases before 801 decision

Clause 801.221(6) requires holding 820, that the applicant would meet (2) or (2A) except for cessation, and qualifying violence or the specified child-rights circumstances. Its violence class is **the applicant or a dependent child of the sponsor, applicant or both**, with the sponsor as perpetrator. There is no express post-application entry limb. Parenthood or pregnancy alone is not the child-rights alternative.

Subclause (7)(d) permits grant within two years. The violence need not have caused the breakup, but the relevant violence, or part of it, must fit the applicable Division 1.5 relationship timing and proof. Do not require post-820 violence alone or a continuing relationship with the perpetrator.

### 21. Holding 309, ongoing relationship to 100

Ordinary 100.221(2) requires holding 309 and spouse/de facto status with the sponsoring partner. Subclause (2A) separately concerns specified ministerial grants. Clause 100.111 preserves the original person identified by the resulting 309 application or specified grant basis; it does not permit substitution of a new partner into the same permanent application.

The ordinary wait runs from application; the specified ministerial clock runs from that decision, subject to the stated exceptions. Clause 100.226's nomination requirement is conditional, with the specified ministerial/death/violence/child-rights exclusions. Do not demand nomination universally across exceptional 100 cases.

### 22. A 309 granted after pre-grant death or cessation

Preserve the actual 309.221(2) or (3) grant record. Current permanent criteria do not retain the repealed **100.221(4B)/(4C)** as automatic alternatives merely because such a 309 grant occurred.

Apply **100.221(3), (4) or (4AA)** to the permanent-decision facts. The violence limb includes entry after application, not entry on 309, and does not limit violence to events after 309 grant. Those exceptional limbs and (7)(b) permit consideration before two years. This differs from the express exceptional-820 grant-basis links in branch 18. Do not require a renewed marriage or ongoing relationship that the exception addresses, and do not promise grant from the earlier exception alone.

### 23. Holding 309, sponsor dies before 100 decision

Clause 100.221(3) requires holding 309, that the applicant would meet (2) or (2A) except for death, and that the applicant would have continued as spouse/de facto partner but for death. There is **no current entry-on-309, post-application-entry or Australian-close-ties limb** in (3).

Subclause (7)(b) permits grant within two years; 100.226(b) excludes this exception from its nomination trigger. Other criteria survive and actual visa holding remains necessary.

### 24. Holding 309, separation and family violence before 100 decision

Clause 100.221(4) requires holding 309 and that the applicant would meet (2) or (2A) except for cessation. It also requires **entry to Australia after application** and violence committed by the sponsoring partner against **the applicant or a family-unit member of the sponsor, applicant or both**.

Do not require entry on 309, presence in Australia at decision, or violence occurring only after the 309 grant. The Division 1.5 route and victim-class match still matter. Subclause (7)(b) permits grant within two years and 100.226(b) excludes this exception from the nomination trigger; neither guarantees the result.

### 25. Holding 309, separation and specified child rights before 100 decision

Clause 100.221(4AA) requires holding 309, that the applicant would meet (2) or (2A) except for cessation, and the exact two-sided child-rights circumstances. It has **no express Australian-entry limb**. Do not import the violence limb's entry requirement or infer the rights from parenthood alone.

Subclause (7)(b) permits grant within two years, and the nomination trigger excludes this branch. The legal effect of actual custody, contact and maintenance records remains a distinct child/family-law review question.

### 26. Long-term relationship at application, either permanent route

At application, the relationship as spouses/de facto partners must have continued for at least **three years**, or at least **two years if there is a dependent child of both partners, other than a step-child**, under the regulation 1.03 definition. Use evidence at the relevant application date, not merely later elapsed processing time or the start of dating.

Clause 801.221(6A) disapplies the specified (2)(d)/(2A)(c) wait; 100.221(5) disapplies (2)(c)/(2A)(c). Actual holding, relationship and other criteria remain. Potential early or consecutive assessment is not guaranteed simultaneous grant or a processing deadline. Calculate relevant dates with a tool and preserve uncertainties.

### 27. Permanent humanitarian sponsor, early 100 assessment only

Clause 100.221(6) requires that the sponsor **is or was a permanent-humanitarian-visa holder**, that the married/de facto relationship existed **before that grant**, and that **Immigration was informed before that grant**. Identify the actual visa and earlier disclosure evidence, such as the relevant application, interview or contemporaneous records. A later notification cannot simply substitute for the pre-grant condition.

It disapplies the specified 100 waiting limb; it does not remove other criteria or 309 holding. No equivalent humanitarian-specific 801 waiting exception was identified in the inspected 801 text. Do not transplant it or confuse it with a humanitarian applicant's de facto duration exception under 2.03A.

## Death evidence and previous relationship

Prepare the death certificate, the relationship chronology up to death, and available evidence supporting that the relationship would have continued. Do not demand a declaration from the deceased sponsor. A later relationship after bereavement does not mechanically answer what would have happened but for death; that counterfactual question needs truthful facts and the actual legal assessment, not an automatic exclusion or success finding.

The named current 820/801 death clauses no longer require Australian close business, cultural or personal ties. Do not retain that removed condition as a current-law test. Where a genuinely historical case engages it, retained examples include active involvement in an Australian business and the nature of its impact; actual participation or contribution in cultural activities; and close family/friend or carer relationships with regular ongoing contact. Evidence must fit the applicable historical provision rather than become a new compulsory current checklist.

Stored specific death-evidence directions ask for an **original or certified copy** of the death certificate, while general public online-upload directions use clear colour scans without generic certification. That preparation-source difference remains unresolved for an affected request. Preserve the actual request and available certificate format, translations and obtaining attempts; seek clarification of the precise original/certified expectation rather than declaring an ordinary scan legally invalid or silently waiving a specific request. Follow [document handling](document-handling.md).

## Family-violence evidence: inventory, not sufficiency determination

### Definition, timing and victim class

Regulations 1.21 and 1.22 distinguish relevant violence and being legally taken to have experienced or committed it. Relevant conduct can be actual or threatened and directed at the listed people or property, causing reasonable fear or apprehension for the alleged victim's wellbeing or safety. Do not reduce it to physical injury or require a police conviction in every case.

First match the **Schedule 2 branch's qualifying victim class**, then the **Division 1.5 evidence route**. The broader family-unit wording in 309/100 is not the same as the applicant/dependent-child wording for pending ordinary 820 or post-820 801. The combined-family-unit wording for current/former-300 applications has its own combined-application predicate. These distinctions cannot be collapsed into “any family violence qualifies”.

The applicable Division 1.5 timing generally requires the relevant violence, or part of it, during the qualifying relationship. Violence need not have caused separation. Violence only after the relationship ended does not by itself establish that timing condition. Keep safe existing evidence of the relationship and event period without demanding a continuing relationship or the perpetrator's agreement.

### Judicial and non-judicial routes

| Route | Distinct conditions to preserve in the inventory |
|---|---|
| Regulation 1.23(2)/(3), Family Law Act injunction | The specified injunction under paragraph 114(1)(a), (b) or (c), on the alleged victim's application, and the married-relationship timing in (3). The prospective-spouse reform does not make every injunction limb identical. |
| Regulation 1.23(4)/(5), State/Territory protection order | The order must be under the relevant law and made after the alleged perpetrator had the opportunity to be heard or make submissions, with the stated relationship timing. An order described as “interim” is not automatically sufficient or automatically excluded: its process and content matter. |
| Regulation 1.23(6)/(7), conviction or finding of guilt | The specified conviction/finding for violence against the alleged victim and relevant relationship timing. A charge alone is not that conviction/finding. |
| Regulation 1.23(8), joint undertaking | The alleged victim and perpetrator made a joint undertaking to a court in the proceedings with the specified violence allegation. An undertaking is not automatically the same as a judicial finding. |
| Regulation 1.23(9), prescribed non-judicial claim | The listed victim relationship and evidence under 1.24, including the 1.25 statutory declaration and instrument-specified evidence. This is a different non-judicial route from (8), not permission to omit the declaration or replace prescribed material with any two documents. |

Regulation 1.23(9)'s listed classes include the perpetrator's spouse/de facto/prospective spouse; the specified dependent children; and a family-unit member of that partner who made the specified combined application. **A broader Schedule 2 family-unit class does not automatically fit every person's non-judicial (9) route.** In an atypical sponsor-family-member or other victim-class case, preserve the actual relationship and available judicial/non-judicial records, and identify the precise class/evidence-route question for professional interpretation. Do not automatically reject the person or certify that one route is sufficient. Complete current companion guidance and the designated independent-expert organisation were not comprehensively audited.

### Required declaration and execution

For the 1.23(9) route, regulation 1.24 requires **both** the statutory declaration under 1.25 **and** the specified type and number of supporting evidence items. Regulation 1.25(1) requires the declaration from the alleged perpetrator's **spouse, de facto partner or prospective spouse**. It does not universally require every alleged victim to make the same declaration, nor require a declaration from the perpetrator.

For the maker's own allegation, identify the allegation and alleged perpetrator and, where conduct was towards someone else, that person and their relationship to the maker. Where another person is the alleged victim, identify that victim, allegation, maker/victim relationship, alleged perpetrator, and, where relevant, the person towards whom conduct was directed and the specified relationships; retain the evidence on which the allegation is based.

A draft, ordinary signed account, generic witness supporting statement or Form 888 is not automatically the prescribed statutory declaration. Confirm the current Commonwealth statutory-declaration execution method, the maker's authority, required witness or other permitted process, signature and date under the applicable rules. Do not invent a universal overseas witnessing or electronic-execution rule, backdate, fill a signature, or pronounce legal validity from a scan. Execution and evidentiary sufficiency remain separate qualified questions where uncertain.

### Specified supporting evidence categories

Under **LIN 23/026**, commencing **31 March 2023**, the prescribed non-judicial route requires at least **two items, each from a different evidence type**. Two medical reports are not two different types. The required declaration is additional; it does not replace the two types. The instrument preserves things done under the repealed IMMI 12/116 as if done under the replacement, so do not discard older evidence solely because the old instrument was repealed.

For professional items, check the required **written English, professional or organisational letterhead, maker's full name, occupation, signature, date, address and contact telephone number**, together with category-specific contents. Do not treat those formal checks as evidence acceptance. Match the maker's actual qualification and professional capacity to the instrument's definition.

| Evidence type | Permitted item examples and required substantive contents |
|---|---|
| Medical | A medical or hospital report, discharge summary, letter or statutory declaration by a medical practitioner, registered nurse or midwife acting professionally; identifies the alleged victim and injuries or treatment that may be consistent with family violence. |
| Police | A professional police report, assault/violence record, risk assessment, witness statement or statutory declaration; identifies the alleged victim, identifies or enables inference of the alleged perpetrator, and details the incidents. The category also includes a statement by someone other than the alleged victim made to police during an investigation. Do not insist police evidence is compulsory in every non-judicial claim. |
| Child welfare officer | A professional report, letter or statutory declaration detailing fears for the dependent child's safety because of violence in the household and identifying or enabling inference of the alleged perpetrator. |
| Family-violence support provider | A professional report, letter, risk assessment or statutory declaration stating that a claim was made, giving the professional opinion on consistency with being subjected to violence, and identifying or enabling inference of the alleged perpetrator. |
| Social worker | A professional report, letter or statutory declaration containing the claim, the required professional consistency opinion and alleged perpetrator identification/inference. Check the specified membership/eligibility and counselling/assistance definition. |
| Psychologist | A professional report, letter or statutory declaration containing the claim, the required professional consistency opinion and alleged perpetrator identification/inference. Check the required registration definition. |
| Family consultant or family relationship counsellor | A professional report, letter or statutory declaration stating that the alleged victim was treated or counselled, giving the required consistency opinion, and identifying or enabling inference of the alleged perpetrator. These are the specified professional roles, not every informal counsellor. |
| Education professional | A professional report, letter or statutory declaration by the specified school counsellor, principal or teacher, describing observations made or reported that are consistent with the claim, detailing those observations and identifying or enabling inference of the alleged perpetrator. |

Record what exists, which category it may address, visible formal/content gaps and safe options to obtain missing material. Do not pressure a person to seek treatment, police involvement or unsafe records merely to complete a template, or ask a professional to assert facts they cannot support. An item count and category match are **inventory findings**, not a conclusion that family violence or visa criteria are legally satisfied.

Under 1.23(10)–(14), a qualifying non-judicial claim is assessed by the Minister; if not satisfied on the specified violence matter, the required independent-expert opinion must be sought and treated as correct for that matter. This is not a right for this agent to appoint any expert, a guarantee of referral for every informal account, or a decision on all remaining visa criteria.

### Safe notification and account separation

Official Partner family-violence guidance describes notification of relationship cessation through ImmiAccount with the family-violence selection, or the Partner processing enquiry form where account access is unavailable. It also describes safe-email/new-account and representative opt-out arrangements and privacy-related removal/transfer of the application. Keep the applicant's safe contact and access wishes separate from the sponsor's; do not use shared credentials or promise privacy outcomes without checking the actual official process.

**Removal from an ImmiAccount is not withdrawal of the visa application.** This skill prepares material; it does not operate accounts, submit notifications or withdraw applications. A public offer of more time to provide evidence is not an automatically granted extension of every request or statutory deadline. Preserve the actual request and written extension outcome under [application procedures](application-procedures.md).

## Unresolved sponsorship, continuation and cancellation gates

### Permanent refusal and primary holding exceptions

**100.221(4A)** concerns a person whose 309 ceased following notification of **100 refusal**, not 309 refusal. A remittal of an **820** or **309** refusal is distinct from the permanent-refusal holding exceptions. This is preparation knowledge about possible sequencing, not a promised priority, outcome or fixed processing time.
