# Australian study: operational check for the 5-point factor

## Scope, dates and authority

Use this reference to assess whether evidence supports **5 provisional Australian-study points**, not overall visa eligibility. Read [factor rules](factor-rules.md) and [coverage/conflict notes](verification-notes.md) alongside it. Ordinary education points, specialist educational qualification points and regional-study points have different tests. An Australian award, a skills assessment or a favourable result on another factor does not establish this factor.

The baseline requires the Australian study requirement to have been satisfied **by invitation**. For an EOI-only case use an expressly hypothetical invitation date and identify outstanding completion. Later-issued evidence may establish earlier completion; study completed after invitation cannot replace the invitation-time fact. There is **no general six-month recency requirement for this GSM 5-point factor**: do not reject old qualifying study or transplant a Temporary Graduate visa application window into it. Occupation-relatedness is not a general requirement for this GSM factor. Other visas or streams may have their own completion windows and occupational tests.

Separate legislation, policy interpretation and suggested evidence throughout. Policy examples illustrate branches, not universal laws. Guidance involving credits, deregistration exceptions, language exceptions or remote study needs applicable-version verification where material. Never treat historical online percentages, visa conditions, qualification nomenclature or concessions as evergreen law.

**Currency limitation:** verification-notes.md records official bodies checked on 9 October 2026, with published Regulations compilation coverage ending 1 October 2026; later amendments are not certified. Its core check covers regulation 1.15F and the relevant points/visa-permission provisions, supplementary verification of the academic-year instrument and public GSM credit method, but not complete resolution of credit conflicts or concessions. Refresh the legal version, instruments, commencement/savings and relevant operational interpretation for the actual dates. A report can be evidence-ready yet legally unresolved. Public official lookup starting points are the [Migration Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/latest/text), [CRICOS](https://cricos.education.gov.au/) and the relevant Home Affairs points guidance; a current URL alone does not prove historical applicability.

## Intake discipline and evidence ledger

Ask **one material-fact question per message**, reuse volunteered information and permit unsure/skipped answers. Choose the unknown that most changes the outcome. If a supplied transcript, completion letter or credit decision can resolve the issue, request the relevant redacted excerpt rather than asking the applicant to restate the same fact. Do not turn this reference's matrix into an intake questionnaire.

For each proposed qualification record: awarding institution; exact award and statutory category; course/CRICOS code and historically relevant registration/duration; instruction language; lecture/study commencement; academic completion; units actually completed; credits and their origin; course changes; location/mode by interval; and visa permission/compliance by interval. Identify evidence by document/page/field and distinguish declaration from inspected content. Suggested evidence is not a universal mandatory document checklist.

Maintain **two separate calculations**:
1. Countable academic study, expressed in registered full-time-equivalent weeks, after dealing with credit, shared units, unsuccessful/repeated work, offshore study and other exclusions.
2. Countable calendar duration, using eligible study intervals, academic commencement/completion and a documented treatment of breaks, concurrency and offshore absences.

Do not substitute weeks for months, or elapsed time for registered academic load. Use a calculation tool, retain assumptions and calculate alternative scenarios where an interpretation is unresolved.

## Core requirements

The baseline in regulation 1.15F is completion of one or more degrees, diplomas or trade qualifications for award by an Australian educational institution as a result of registered courses, completed over at least **16 calendar months**, involving at least **two academic years** of study, with instruction in English, undertaken in Australia while holding a visa authorising study. Academic requirements met, rather than ceremonial conferral, define completion. Every element needs its own evidence and applicable-rule check.

### Completion: academic outcome, not graduation ceremony

Use the date final results or confirmation of all academic requirements became available/published. Enrolment, finishing classes, paying fees, attending graduation and a certificate's conferral date are not substitutes. A transcript showing only admission to the degree/award date may require an institutional completion letter. A May conferral may follow completion the previous November; supplementary examinations could produce a later actual completion date. Determine the real fact rather than automatically choosing either date.

For research study, **thesis submission alone is insufficient**. Obtain confirmation the assessing panel accepted the thesis as complete with no further changes required and all other academic requirements were met. If the confirmation omits the exact completion date, the letter date is a policy evidentiary fallback, not proof that submission was completion. Seek a precise institutional date where timing affects invitation or duration. Do not extend duration using graduation evaluation, administrative notification delays or later conferral.

### Qualifying award: substance and definitions

Check the applicable regulation 2.26AC(6) definitions, not course marketing or an AQF label alone:
- A degree includes qualifying bachelor/higher degrees and postgraduate diplomas within the statutory definition. Check entry requirements; the bachelor limb includes a minimum three-year full-time requirement or equivalent part-time period.
- Diploma includes authorised associate diploma/diploma under the historical tertiary register as at **1 July 1999**, and diploma/advanced diploma under the separate AQF limb. An associate **degree** is not automatically a qualifying diploma.
- A trade qualification can fall within the specified apprenticeship/training-contract branch involving formal technical study and employment, or the specified AQF Certificate III-or-higher skilled-trade occupational major-group branches. Check the actual trade and classification/version; not every Certificate III/IV is a trade qualification.

A Certificate II, a non-trade Certificate III/IV, or a Graduate Certificate is not a qualifying award on its own. This list is illustrative, not exhaustive. Their credited study may need separate analysis where it forms part of a completed qualifying award. A prerequisite/enabling course is not automatically part of the final qualification: an English-entry certificate required by the provider does not count merely because it allowed entry to a bachelor's course.

### Combining courses and pathways

One single two-year award is not essential. More than one qualifying award may contribute, with no universal requirement to complete the courses inside a continuous 24-month window. Breaks do not automatically invalidate otherwise qualifying study. However, do not assume any collection of courses can be added: document qualifying awards and the relationship of pathway, packaged or credited study to the resulting qualification.

The operational guidance describes same-course/packaged pathways while also allowing more than one qualifying degree, diploma or trade qualification. These propositions require careful application, not a blanket rule that all awards must share a title or every unrelated award can be combined. It also gives an exclusion example involving unrelated trade study after a degree/diploma in a non-trade nomination context. Preserve that fact-sensitive concern; do not turn a Temporary Graduate occupation-link test into a universal GSM requirement. If the outcome depends on unrelated-course aggregation or the scope of that exclusion, mark the combination unresolved pending authoritative clarification rather than automatically reject or add it.

**Nested/packaged study:** successive modular certificates can contribute where the series results in a qualifying degree/diploma/trade award. Until that final qualifying stage is completed, non-qualifying interim certificates do not alone establish compliance. Illustrations include a four-module pathway ending in a diploma and a cookery/hospitality progression ending in a diploma. The examples show natural progression and sufficient academic load; they do not establish that every similarly named package satisfies all other elements. Accounting bachelor's/master's, accounting master's/MBA and accounting graduate diploma/commerce master's are illustrative pathway patterns, not a closed whitelist.

**Course change:** abandoned initial study does not count merely because it was attended. If eligible Australian nursing units are accepted into a completed science degree, the earlier actual study can contribute to calendar duration and the receiving course's registered equivalent of the credited units can contribute to academic weeks. These durations may differ. Initial nursing study with no credit into the later commerce degree does not contribute on that basis; consider any separately completed qualifying award on its own merits.

**Institution change:** eligible study carried into the eventual qualifying award can count across providers. Document the first provider's units/dates, transfer credit and the receiving course's proportional registered duration. Do not double-count the original units and the credited exemption. A provider-transfer example using a Certificate IV does not make every standalone non-trade Certificate IV a qualifying award; the final-award/credit pathway still needs to be established.

**Credited prior Australian certificate study:** a non-trade certificate that otherwise satisfies the relevant Australian-study conditions can contribute through units accepted into a subsequently completed qualifying degree/diploma. Count only the attributable eligible study, not automatically the whole earlier certificate. Use actual original study for calendar time and supported registered equivalents for academic load. The same study cannot count once for the certificate and again for the later award.

### Credit, RPL and recognition: do not flatten distinct branches

Record every exemption, recognition of prior learning (RPL) and transferred credit, its basis and effect on units, registered duration and actual completion. Separate:
- Prior **eligible Australian study** accepted into the final award: potentially countable once, with original eligible time and appropriate registered-equivalent academic load documented.
- Prior **offshore study** credited by an Australian provider: not actual study undertaken in Australia, and not Australian calendar time merely because recognised.
- Other RPL, including experiential recognition or a poorly documented exemption: do not invent a period of eligible Australian study; establish the basis and governing treatment.

Official GSM public guidance uses non-duplicated qualifying study after excluding overseas/non-CRICOS and previously counted credits. There is an unresolved interpretive tension with the stored operational treatment of RPL. One branch allows provider-permitted RPL, including overseas learning, toward the academic-year test because the test concerns completion of registered courses; another restricts offshore RPL where it reduces duration below the minimums and warns against fast-tracking. An illustration uses a three-year registered bachelor's plus a one-year master's, despite a year of overseas credit, distinguishing the remaining Australian actual study from the gross registered course lengths. **That illustration is not a universal rule that gross registration always counts or that every credit must always be deducted.**

For a credit-sensitive claim, show gross registered weeks, recognised/exempted units, original study location/eligibility, receiving-course equivalents, net remaining study and actual Australian duration separately. Preserve both relevant branches. Do not use offshore credit to cure an established Australian-duration shortfall; do not automatically delete genuinely eligible prior Australian credited study. If gross-versus-adjusted weeks or an RPL exception decides whether the threshold is reached, retain alternative calculations and report **unresolved** until the applicable rule/interpretation is established. A generic RPL exception does not authorise unlimited offshore or unauthorised study for GSM.

### Two different kinds of overlap

For **academic load**, count identical/shared/credited units once. Distinct units in concurrently completed qualifications may contribute separate academic load. A proportional illustration: two registered 78-week master's courses each contain shared core units equivalent to 52 weeks and separate electives equivalent to 26 weeks per course. Count the shared core once plus the distinct elective sets, not both whole courses. Verify real course structure and weighting; equal unit counts do not always mean equal registered weeks.

If only the final qualification is relied on, eligible credits from earlier qualifications can contribute its academic registered equivalents, with the original eligible actual study used for calendar duration. If both qualifications are relied on, select one supported allocation of the overlapping study and record it; do not count it in both.

For **calendar time**, concurrent elapsed periods count once even where the academic units are distinct. Eight weeks spent finishing one qualification while commencing another remain eight elapsed weeks, not sixteen. A high academic-week total from concurrent study does not establish 16 calendar months.

### CRICOS registration and provider disruption

Check that the actual courses, not merely the institution, were registered during the relevant study; obtain historical code, duration and status evidence where present-day search results are insufficient.
- **Deregistration mid-course:** a transfer to a registered provider may preserve earlier eligible study incorporated into the completed award. Document registration dates, transfer and credits.
- **Continuing after deregistration:** post-deregistration study ordinarily does not count; mere continued delivery is insufficient.
- **Suspension of new enrolments with registration retained:** existing students' study may still qualify. Suspension is not automatically cancellation.
- **Suspension leading to loss of registration:** post-loss study does not count on the ordinary branch.
- **Late deregistration:** a policy exception exists where deregistration occurs less than two months before expected completion, because transfer may be unreasonable. Related guidance describes deregistration within two months of completion and potential officer escalation. Preserve the expected/actual-date distinction and boundary uncertainty. This needs discretionary/authoritative consideration, not an applicant entitlement or an agent waiver of registration. Record both dates, remaining study and practical alternatives. Seek qualified advice or an appropriate public clarification channel; do not direct applicants to staff-only mailboxes.

### At least 16 calendar months

Start from actual study commencement, generally lectures, **excluding orientation**. End at academic completion as established above. Overload, extra summer/winter teaching or accelerated completion may legitimately finish a long registered course in under 16 months; that still fails the independent calendar-duration threshold.

Build eligible intervals across the contributing courses and merge concurrent time. Ordinary in-course vacations may be compatible with the course's elapsed calendar duration; an arbitrary inter-course gap does not create countable study time merely because the first commencement and last completion are far apart. Separate scheduled recess, deferral, unrelated inactivity, travel and excluded offshore study rather than adding every day indiscriminately. Courses can be separated by a break without forfeiting their eligible study; acceptance of a break is not proof the entire break counts.

**Offshore holidays:** continuous physical presence throughout every calendar day is not required. A return trip overseas during an out-of-session holiday need not itself disqualify the course. The operational wording about offshore time and in-session study is internally inconsistent; do not convert it into a continuous-presence rule, an automatic deduction of every holiday day, or permission to count offshore teaching. Map travel against the course calendar and actual study activity. If treatment of an absence changes the 16-month result, leave that treatment unresolved and obtain clarification.

### At least two academic years: 92-week baseline

The stored academic-year instrument specifies **at least 92 weeks** of registered course duration for two academic years. LIN 19/085 section 6 was checked on 9 October 2026 and its official landing page labelled it in force: one academic year is at least 46 registered-course weeks. Verify the version applicable to the study/assessment dates; this does not close the separate Regulations coverage gap. In straightforward cases without credit/RPL or other exclusions, use the full-time duration registered on CRICOS for one course, or supported non-duplicated durations for multiple courses.

This measures study load, not simply time enrolled. A registered 78-week course does not become 92 weeks because the student took two calendar years. Failed/repeated subjects cannot create extra academic load by being counted twice; only successfully completed study contributes. Apply credit, overlap, offshore and other branches before concluding. Slower or part-time completion can affect calendar duration without increasing registered academic weeks.

### Instruction in English

An Australian qualification can ordinarily be accepted as evidence of English instruction unless course content suggests otherwise. For a foreign-language component, inspect the transcript and instructional content.
- **Incidental language study:** policy permits incidental foreign-language units while still treating the English requirement as satisfied. The stored acceptable measure is **no more than 10%** of study in a language other than English during the two academic years. First-year language electives are an illustration, not an automatic exemption. Verify proportion/content and applicable policy; this is not a legislative amendment that all courses may deliver 10% in any language.
- **Advanced interpreting/translating:** some non-English instruction may be compatible where its purpose is acquisition of interpreting/translating skills and English is central, rather than a foreign-language qualification. The stored criteria include entrance IELTS average 6.5 with at least 6 in each component and a resulting qualification at NAATI Professional Level; advanced diploma, bachelor and master's awards are examples. These are historical policy criteria requiring verification against actual course and applicable terminology. Do not silently substitute a modern credential, impose these scores on every study claimant or treat this as proficient/superior English evidence.

### Physical location, mode and lawful study

Match actual study location and study-authorising visa status to the counted intervals. Substantive visas and the applicable Bridging A/Bridging B routes must permit study and conditions must be complied with under the relevant provisions. Not every bridging visa qualifies; not every course requires a Student visa. A visa held now does not cure a past unauthorised interval.

Study in excess of a visa's permitted duration, or when study was prohibited, cannot count for the period in breach. The historical Working Holiday example counts only the permitted four months of a six-month course; check the actual condition and law at those dates, rather than universalise that cap. Map changes of visa/condition and any exemption. RPL-related caveats require their own analysis; they are not automatic permission to count unlawful study. Preserve confirmed breaches neutrally and recommend qualified advice where consequences extend beyond points.

**Online/blended study:** physical presence in Australia remains material for the ordinary GSM branch. Online study is not automatically offshore, and an Australian provider is not proof of Australian presence. Obtain actual location, delivery mode and course/regulatory permissions. Historical guidance's 25% online limit is not a certified current cap. Ordinary study on campus is the policy intention; physically present online/blended study requires the applicable rules, rather than automatic acceptance or exclusion solely from the label.

**Distance education:** offshore time ordinarily cannot count. Being resident in Australia does not, by itself, settle every distance-delivery question. Review actual arrangements and applicable rules; do not import the separate regional-study prohibition on distance education into this ordinary factor without analysis.

**Overseas campus/exchange:** offshore study at an Australian institution's overseas campus or on exchange ordinarily cannot count as Australian study. A person may nevertheless satisfy the thresholds using sufficient eligible Australian portions of the overall qualification. The order of Australian/offshore semesters is not decisive: the course may start or finish offshore while the eligible Australian portions still satisfy the requirement. Do not insist the final semester was Australian. Overseas research periods likewise cannot count as actual Australian study. Separately recognised RPL must go through the conditional credit analysis, not automatic inclusion.

**COVID-related offshore learning:** the stored concession expressly concerns impacted Subclass 485 applicants studying offshore on a study-authorising Australian visa and unable to travel to Australia. Do not automatically transfer it to GSM points, treat past 485 acceptance as proof of this factor, or assume its original duration remains current. Any proposed GSM concession needs its own applicable legislative/policy basis and dates; until verified, the affected GSM study remains unresolved or excluded under the ordinary branch.

## Mandatory report gate

Start each standalone report with the notice above. Report **Australian-study 5-point factor: supported / not supported / unresolved**, and label the result a preparation finding, not an official decision or visa eligibility opinion.

- **Supported — provisional 5:** all material elements are evidenced and the applicable-rule checks cover the relevant branches/dates. Identify the invitation date and counted study. If using a stored baseline only, say evidence supports the baseline but applicable-law status is unresolved; do not present an unqualified supported legal conclusion.
- **Not supported — provisional 0 for this factor:** reliable facts and applicable rules establish a failure, such as academic completion after invitation or an established shortfall in either threshold. State the decisive element. Do not convert this into overall visa ineligibility.
- **Unresolved — possible 5, not included as unqualified supported points:** missing evidence, credit ambiguity, material discretion, disputed dates/location or incomplete legal coverage can change the outcome. Do not label unknown study as zero or discard it because old.
