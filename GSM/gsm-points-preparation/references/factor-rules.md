# GSM points: factor rules and preparation branches

General information and preparation support only, not legal advice. No visa approval, processing time or other immigration outcome is guaranteed. Materials are provided as-is. Creators, maintainers and distributors disclaim liability only to the extent permitted by applicable law; rights and liabilities that cannot lawfully be excluded are preserved.

## Scope, authority and currency gates

Use this cross-subclass reference to organise a provisional points claim and its supporting evidence, not to decide visa eligibility, issue an official assessed score or predict an invitation. Points recognise attributes associated with skilled employment and settlement; selection, program priorities, occupation settings and other visa criteria remain separate. A points total does not establish a valid application or entitlement to a visa.

The rules and numbers below are a **stored preparation baseline, subject to refresh against applicable legislation**. They are not a blanket statement of current law. Check the visa subclass and stream, invitation date, application date, assessment stage, relevant legislative compilation and commencement/transitional instruments before treating a branch as applicable. Historically this framework covered the points-tested 189 stream, 190, the first provisional 489 stream and 491 applications lodged from 16 November 2019. Preserve 489 for dated historical cases; do not offer it as an automatically available current pathway or assume every stream uses the same test.

Read [coverage/conflict notes](verification-notes.md) alongside these rules. Actual official bodies were checked on 9 October 2026, but the Regulations compilation coverage ends 1 October and later amendments remain unverified. Those notes supplement missing detail and qualify the English-transition, diploma and specialist-location branches. Links below remain lookup starting points, not blanket currency certification:
- [Migration Regulations 1994](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B03551/latest/text): locate Schedule 6D, Division 2.6, the relevant Schedule 2 criteria, definitions and dated instruments. A `latest` URL alone does not establish the version applicable to an earlier event.
- [Home Affairs 189 points table](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/supporting/Pages/Work/189-points-table.aspx): useful cross-check for that stream; do not use it alone to prove historical rules or another subclass's criteria.

Keep legislative requirements, policy interpretation/discretion and suggested preparation evidence distinct. Suggestions below are not universal mandatory document lists. Unknown or missing evidence means **unresolved**, not automatically zero, not applicable or waived. Preserve potentially relevant claims and alternative branches pending verification; do not delete substantive guidance merely because public corroboration is unavailable.

## Dates, scores and decision boundaries

Maintain separate date fields for invitation, application, points assessment and any relationship/status changes. Ask for **one intake fact per turn**, reuse volunteered information, allow unsure/skipped answers and skip irrelevant branches. A report may summarise several issues but must not become a bulk intake questionnaire.

Baseline timing distinctions:
- Most factor facts must have been satisfied at invitation. Later documents may evidence an earlier fact; later acquisition of a qualification is not the same thing.
- The formal assessment applies the regulations in force when the points assessment is made, subject to applicable savings/transitions. Do not freeze all legislation at lodgement simply because most underlying facts are tested at invitation.
- The qualifying score is determined by the applicable pass-mark instrument at assessment. Do not hard-code a pass mark or confuse it with a competitive invitation threshold.
- The relevant visa criterion requires the assessed score to be at least both the invitation score and the qualifying score.
- An EOI/SkillSelect indicative score, invitation score and an applicant-agent estimate are not the formal assessed score. Label every preparation total **provisional estimate** and identify unresolved factors.
- Factors contributing to the eventual assessed total need not be identical to the EOI estimate. This does not excuse an inaccurate EOI or permit post-invitation facts to substitute for invitation-time requirements.

A formal assessed score is a separate statutory decision, made under a separate delegated power, not an automatic consequence of authority to grant/refuse a visa. Under the stored decision framework it is final once given and cannot simply be revisited or revoked, even to correct an error in either direction; merits review may set aside/substitute decisions. An official's internal estimate must be clearly recorded as an estimate; communicating or inadequately labelling it can have legal consequences. This agent must never present itself as that decision maker.

A refusal based on failing the invitation-score or qualifying-score criterion requires that the applicant first be given the formal assessed score. Do not broaden this into a claim that every possible refusal ground requires a points assessment. Obtain qualified advice for a formal assessment dispute or proposed refusal; do not invent a review forum, deadline or remedy from an old framework.

## Nominated occupation and skills assessment

Baseline application requirements link the primary applicant's nominated skilled occupation to the occupation instrument at invitation and the occupation specified in the invitation, together with a declaration of a suitable assessment by the relevant assessing authority. Check the actual subclass/stream's validity and substantive criteria separately. Skills suitability is not itself a standalone points factor, but the nominated occupation affects employment and professional-year claims.

**Suggested preparation:** retain the invitation, occupation code/title and applicable list, assessment outcome and date, authority, purpose of assessment, and any stated skilled-from date or limitations. Check primary-applicant assessment validity under the relevant visa criteria; do not import the partner-specific older-assessment rule below. General professional society membership is not a suitable migration skills assessment. Medical and legal exceptions require their own checks rather than a blanket registration shortcut.

## Age

Use age **at invitation**, not at intake, lodgement or today's birthday. Baseline table, subject to applicable legislation refresh:

| Age at invitation | Points |
|---|---:|
| 18–24 inclusive | 25 |
| 25–32 inclusive | 30 |
| 33–39 inclusive | 25 |
| 40–44 inclusive | 15 |

Do not infer an eligible application outside these bands from this table or assign an invented band. Verify separate visa age criteria. Suggested evidence: identity/date-of-birth record and invitation date. Resolve discrepancies before computing the band.

## English language

Baseline table, subject to applicable legislation refresh; use **one category**, not accumulated tiers:

| Category established under the applicable rules | Points |
|---|---:|
| Competent English | 0 |
| Proficient English | 10 |
| Superior English | 20 |

Competent English is a separate primary-applicant baseline requirement in this framework even though it earns no English points. Zero points does not mean the English requirement is absent or satisfied without evidence.

The stored proficient/superior branch requires an accepted test with the required result taken in the three years immediately before invitation; it provides no exception to the test requirement for these points. Do not assume a passport, provider exemption, professional-year completion or native-language claim substitutes for that test. Competent-English evidence has its own definition and possible evidence routes: verify rather than importing the higher-tier test rule wholesale.

**Currency gate:** English test arrangements changed in 2025. Retrieve the rules applicable to the **test date and invitation**, accepted provider/test/version, component thresholds, delivery mode, validity period and transitional treatment. Do not use memorised test scores or claim a historical test instrument is current. Keep the three-year rule as baseline pending that dated check, not an unqualified present-law assurance.

Suggested evidence: result report, test date/type and identifying details sufficient for verification, with unnecessary identifiers redacted. Do not count a post-invitation test as satisfying an invitation-time English-points requirement merely because the score is higher.

## Employment duration, location and points

Consider relevant skilled work in the **ten years immediately before invitation**. Eligible periods may be non-continuous; unrelated work, study or gaps do not erase other qualifying periods. Australian and overseas durations are assessed separately. Do not combine short overseas and Australian periods to reach either duration threshold.

Baseline tables, subject to applicable legislation refresh:

| Eligible overseas skilled employment in that window | Points |
|---|---:|
| Less than 3 years | 0 |
| At least 3 but less than 5 years | 5 |
| At least 5 but less than 8 years | 10 |
| At least 8 years | 15 |

| Eligible Australian skilled employment in that window | Points |
|---|---:|
| Less than 1 year | 0 |
| At least 1 but less than 3 years | 5 |
| At least 3 but less than 5 years | 10 |
| At least 5 but less than 8 years | 15 |
| At least 8 years | 20 |

**Combined overseas and Australian employment points are capped at 20.** Calculate each category first, then apply the cap. Do not convert excess employment points into another factor or count the same elapsed period twice within a duration. Uncertain overlapping work arrangements require resolution before finalising a duration.

Location follows where the work was physically undertaken, not just the employer's registration or payroll country. Work performed while physically in Australia is assessed as Australian employment, not overseas employment merely because the employer is overseas. A postcode is not universally necessary: work for an Australian-registered company on qualifying resources/sea installations attached to the Australian seabed in Australian waters can fall within Australian work under the statutory territorial provisions. Keep offshore/remote-location cases conditional pending verification of those provisions and the actual location.

Suggested preparation: a dated timeline of each role, actual work location, occupation/duties, paid hours, leave, relevant visas/work permission and documentary support. Aggregate eligible intervals without assuming every period in a CV qualifies.

## Paid hours, leave and flexible work

The baseline definition of employed is engagement in an occupation **for remuneration for at least 20 hours per week**.

- Emotional satisfaction, unpaid experience, living allowances or scholarships intended to meet a postgraduate student's expenses are not remuneration for this factor.
- The professional-year placement is developmental training, not skilled employment for points, even if described as work experience. Separate paid skilled employment undertaken concurrently but not connected with that placement can be considered on its own facts.
- Ordinarily the threshold concerns each week, not an unrestricted average across a career. Contracts distributing hours over longer cycles, including shift, fly-in/fly-out and seafaring work, may be taken into account by the decision maker.
- Policy allows fair and reasonable consideration of casual work falling short in some weeks because of illness or other compelling/compassionate reasons. This is a discretionary consideration, **not an automatic waiver** or permission to average away every shortfall.
- Under the stored leave rule, only leave on full pay counts. Extended unpaid leave, including unpaid parental leave, does not count. Other leave/pay arrangements need clarification rather than an invented inclusion rule.

Suggested support for variable hours/shortfall branches: employment contract/roster, payroll records, paid hours and reasons for exceptional weeks. These are preparation suggestions; a decision maker may seek detailed terms and salary evidence. Separate an ordinary threshold claim from an exception requiring consideration.

## Skilled level, related occupations and career progression

Assessment considers the relevant authority's employment opinion, including its deemed-skilled date; the applicable occupational classification and prerequisite qualifications/experience; and other relevant records/references. Do not treat a negative employment opinion as the sole analysis or blindly delete every earlier period.

The stored policy directs consideration of classification information and the more beneficial outcome where the authority's opinion would give fewer points than claimed in the EOI. It also recognises that the regulation does not demand skills of a particular standard throughout employment and that actual performance of at least some duties prescribed for the occupation can be relevant. Preserve this as a reasoned assessment branch, **not a promise that all pre-assessment work qualifies**. Check the applicable rule/classification, duties, prerequisites and authenticity; leave contested periods conditional.

Closely related employment ordinarily falls within the same occupational unit group, in a skilled occupation on the lists applicable to the subclass. For example, general, management and taxation accounting can be related within a unit group. Job titles alone are insufficient.

There are policy exceptions for well-established career advancement outside that unit group: promotion from engineering/accounting to chief professional or executive roles can retain a field-of-expertise connection and greater responsibility. Some related work lies outside even the minor group, such as a geologist becoming a lecturer using that expertise; managerial roles may sit in a different major group. Do not automatically reject these roles solely on code mismatch or accept all management/teaching work as related. Suggested support: duties, progression, expertise used, responsibility and occupational links. Verify classification/version changes before relying on an old code grouping.

## Medical and legal assessment exceptions

**Medical practitioners:** the stored exception accepts specified full medical registration as a favourable skills assessment: full/unconditional/general registration, or conditional specialist registration confined to a specialty **without further training or supervision requirements**. Verify the actual registration certificate, applicable authority and current migration assessment pathway; not every conditional registration is interchangeable with those categories.

For employment points, intern/registrar work and Australian medical-practitioner employment under conditional medical registration may be recognised as skilled work. This employment branch is distinct from the full-registration assessment exception. Previous general-practitioner work may count for a specialist despite different unit groups within the medical minor group; do not reject it solely on the usual unit-group boundary. Assess duties, dates, paid hours and lawful work independently.

**Solicitors and barristers:** the stored exception recognises admission to the relevant state legal admissions authority or a state/territory Supreme Court as a suitable skills assessment. Obtain the admission evidence and verify the applicable pathway. General professional association membership/registration in other occupations does not become a skills assessment by analogy.

## Lawful Australian work

The baseline Australian-employment rule requires a substantive visa, Bridging A or Bridging B with work permission, and compliance with its conditions during the claimed period. Work without permission or in breach of hours/other work conditions cannot count for the period in breach. Do not count a role merely because it was paid, skilled or on an employer letter.

Check visa conditions **applicable at the actual work dates**. Historical student work-hour examples are not current caps. Map permission and compliance to the timeline, identify disputed intervals and seek qualified advice if a breach may have consequences beyond points. Do not conceal a breach, retrospectively alter hours or assume permission held at application cures earlier unauthorised work.

## Australian professional year

Baseline: **5 points** for a completed qualifying Australian professional year (PY), subject to applicable legislation refresh. The checked Schedule 6D item 6D61 specifies at least **12 months in the 48 months immediately before invitation**; official guidance clarifies completion within that window, with commencement able to precede it. Verify the specified program, occupational relationship, completion and applicable version. See verification-notes.md for checked source and coverage limits.

A PY is structured professional development combining formal learning and workplace experience. Stored programs include IT through the Australian Computer Society, accounting through Chartered Accountants Australia and New Zealand/CPA Australia/Institute of Public Accountants, and engineering through Engineers Australia. This is not a claim that all remain available or specified today; check the dated ministerial specification and program status.

Provider enrolment can establish the provider's assessment of a closely related occupation under the stored policy. If degree, assessment and program appear unrelated, preserve the concern for clarification rather than automatically overriding the provider or ignoring the discrepancy. A missing graduate-list entry requires confirmation with the provider/appropriate channel, not fabricated completion evidence or applicant access to an internal departmental mailbox.

A PY/provider English exemption does not confer proficient/superior English points. The internship component cannot also be claimed as skilled employment; unrelated concurrent paid skilled work remains separately assessable. Suggested evidence: completion certificate, provider/program, dates and occupational linkage.

## Education points and highest qualification

Baseline table, subject to applicable legislation refresh. Award **only the highest eligible education category**, not points for each degree:

| Eligible category | Points |
|---|---:|
| Doctorate from an Australian institution or overseas doctorate of a recognised standard | 20 |
| At least bachelor-level qualification from an Australian institution or overseas qualification of a recognised standard | 15 |
| Eligible Australian diploma or trade qualification, or qualification/award recognised by the relevant assessing authority as suitable for the nominated occupation | 10 |

Use requirements met by invitation under the applicable category, with reliable award/completion evidence. An Australian-awarded qualification can be delivered offshore and still fall within the education factor; that does **not** establish Australian study, regional study or specialist Australian-study points.

Australian degree/diploma/trade categories use statutory definitions rather than informal labels. In the checked legislative definition, the historical tertiary register as at **1 July 1999** covers authorised associate diploma or diploma; the separate Australian Qualifications Framework (AQF) limb includes diploma or advanced diploma. Do not import inconsistent historical-summary wording; see verification-notes.md and check the applicable version. The trade branch covers qualifying apprenticeships/training contracts under industrial training law/awards involving formal technical study and employment, and applicable AQF Certificate III-or-higher skilled trades under the specified occupational major groups. Historical ASCO classifications may matter for old qualifications; do not replace their dates with current classification assumptions. Do not treat every Certificate III or every overseas diploma as automatically entitled to these Australian categories.

Other occupation-suitable qualifications/awards recognised by the relevant assessing authority can support the 10-point branch even where the formal Australian diploma/trade definitions are not met. Recognised-standard assessment can also support an overseas bachelor-or-higher branch. Keep those routes distinct.

## Qualification recognition and alternative evidence

For an overseas bachelor-or-higher qualification, assess recognised standard by considering:
1. Recognition by the relevant occupation assessing authority **at invitation** as suitable for that occupation.
2. Recognition by the ministerially specified qualification-recognition body.
3. Duration of study.
4. Other relevant matters.

The stored specified recognition body is VETASSESS, but its specified status must be refreshed. A recognition opinion can be provided up to the points decision; distinguish the date of the later opinion from the earlier qualification fact and any invitation-time authority-recognition requirement. The decision maker must have regard to that opinion; it does not replace authenticity checks or automatically determine every category.

Absence of an opinion from the occupation authority or recognition body does not erase the qualification. Preserve alternative relevant material such as Country Education Profiles, education-recognition advice and applicant-provided evidence. Do not turn a suggested extra assessment into a universal mandatory purchase.

An overseas postgraduate qualification may be considered without a prior bachelor's degree if its recognised standard is at least comparable to Australian bachelor level. A title such as graduate certificate/diploma or master's alone is insufficient. An occupation authority's recognition is relevant but not conclusive of bachelor-level equivalence. If equivalence supports the higher category, the baseline is 15 points; if it does not, but the award is recognised as occupation-suitable, the 10-point alternative may remain. Do not stack them.

A bachelor-or-higher qualification need not relate to the nominated occupation. An unrelated overseas bachelor's may support 15 points through recognised-standard evidence even if the occupation authority's skills assessment relied on other qualifications/experience. Verify the recognition rather than requiring an occupational link that this education branch does not demand.

Suggested evidence: qualification, transcript, entry requirements, course duration, completion/award dates, awarding institution, recognition opinion and verifiable records. Assessing authorities rely on applicant claims; their favourable opinion does not immunise false or inauthentic supporting documents.

## Doctoral versus professional titles

A qualifying doctorate requires substantial postgraduate study and research, with a thesis/dissertation or equivalent, not simply a professional title containing “Doctor”. Both research and professional doctorates can qualify where the required standard is demonstrated. In the Australian framework, doctoral study ordinarily has honours bachelor/master's entry and substantial original research independently examined by external experts; check the qualification against the applicable degree definition/AQF standard.

A medical MD or MBBS does not automatically establish doctoral-level education points. The stored comparison distinguishes an MD typically at AQF 9 and MBBS at AQF 7 from a doctorate at AQF 10; verify the actual course, not the initials. Further qualifying postgraduate research may establish the doctorate branch. An Australian fellowship program does not itself supply eligible AQF postgraduate study under the stored rule.

Suggested evidence: research component, thesis/examination and institutional level/entry information in addition to the award title. Do not conflate medical registration, professional fellowship, academic doctorate and specialist-education points.

## Specialist educational qualification

Baseline: **10 additional points**, subject to applicable legislation refresh, for a qualifying Australian master's **by research** or doctorate awarded by an Australian institution in a specified field. Stored operational guidance describes two academic years **in Australia**, while checked regulation 2.26AC(5A) says at least two academic years **at the institution**. Read verification-notes.md: do not silently combine these as one confirmed location test. Offshore/unusual-location claims remain Needs verification pending authoritative interpretation. Coursework master's study is not interchangeable with a research master's.

Check the ministerial field specification and actual course content/classification rather than assuming every course marketed “STEM” qualifies. Stored narrow fields include:
- Natural/physical sciences: biological, chemical, earth, mathematical, natural and physical sciences, other natural and physical sciences, physics and astronomy.
- Information technology: computer science, information systems, information technology, other information technology.
- Engineering/related technologies: aerospace, civil, electrical/electronic, engineering and related technologies, geomatic, manufacturing, maritime, mechanical/industrial, other engineering and related technologies, process/resources engineering and technology.

A narrow-field label need not be an exact textual match: microbiology can fall within biological sciences. If registration/classification information is unclear, use institutional documentation and other available evidence. Do not demand applicant access to departmental systems. Suggested support: research/course details, institution's field statement, duration and Australian study-location evidence. Ordinary education and this additional factor have distinct tests; refresh each rather than assuming one award proves both.

## Australian study assessment

Baseline: **5 points**, subject to applicable legislation refresh, for satisfying the Australian study requirement by invitation. The stored rule does not require study related to the nominated occupation or a recent completion date, provided the requirement was met by invitation.

**Required operational reference:** read [Australian study assessment](australian-study.md) for the full requirement matrix, independent 16-calendar-month and 92-week calculations, credits/overlaps, completion, registration, language, location, visa conditions and exception gates. Do not award the 5 points from this summary alone.

**Core baseline:** checked regulation 1.15F supplements the stored foundation: completed Australian degree(s), diploma(s) or trade qualification(s), registered courses, at least **16 calendar months** and **two academic years** of study, instruction in English, undertaken in Australia with study-authorising visa permission. Completion means academic requirements met, not necessarily formal conferral. Read verification-notes.md for sources and the uncovered later period. The academic-year instrument, credit/combined-course handling, online arrangements and concessions require separate checks before an edge-case conclusion. An Australian certificate alone does not establish compliance.

Study counts only where the applicant held the relevant substantive/applicable bridging visa permitting study throughout the period and complied with its conditions. Verify the bridging classes and applicable law rather than assuming any bridging visa suffices. Suggested preparation: award/completion evidence, transcript, course information, dates/location and study-permitting visa history. Preserve the potential 5-point claim as unresolved until the detailed gate is checked.

## Credentialled community language

Baseline: **5 points**, subject to applicable legislation refresh, for a qualifying NAATI credential held **at invitation**. The stored recognised routes are accreditation at paraprofessional level or above, certification at certified provisional level or above, or a community-language credential. A community-language credential need not qualify its holder as a professional interpreter/translator.

Preserve the historical transition from 19 March 2018: recognised older NAATI qualifications can remain eligible without automatically requiring reapplication under the new certification scheme. Check the applicable instrument, credential date/type, validity and transitional conditions; do not turn this historical protection into an unlimited present assurance for every expired credential. Suggested support: credential/outcome and dates. Language fluency without the specified credential does not establish these points.

## Regional study, residence and travel

Baseline: **5 additional points**, subject to applicable legislation refresh. By invitation, all four tests must be met:
1. Australian study requirement satisfied, including its external verification gate.
2. Campus/campuses where the relevant study occurred were at an Australian institution in the qualifying designated regional/low-population-growth area under the applicable dated rules.
3. The applicant lived in the qualifying regional area **throughout the course**, not merely the segment used to meet the Australian study requirement.
4. None of the relevant study constituted distance education.

The designated-area definition is determined at **points assessment** under the stored rule and may change over time. Check the instrument's postcode/campus coverage, commencement and savings. The historical regional instrument commencing 16 November 2019 applied to relevant applications without an assessed score before commencement; this is a dated transition, not an evergreen statement of postcode coverage. Historical coverage included all South Australia, Tasmania, Northern Territory and Australian Capital Territory; verify rather than assuming that list is still exhaustive or sufficient.

The study need not relate to the nominated occupation and its age is not itself disqualifying if completed before invitation. Lawful study permission/compliance throughout remains necessary; the stored regional branch specifies substantive, Bridging A or Bridging B visas permitting study. Check the applicable version. Regional institutional enrolment does not override the distance-education exclusion; flag online/blended arrangements for applicable-rule verification rather than quietly classifying them as on-campus.

Brief travel outside the regional area for business, holiday or work training can be consistent with continued regional enrolment and residence. Occasional stays with friends outside the area are not the same as establishing an outside residence. A pattern of living in a metropolitan area when classes do not require attendance may fail the residence intention. Do not invent a maximum number of nights or treat every weekend trip as automatically fatal; examine actual residence and duration/purpose of absences.

Suggested study evidence: transcript/course-completion letter identifying campus and dates. Suggested residence evidence: rental agreements, utility/telephone bills and other credible address records across the **whole course**. These examples are non-exhaustive suggestions, not a rule that every applicant must submit every bill type. Distinguish campus location from home address and preserve uncertain travel/residence branches for clarification.

## Partner branches and separate assessment dates

Baseline partner alternatives, subject to applicable legislation refresh and transitions. **Do not stack mutually alternative partner categories.** If none is established, do not invent partner points; mark missing facts separately from a verified zero.

| Established branch | Points |
|---|---:|
| No spouse/de facto partner, or partner is an Australian citizen/permanent resident | 10 |
| Qualifying skilled spouse/de facto partner meeting all skilled-partner tests | 10 |
| Same-subclass applicant partner, neither Australian citizen nor permanent resident, with competent English at invitation | 5 |

Critical distinction: relationship status and any partner's Australian citizenship/permanent-residence status are tested at the **date of the points assessment**, not automatically frozen at invitation or application. Partner English and skills-assessment requirements are tested at **invitation**. Changes after invitation/application can therefore change the applicable partner branch; report such changes accurately rather than retaining an obsolete EOI category.

The skilled-partner baseline requires a spouse/de facto partner who:
- is an applicant for the **same visa subclass**;
- is neither an Australian citizen nor permanent resident;
- was **under 45 at invitation**, unless an applicable dated transition applies;
- nominated an occupation specified at invitation and on the lists applicable to the primary applicant's points-tested visa;
- had a suitable assessment for that occupation by the relevant authority **at invitation**, not an assessment for a Subclass 485 visa; and
- had competent English **at invitation**.

**Older skills assessment branch:** the stored partner-points rule does not import the primary applicant's Schedule 2 assessment time limits. A suitable partner assessment obtained **more than three years before invitation** may be accepted for that criterion. Preserve it for verification instead of automatically discarding it as expired under a primary-applicant rule. Confirm applicable legislation and suitability/purpose; this does not guarantee acceptance of every old, revoked or unsuitable assessment.

**Historical age transition:** invitations **before 1 July 2018** may use an under-50 partner age criterion under the relevant savings provision. Require the exact invitation date and applicable Schedule 13 transition check. Do not apply under 50 automatically to present invitations or infer historical partner point values merely from the post-2019 table. Partner-point changes commencing 16 November 2019 also require dated assessment/transitional checks in historical cases.

For the no-partner branch, legal spouse/de facto definitions matter; “not living together”, “unmarried” or “partner not included” does not automatically mean single. For the citizen/permanent-resident branch, obtain reliable status evidence; a birth certificate alone may not resolve citizenship, and a grant notice requires status/date checks. Suggested preparation: relevant relationship history, explanation/evidence of changes, and appropriate status records. Compare prior declarations, disclose genuine inconsistencies and provide an opportunity for explanation; never manufacture a single status to secure points. Departmental status verification and procedural-fairness duties are not systems/actions this agent can perform.

## State nomination and regional nomination or sponsorship

Baseline, subject to applicable legislation refresh:

| Applicable branch | Points |
|---|---:|
| 190 state/territory nomination | 5 |
| Qualifying regional nomination or accepted family sponsorship for the applicable 491 / historical 489 branch | 15 |

For the 190 nomination branch, nomination must not have been withdrawn **before the assessed score is given**. For the regional branch, the stored rule requires state/territory nomination not withdrawn before assessment, or family sponsorship **accepted by the Minister**; merely naming a relative or proposing sponsorship is insufficient. Verify the applicable subclass, stream, sponsor/nomination requirements, dates and status. Historical 489 needs a dated legal/transitional check; do not infer its earlier point value or present availability from this baseline.

Withdrawal after a formal assessment does not simply reopen/revoke that assessed score under the stored decision framework. Nevertheless, it can mean the applicant no longer satisfies separate Schedule 2 nomination/sponsorship criteria. Preserve that distinction: retained points do not guarantee visa eligibility or approval. Suggested evidence: nomination/sponsorship acceptance and any withdrawal/changes. Do not award both nomination tables to one application just because both are listed.

## Integrity, uncertainty and output discipline

For every factor, retain a claim, timing rule, evidence status, applicable-rule verification status and provisional points treatment. Distinguish **requirement**, **policy/discretion branch** and **suggested supporting evidence**. Use “conditional/unresolved” where documents, applicable law or conflicting facts remain unverified; do not present an unverified optimistic total as confirmed.

Suspected bogus documents or false/misleading information can engage integrity criteria and procedural fairness. A preparation agent should identify discrepancies neutrally, request one clarifying fact at a time, preserve originals, avoid editing evidence to change its meaning and suggest qualified advice where consequences may be serious. Suspicion is not a finding of fraud. Do not accuse, conceal, fabricate or pronounce an automatic refusal; the decision maker must follow the applicable integrity and fairness procedures.

A concise applicant report should identify the provisional baseline total (only if enough facts exist), separate unresolved additions/deductions, flag invitation-score/pass-mark and other eligibility limits, and state the specific official-source checks still needed. It must not reproduce private source material, contain a purported official assessed score or promise an outcome. Start every standalone report/checklist/plan with the notice above. This Markdown reference is executable as preparation guidance without private files or special software; legal-source refresh remains necessary before an applicable-law conclusion.
